# "€1,500 in Compensation Is Adequate" – Decoder

> **In brief:** Insurers deliberately set a **low anchor** with blanket pain-and-suffering offers — typically €800–2,500 even for moderate injuries. BGH case law, however, requires **individual assessment** based on severity, duration, intensity and consequences. The [Hacks/Wellner pain-and-suffering table](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253) — the standard reference used by German courts — shows that comparable cases often yield **3 to 5 times** the insurer's initial offer.

## Also known as

Initial pain-and-suffering offer · Anchor offer from insurer · Blanket pain-and-suffering payment · Flat-rate compensation

---

## What you have just received — verbatim

Typical variants:

> *"Based on the severity of the injury, we offer pain-and-suffering compensation of €1,500 — adequate in our assessment."*

> *"Comparable cases are valued at approximately €1,200. We are guided by that benchmark."*

> *"A higher amount of Schmerzensgeld is not justified for a simple HWS-Distorsion (whiplash injury)."*

> *"Our offer of €2,000 is in line with market practice and corresponds to the courts' approach."*

Four characteristics:

- **Low flat-rate amounts** without individual calculation
- **Vague "comparable cases"** with no specific case references
- **Blanket reference** to "market practice" or "court practice"
- **No differentiation** by degree of injury, duration or consequences

---

## What is really behind it

### 1. Anchor-setting as a negotiation tactic
Psychological research (Tversky/Kahneman) shows: once a concrete figure is on the table, negotiations typically revolve around that figure. Starting at €1,500 lands you at €2,000 or €2,500 — not at €6,000, which may well be the appropriate amount.

### 2. Bypassing the Hacks/Wellner table
The [Hacks/Wellner pain-and-suffering table](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253) is the recognised standard reference of German courts for assessing Schmerzensgeld. Insurers' initial offers typically sit **at the lower end of — or below — the Hacks/Wellner ranges**. Anyone unfamiliar with the table will not notice the gap.

### 3. Exploiting the information asymmetry
Insurers know the relevant judgments; injured parties do not. Anyone who does not engage a lawyer negotiates blind against an experienced claims handler with database access.

### 4. Excluding late-onset consequences
Blanket offers frequently contain **no reservation for late-onset consequences**. Accepting means forfeiting future PTSD diagnoses, chronic pain or further sequelae — see the [Pauschal-Abgeltung Decoder](/decoder/pauschal-abgeltung).

## What the courts say

### § 253 BGB — legal basis for pain-and-suffering claims
Under the BGH's established line of authority, Schmerzensgeld serves two functions:
- **Compensatory function** — material compensation for pain and impairment
- **Satisfaction function** — symbolic acknowledgement of the wrong suffered

### Assessment criteria (BGH settled case law)
| Criterion | Significance |
|---|---|
| Severity of injury | ICD diagnosis, clinical findings |
| Duration and course | Acute phase + healing trajectory |
| Pain intensity | Pain scale, analgesic requirements |
| Consequences (loss of function, chronification) | Permanent impairments |
| Surgeries | Number and severity |
| Psychological consequences | PTSD, adjustment disorder, driving anxiety |
| Occupational impact | Sick leave, loss of ability to work |
| Degree of fault of the at-fault party | Gross negligence vs. ordinary negligence |

### Hacks/Wellner table as the courts' standard reference
The annually updated Hacks/Wellner pain-and-suffering table (C.H. Beck Verlag) collects German Schmerzensgeld judgments and organises them by injury type. It is an **orientation point**, not a ceiling — in cases of particular severity, awards can exceed the tabulated values.

### Typical ranges (for orientation)

| Injury | Range |
|---|---|
| HWS-Distorsion grade I (minor) | €250–1,500 |
| HWS-Distorsion grade II | €800–3,500 |
| HWS-Distorsion grade III | €3,000–10,000 |
| Bone fracture (healed) | €2,000–8,000 |
| Shoulder surgery | €5,000–15,000 |
| Intervertebral disc damage | €15,000–50,000 |
| Traumatic brain injury grade II | €20,000–80,000 |
| Treatable PTSD | €10,000–30,000 |
| Chronified PTSD | €20,000–60,000 |
| Adjustment disorder | €2,000–10,000 |

→ These ranges reflect judicial practice and serve as orientation — they are **not** a fixed rule. Individual cases may fall above or below these figures.

### The upward trend in awards
Since the 2010s, German courts have recognised a consistent upward trend in Schmerzensgeld ranges — particularly for psychological sequelae such as PTSD or chronic pain syndromes.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The single most important rule
Never accept an insurer's initial offer without legal review. Statistically, the appropriate Schmerzensgeld for moderate to severe injuries is often **3 to 5 times** the insurer's opening figure.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Complete documentation of your injuries**
ICD diagnosis, medical certificates, treatment history, surgeries, functional impairments, psychological consequences, occupational impact. The more complete the record, the stronger your position.

**2. Lawyer with Hacks/Wellner research**
A specialist road traffic lawyer reviews which comparable judgments apply to your case and quantifies the appropriate Schmerzensgeld.

**3. Late-onset reservation — non-negotiable**
No settlement without a reservation for unforeseeable late-onset consequences (see [Pauschal-Abgeltung Decoder](/decoder/pauschal-abgeltung)).

**4. Concrete counter-offer with case references**
A formal counter-offer from your lawyer, citing specific comparable judgments and referencing the Hacks/Wellner table, shifts the anchor and forces serious negotiation.

## What the insurer will likely do next

### Response A — Blanket increase
> *"We are increasing to €2,500 — final offer."*

**Your response:** If the increase is still too low, reject it. Present specific comparable judgments. Signal readiness to litigate.

### Response B — Litigation-risk threat
> *"In court proceedings, you could end up with less."*

**Your response:** With a clear factual basis and Hacks/Wellner ranges on your side, the litigation risk is manageable. Your lawyer will assess.

### Response C — Rejecting late-onset consequences
> *"We assume no late-onset consequences will arise — hence a blanket settlement."*

**Your response:** A reservation for late-onset consequences is non-negotiable. If refused: no settlement, proceed to litigation.

### Response D — Negotiation fatigue
> *"These negotiations have been going on for a long time — time for a decision."*

**Your response:** This is an insurer tactic. Take a break, speak to your lawyer, and do not accept out of exhaustion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is €1,500 Schmerzensgeld adequate for a HWS-Distorsion?**
Only for grade I (minor symptoms, short recovery). For grade II, the typical range is €800–3,500; for grade III, €3,000–10,000. The precise assessment depends on the medical diagnosis, treatment duration and consequences.

**How much Schmerzensgeld can a lawyer achieve?**
Statistically, 2 to 5 times the insurer's initial offer. Concretely: with documented Hacks/Wellner references and readiness to litigate, 60–80% of the upper end of the table range is realistic.

**What if the insurer says "final offer"?**
Standard pressure tactic. "Final offer" has no legal meaning. If the amount is still too low: file suit — the insurer pays your legal costs.

**Can I claim Schmerzensgeld for psychological consequences too?**
Yes. PTSD, adjustment disorders and driving anxiety are independently compensable under § 253 BGB. Ranges: €2,000–60,000 depending on severity and chronification.

**Do my financial circumstances play a role?**
Rarely. Only in exceptional cases — very low-income injured parties or very high-net-worth at-fault parties — are financial circumstances factored into the assessment.

**What does escalation cost me?**
In a no-fault accident: nothing. Legal costs are borne by the opposing insurance (BGH VI ZR 235/13) — see [Anwaltskosten-Erstattung](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung).
