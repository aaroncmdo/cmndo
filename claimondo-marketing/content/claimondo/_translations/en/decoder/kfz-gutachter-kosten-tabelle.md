# Vehicle Damage Expert Fee Table (BVSK 2025) – Decoder

> **In brief:** The fee of an independent vehicle damage expert (Sachverständiger) is based on the BVSK-Honorarbefragung (HB V 2025) and scales with the damage amount — the typical range runs from around €550 to €2,600. In a no-fault accident, the opposing party's liability insurer covers the expert report 100% (§ 249 BGB); billing is handled directly between the expert and the insurer via a Sicherungsabtretung (§ 398 BGB — assignment of the fee claim as security). A common insurer tactic is to retroactively reduce the fee citing "not locally customary" — the BGH (Germany's Federal Court of Justice) has repeatedly rejected this argument.

## Also known as

Expert fees after an accident · Sachverständigenhonorar · BVSK-Honorartabelle · What does a vehicle damage report cost

---

## What you have just received — verbatim

When an insurer reduces the fee, it typically reads like this:

> *"The fee charged by the expert is not locally customary. We are settling on the basis of the BVSK fee corridor HB V."*

> *"The ancillary costs claimed (photographs, written documentation, travel costs) are excessive and will be reduced proportionally."*

> *"A base fee of this amount is not appropriate given the scope of the damage."*

Two recurring lines:

- **Base fee reduction:** "not locally customary / above the corridor"
- **Ancillary cost reduction:** photo, documentation and travel costs summarily struck

Both are **not** blanket-permissible under BGH case law.

---

## What is actually behind it

### 1. The fee is not an hourly rate — it scales with the damage amount
Unlike workshop invoices, the expert's fee (Sachverständigenhonorar) is benchmarked against the **replacement or repair value**. This is BVSK standard and is recognised by the courts — a reduction "on a per-hour basis" therefore misses the point entirely.

### 2. "Locally customary" as a reduction lever
Insurers apply their own, lower fee corridor and call it "locally customary." The injured party (Geschädigter) is entitled to rely on the **actually agreed** fee — no price comparison is required (BGH).

### 3. Ancillary costs as easy targets
Photo, documentation and travel costs are independent, reimbursable line items. Blanket cuts are only permissible where the costs are **demonstrably** well outside the norm — not as a matter of course.

## The fee table — BVSK Honorar by damage amount

Reference values based on the BVSK-Honorarbefragung **HB V 2025** (base fee including typical ancillary costs, gross). The exact fee is shown in the expert report.

| Damage amount / repair costs (gross) | Expert fee (range) |
|---|---|
| up to €750 | Minor damage — generally no expert needed; a repair estimate suffices |
| €750 – €1,500 | approx. €380 – €550 |
| €1,500 – €3,000 | approx. €500 – €750 |
| €3,000 – €5,000 | approx. €600 – €850 |
| €5,000 – €10,000 | approx. €700 – €1,150 |
| €10,000 – €15,000 | approx. €1,100 – €1,500 |
| €15,000 – €25,000 | approx. €1,500 – €2,050 |
| €25,000 – €50,000 | approx. €2,050 – €2,600 |

→ These figures are **guidance**, not a fixed schedule. Rule of thumb from HB V: damage €5,000 → approx. €700; €15,000 → approx. €1,400; €30,000 → approx. €2,200 in expert fees.

**Who pays?** In a no-fault accident, the **opposing party's liability insurer** pays 100% (§ 249 BGB) — you pay €0. If you are at fault, your comprehensive insurance (Vollkasko) covers it (subject to excess); without comprehensive cover, you pay yourself.

## What the case law says

### BVSK table is a permissible estimation basis
The BGH has repeatedly held that the BVSK-Honorarbefragung is a suitable basis for estimating the required expert costs under § 287 ZPO (among others: **VI ZR 50/15**, **VI ZR 76/16**). A blanket reduction citing "not locally customary" is therefore not automatically permissible.

### The injured party bears no price-research risk
The injured party may freely choose their expert and is **not required to conduct market research** on the lowest available fee. Where the agreed fee does not **demonstrably** and significantly exceed the norm, it is fully reimbursable.

### Ancillary costs are reimbursable
Photo, documentation and travel costs are independent line items. Blanket cuts are the exception, not the rule.

### Sicherungsabtretung may not be refused
Via the Sicherungsabtretung (§ 398 BGB — assignment of the fee claim as security), the injured party assigns their fee entitlement to the expert. The insurer may not unilaterally reject the assignment — billing runs directly between expert and insurer.

## What to do now — step by step

### 💡 The key rule
In a no-fault accident above the minor-damage threshold, commissioning your **own** expert report is your legal right — and it costs you nothing. Without your own report, the insurer's figures set the baseline.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Commission your own BVSK expert**
Fee according to BVSK HB V — the report itemises base fee and ancillary costs transparently.

**2. Sign the Sicherungsabtretung**
This allows the expert to bill the insurer directly — no upfront payment required from you.

**3. Reject any fee reduction in writing**
For "not locally customary" reductions: cite the BGH line (VI ZR 50/15, VI ZR 76/16) and the absence of any price-research obligation on your part.

**4. Instruct a lawyer for any remaining shortfall**
If a difference remains, our partner law firm recovers it — in a no-fault accident, the insurer bears the legal costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does a vehicle damage expert actually cost?**
Under BVSK HB V, scaled to the damage amount: typically €550–€2,600. Examples: damage €5,000 → approx. €700; €15,000 → approx. €1,400; €30,000 → approx. €2,200. The exact fee is shown in the expert report.

**Who pays for the expert after an accident?**
In a no-fault accident (damage above approx. €750), the opposing party's liability insurer pays 100% under § 249 BGB. You pay €0. If you are at fault, comprehensive insurance (Vollkasko) covers it (subject to excess); without comprehensive cover, you pay yourself.

**What is the minor-damage threshold of €750?**
Below approx. €750, case law generally does not recognise a right to commission an expert — a repair estimate suffices. The exact threshold varies by appellate court (OLG), ranging from €700 to €1,000. For visually minor damage with potentially hidden structural issues, a closer look is worthwhile.

**What is a Sicherungsabtretung?**
Under § 398 BGB, the injured party assigns their fee entitlement — up to the amount of the expert costs — to the expert. You sign once; the expert bills the insurer directly. No upfront payment, no insolvency risk on your part.

**What if the insurer reduces the fee?**
A common tactic: "not locally customary." The BGH (VI ZR 50/15, VI ZR 76/16) accepts the BVSK table as the estimation basis; blanket "not locally customary" reductions are not permissible. Our partner law firm routinely recovers reduced fees — in a no-fault accident, the insurer bears the legal costs.
