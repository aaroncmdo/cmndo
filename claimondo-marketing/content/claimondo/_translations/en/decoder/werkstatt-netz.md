# "Workshop from Our Partner Network" — Decoder

> **In brief:** Insurer recommendations to use partner workshops ("premium partner network", "cooperation workshop") are one of the most common claim-reduction tactics: flat-rate pricing with lower hourly rates, replacement parts instead of original manufacturer parts, no right to an authorised dealership repair. Under established BGH case law, you have **free choice of workshop** — for vehicles under 3 years old or with an unbroken authorised-dealership service history, you even have a **right to authorised dealership repair, including original manufacturer parts and manufacturer hourly rates**.

## Also known as

Werkstattbindung (workshop tie-in) · workshop referral · partner workshop · premium workshop · cooperation workshop

---

## What you have just received — verbatim

Typical variants:

> *"To speed up the repair, we recommend workshop [X] from our premium partner network."*

> *"Our partner workshop network offers you the following benefits: fast repair, free collection and delivery service, mobility guarantee."*

> *"Repair at an authorised dealership is not necessary — a proper repair by our partner is sufficient."*

> *"If you choose to have the repair done at an authorised dealership, we will not reimburse the additional costs."*

Three characteristics that are almost always present:

- **Recommendation framed as a service** ("to speed things up", "with collection and delivery")
- **Implicit threat** of non-reimbursement if you choose your own workshop
- **No mention of your right** to free choice of workshop

---

## What is really going on

### 1. Flat-rate agreements with workshops
Insurers conclude framework agreements with selected workshops — lower hourly rates, fixed parts pricing, often replacement parts (equivalent aftermarket parts) instead of original manufacturer parts. Difference compared with an authorised dealership invoice: typically **15–30 %**.

### 2. Circumventing the authorised dealership right
The BGH line of case law protects your right to an authorised dealership repair for new or continuously dealership-serviced vehicles. If you accept the insurer's recommendation, you effectively waive this right — even if you were aware of it.

### 3. Restwert (residual value) and Wertminderung (diminished value)
A repair carried out at a non-authorised workshop affects the [merkantile Wertminderung (diminished market value)](/haftpflicht/wertminderung) — the resulting loss stays with you. For newer vehicles, the difference can amount to several thousand euros.

### 4. Control over the repair
At the partner workshop, the insurer has visibility into the repair process, can steer repair measures, and can resolve potential disputes about what work is necessary directly with the workshop — all to your disadvantage.

---

## What case law says

### The principle: free choice of workshop
The BGH makes clear in established case law: injured parties have **free choice of workshop**. The insurer's recommendation is **not a requirement** — you may decline it, and the insurer has no right to reduce reimbursement as a result.

### The authorised dealership right — the key line of case law
In two situations, there is a **right to repair at an authorised dealership** (BGH, judgment of 20.10.2009, VI ZR 53/09 — the so-called "Porsche ruling" and subsequent case law):

1. **Vehicle under 3 years old** — new-equivalent vehicle, authorised dealership standard
2. **Unbroken authorised dealership service history** — also for older vehicles, provided servicing is documented (service booklet, invoices)

In these situations the following apply:
- **Manufacturer hourly rates** as "customary" within the meaning of BGH case law
- **Original manufacturer parts** instead of aftermarket/remanufactured parts
- **Transfer costs and UPE surcharges** of the authorised dealership are reimbursable

### Scope of the right
Even if an authorised dealership repair is more expensive than the partner workshop option, the **full additional costs are reimbursable** — provided you have the authorised dealership right.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
You decide where your vehicle is repaired — not the insurer. For new or dealership-serviced vehicles: always use an authorised dealership.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Check your authorised dealership right**
Is the vehicle under 3 years old? Is there an unbroken authorised dealership service history? Find the service booklet and gather workshop invoices.

**2. Choose your own workshop**
Authorised dealership (if entitled) or a trusted independent workshop — **not** an insurer partner.

**3. Notify the insurer of your workshop choice in writing**
Letter to the insurer citing the BGH ruling and setting out your reasons (see template below).

**4. If additional costs are threatened, engage a lawyer**
A specialist road traffic lawyer reviews your claim and acts against the insurer — the insurer pays the legal fees.

## What the insurer will most likely do next

### Response A — threat of additional costs
> *"You will have to bear the additional costs compared with the authorised dealership yourself."*

**Your response:** Do not accept this. Where an authorised dealership right is established, all additional costs must be reimbursed in full. Cite the BGH line of case law in writing.

### Response B — "proper repair is sufficient"
> *"A proper repair at our partner workshop meets all requirements."*

**Your response:** The authorised dealership right applies regardless. "Proper" is not the same as "authorised dealership standard" — original manufacturer parts and manufacturer hourly rates are part of the entitlement.

### Response C — aftermarket parts argument
> *"We only reimburse aftermarket parts prices — original manufacturer parts are not required."*

**Your response:** Where an authorised dealership right exists, original manufacturer parts are the applicable standard. The BGH line of case law is unambiguous.

### Response D — hourly rate reduction to Schwacke regional average
> *"The authorised dealership hourly rate is excessive — we only reimburse the regional average under Schwacke."*

**Your response:** Where an authorised dealership right exists, the "customary" hourly rate is the authorised dealership rate, not the regionally averaged Schwacke figure. Reject the reduction in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the insurer tell me which workshop to use?**
No. Under established BGH case law you have free choice of workshop. The insurer's recommendation is a recommendation, not a requirement.

**When am I entitled to an authorised dealership repair?**
For vehicles under 3 years old or with an unbroken authorised dealership service history (BGH VI ZR 53/09). In these situations the insurer must reimburse the full authorised dealership costs.

**What are aftermarket parts vs. original manufacturer parts?**
Original manufacturer parts come directly from the manufacturer and are fitted at the authorised dealership. Aftermarket parts are equivalent replacement parts (often cheaper). Where an authorised dealership right exists, you are entitled to original manufacturer parts.

**What will the authorised dealership repair cost me?**
Where entitled: nothing — the full authorised dealership costs, including original manufacturer parts and manufacturer hourly rates, are borne by the insurer. Even the additional costs compared with partner workshops.

**What if my vehicle is more than 3 years old?**
With an unbroken authorised dealership service history, the right still applies. Without a service history: you still have free choice of workshop, but not the authorised dealership right. Even so, using an insurer partner workshop is not mandatory — you choose freely.

**How does the workshop choice affect the Wertminderung (diminished value)?**
A repair at a non-authorised workshop typically results in a higher merkantile [Wertminderung](/haftpflicht/wertminderung). For newer vehicles, the difference can amount to several thousand euros.
