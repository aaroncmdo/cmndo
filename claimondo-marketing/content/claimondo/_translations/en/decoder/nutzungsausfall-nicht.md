# "We Cannot Reimburse the Loss of Use" – Decoder

> **In brief:** Insurers refuse Nutzungsausfall (loss of use) with three standard arguments: "not sufficiently proven," "you had a second vehicle," "the Sanden-Danner table does not apply." Under established BGH case law, a presumption of personal use applies to private vehicles – you do **not** need to prove specific journeys. The Sanden-Danner table is court-tested and firmly established: typical daily rates of €27–175 depending on vehicle class, multiplied by the repair period.

## Also known as

Nutzungsausfall-Entschädigung · Nutzungsausfallentschädigung · NAE · Flat-rate mobility compensation

---

## What you have just received – verbatim

Typical variants:

> *"You have not sufficiently proven the loss of use – we cannot reimburse this item."*

> *"Since you own a second vehicle, no loss of use has occurred."*

> *"The Sanden-Danner flat-rate table does not apply here – you must provide concrete proof of usage costs."*

> *"You were on holiday / ill / abroad during the relevant period – no use was possible."*

Four recurring lines of argument:

- **"Not proven"** – as if you were required to produce evidence of use
- **"Second-vehicle argument"** – often constructed without examining the actual family circumstances
- **"Sanden-Danner not applicable"** – the recognised flat-rate table rejected outright
- **"No use possible"** – cited in cases of holiday, illness or time abroad

---

## What is really behind this

### 1. Eliminating the damage item
Nutzungsausfall typically amounts to €30–100 per day. Over 14 days of repairs that comes to **€420–1,400 per case** – a substantial sum the insurer saves entirely by refusing to pay.

### 2. Manufacturing a reversed burden of proof
Insurers phrase their refusals as if **you** must prove the use. In fact, the reverse is true: for private vehicles, use is **presumed** (BGH case law). The insurer would have to prove non-use – if it cannot, the claim stands.

### 3. Constructing the second-vehicle argument
When two vehicles are registered within a family, the insurer simply claims "you had another car." What actually matters is the **specific usage arrangement**: where two employed persons each have their own vehicle, each vehicle is used by one person – the loss remains.

### 4. Rejecting the Sanden-Danner table
The Sanden-Danner table significantly simplifies the assessment – flat daily rates by vehicle class rather than a complex case-by-case valuation. Insurers instead demand "concrete proof" that is practically impossible to provide (fuel card records, route plans, mileage logs).

---

## What the courts say

### Presumption of personal use
The BGH has established in consistent case law that for privately used vehicles, **the intention to use and actual use are presumed**. Injured parties do not need to prove specific journeys or routes – a **sworn declaration** (eidesstattliche Versicherung) regarding typical use is sufficient.

### Sanden-Danner table as a flat-rate basis
The Sanden-Danner table is a judicially recognised basis for estimation. It assigns flat daily rates to vehicle classes:

| Vehicle class | Daily rate (indicative) |
|---|---|
| Small car / Class A | €27–32/day |
| Compact / Class B | €35–45/day |
| Mid-range / Class C | €45–65/day |
| Upper mid-range / Class D | €65–95/day |
| Executive / Class E+ | €95–175/day |

→ Figures are indicative based on Sanden-Danner – exact rates follow the current edition of the table.

### Standard calculation

```
Nutzungsausfall = daily rate × repair period in days
```

Example: mid-range vehicle, 14 days of repairs → €55 × 14 = **€770 Nutzungsausfall**.

### The second-vehicle question – BGH case law
A second vehicle does **not** automatically exclude Nutzungsausfall. What matters is the specific family arrangement:

- **Two employed persons, one vehicle each** → loss of use remains, because the second vehicle is used by the other person
- **One person, two vehicles** → the second vehicle may reduce or exclude the claim
- **Family second vehicle for occasional use** → loss of use remains, because the primary vehicle is not replaced by it

### Repair period as the basis for calculation
The benchmark is the **actual** repair period (workshop order, waiting times for parts, workshop capacity) – not a theoretical ideal. In the event of a total loss: 14 days is the typical replacement procurement period.

## What to do now – concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
You prove nothing – you simply describe your usage arrangement. The presumption of personal use protects you.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Identify your vehicle class**
Determine the Sanden-Danner class for your vehicle. If uncertain: consult a lawyer or appraiser (Sachverständiger).

**2. Document the repair period**
Workshop order, date of return, any waiting times for parts.

**3. Set out your usage arrangement in writing**
Commute to work, family trips, errands. If a second vehicle is involved: explain the specific division of use.

**4. If refused: sworn declaration + lawyer**
A sworn declaration (eidesstattliche Versicherung) is sufficient as a level of proof. For disputes over €300, escalating to a lawyer is worthwhile.

```
Dear Sir or Madam,

I refer to your refusal to reimburse Nutzungsausfall (loss of use)
in claim [claim number].

I reject your blanket refusal on the grounds of "not provable."

Under the BGH's established case law on the presumption of personal
use, I as the injured party am NOT required to prove specific
journeys. For private vehicles, use is presumed.

I hereby declare under oath (eidesstattliche Versicherung):
- My vehicle would have been in actual use during the period of loss.
- Specific usage arrangement: daily commute of [X] km, family
  trips, errands.
- No other vehicle fully available to me as a substitute.

[If the second-vehicle argument is raised:]
My family's second vehicle [make, model] is used by [person]
for [purpose] and was not available to me during the period of loss.

Specific calculation:
- Vehicle class [X] under Sanden-Danner
- Daily rate: [Y] €/day
- Repair period: [Z] days (workshop order enclosed)
- Total Nutzungsausfall: [Y × Z] €

I request full reimbursement of [Y × Z] € within 4 weeks.

Yours faithfully,
[Signature]
```

## What the insurer will most likely do next

### Response A – Reduce the daily rate
> *"The daily rate is too high – we offer €30/day instead of €65."*

**Your response:** Refer to the Sanden-Danner table. Determine your vehicle class precisely. If disputed, instruct a lawyer.

### Response B – Shorten the repair period
> *"The repair could have been completed in 7 days – we will only reimburse that period."*

**Your response:** Enclose the workshop order and repair report. Document waiting times for parts. Cite BGH case law on the actual repair period.

### Response C – Reject the sworn declaration
> *"A sworn declaration is not sufficient – we require fuel card records and routes."*

**Your response:** Cite BGH case law on the presumption of personal use. A sworn declaration (eidesstattliche Versicherung) is a sufficient level of proof. Take the matter to court if necessary.

### Response D – Construct the second-vehicle case
> *"Your household has a second vehicle – no entitlement."*

**Your response:** Set out the specific family arrangement. Who uses which vehicle and for what purpose. Cite BGH case law on multi-vehicle families.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much Nutzungsausfall do I receive per day?**
Depending on vehicle class, €27–175 per day under the Sanden-Danner table. For a mid-range vehicle, typically €45–65/day.

**Do I have to prove I would have used the car?**
No. For private vehicles, the presumption of personal use applies (BGH consistent case law). A sworn declaration (eidesstattliche Versicherung) regarding typical use is sufficient.

**Can I claim Nutzungsausfall if I have a second vehicle?**
Possibly. What matters is the specific family arrangement. If the second vehicle is otherwise in use (e.g. by a partner), the claim remains valid.

**For how long is Nutzungsausfall paid?**
For the actual repair period (workshop order, waiting times for parts). In the event of a total loss, typically 14 days' replacement procurement period.

**What if I used a rental car (Mietwagen) at the same time?**
Then there is **no** Nutzungsausfall – rental car costs and Nutzungsausfall are mutually exclusive (duty to mitigate loss). You decide which item to claim.

**Is it worth going to court over €500 refused?**
Economically yes – the insurer pays your legal costs. BGH case law on the presumption of personal use is clearly established, giving good prospects for success.
