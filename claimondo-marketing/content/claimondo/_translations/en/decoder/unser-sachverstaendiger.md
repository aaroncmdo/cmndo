# "We'll Send You Our Trusted Expert" – Decoder

> **In brief:** Insurer-appointed experts (Sachverständige) are not neutral — their client is the insurer, and their assessments systematically tend toward **lower replacement values (Wiederbeschaffungswert), higher residual values (Restwert), and lower diminished value (Wertminderung)**. Under established BGH case law, you have **free choice of expert** and the right to an independent, BVSK-certified appraiser — the insurer pays all costs in full. The cost argument ("you'll save money") is factually wrong.

## Also known as

Vertrauens-Sachverständiger · insurer-appointed expert · SV referral · claims-service appraiser

---

## What you have just received — verbatim

Typical variants:

> *"We will send our trusted expert to assess your vehicle. Please arrange an appointment with Mr [X]."*

> *"To speed up settlement, we recommend engaging our experienced expert [X]."*

> *"You can save costs — our expert produces the report at no additional charge to you."*

> *"There is no need to appoint your own expert — we will handle this."*

Three recurring arguments:

- **"Trusted expert" or "our expert"** — implies neutrality that is structurally absent
- **Cost argument** — "you'll save money" (factually wrong)
- **Your own choice is presented as unnecessary**

---

## What is really going on

### 1. Structural assessment bias
Insurer-appointed experts are commissioned and paid by the insurer. Even without active manipulation, a systematic tendency emerges: lower replacement value, higher residual value, lower or zero diminished value (Wertminderung). The difference compared to an independent assessment is typically **15–40 %** of the total payout.

### 2. Minimising diminished value (Wertminderung)
Wertminderung (diminished value) is one of the largest disputed items ([Decoder "Wertminderung nicht angefallen"](/decoder/wertminderung-nicht)). Insurer-appointed experts frequently set it at €0 or a very low figure — independent assessments typically arrive at €500–3,000.

### 3. Maximising residual value (Restwert)
Higher Restwert → lower replacement-value payout. The difference per case is often €1,000–3,000.

### 4. Influencing the repair recommendation
Insurer-appointed experts tend to assess repair costs and replacement value in ways that apply the [130 % rule](/decoder/reparatur-unwirtschaftlich) in favour of replacement. This removes your repair option.

### 5. The cost argument as bait
"You'll save costs" is factually wrong — in a no-fault accident, expert costs are **fully reimbursable** (established BGH case law). You pay nothing, regardless of whether you use the insurer's expert or your own BVSK expert.

---

## What the courts say

### Free choice of expert
The BGH makes clear in established case law: injured parties have the right to **free choice of expert** (freie Sachverständigen-Wahl). The insurer's recommendation is a recommendation, not a requirement — you may decline it without giving reasons.

### Expert costs as a heads of loss
Expert costs are an independent head of loss under § 249 BGB (German Civil Code — compensation in kind). In a no-fault accident, the opposing liability insurer bears them **in full** — the amount is assessed against the BVSK-Honorartabelle (BVSK fee schedule) and is reimbursable under BGH case law even if the report does not ultimately lead to litigation.

### BVSK certification
The Bundesverband freiberuflicher und unabhängiger Sachverständiger (BVSK — Federal Association of Independent Automotive Experts) is Germany's principal expert association. BVSK members are bound by independence obligations — they may not maintain a permanent contractual relationship with any insurer. BVSK certification is therefore the strongest neutrality signal available.

### Evidential weight of the report
An independent expert report is **court-proof** (prozessfest) and accepted by courts as a basis for valuation. Where assessments are disputed, the outcome is not a "report versus report" deadlock — the court appoints a judicial expert whose task is to compare both reports. Insurer-appointed expert reports carry **lower weight** in that process due to their structural dependency.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The single most important rule
Always choose your own expert — never the insurer's. The insurer pays costs either way, but the valuation difference can amount to several thousand euros.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Find a BVSK-certified expert**
BVSK website (bvsk.de) → search by postcode region. Alternatively: recommendation via your workshop or lawyer.

**2. Written instruction within 7 days**
Before repairs begin. If repairs are already complete: a post-repair assessment is possible, but accuracy suffers.

**3. Specific brief to the expert**
Repair costs, replacement value (Wiederbeschaffungswert), residual value (Restwert — realistic for your local market), diminished value (Wertminderung) — name each explicitly.

**4. Decline the insurer's expert politely**
Letter citing the BGH (see template below).

## Letter template — declining the insurer's expert

```
Dear Sir or Madam,

I refer to your letter of [date] regarding claim [claim number].

Thank you for your offer to appoint an insurer-appointed expert
to assess the damage.

I will exercise my right of free choice of expert
(freie Sachverständigen-Wahl, established BGH case law)
and appoint an independent BVSK-certified expert.

Expert instructed:

- Name: [expert name]
- BVSK membership: yes
- Address: [address]
- Fee: in accordance with BVSK-Honorartabelle

The expert costs will be claimed against you as an independent
head of loss (§ 249 BGB, established BGH case law).

The report will be provided to you within [7–14 days].

Yours faithfully,
[Signature]
```

## What the insurer is likely to do next

### Response A — reducing expert costs
> *"The expert costs are excessive — we will only reimburse [lower amount]."*

**Your response:** Cite the BVSK-Honorartabelle as the reference. Flat-rate adjustments are typically permissible, but BGH case law governs reimbursability. Where there is a shortfall: instruct a lawyer.

### Response B — commissioning a second report
> *"Our expert reaches different conclusions — we proceed on that basis."*

**Your response:** A BVSK expert report is an equally valid valuation basis. Where disputed, a judicial expert decides — insurer reports carry lower weight in that process.

### Response C — delay argument
> *"We can only settle after our expert appointment."*

**Your response:** Your own report accelerates settlement — the insurer can settle on that basis immediately. No duplicate assessment is required.

### Response D — pseudo-helpfulness
> *"We want to help you — our expert makes everything simpler."*

**Your response:** Decline politely, communicate your own choice. The "helpfulness" is strategy, not service.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I choose my own expert?**
Yes. You have free choice of expert (freie Sachverständigen-Wahl) under established BGH case law. The insurer's recommendation is a recommendation, not a requirement.

**What does my own expert cost?**
In a no-fault accident: nothing. Expert costs are borne by the opposing liability insurer as an independent head of loss (§ 249 BGB). Fee according to the BVSK-Honorartabelle, typically €300–1,200 depending on the extent of damage.

**What is a BVSK expert?**
A member of the Bundesverband freiberuflicher und unabhängiger Kfz-Sachverständiger (BVSK). Members are bound by independence obligations — no permanent contractual relationship with any insurer. The strongest neutrality signal in Germany.

**What if the insurer does not accept my expert report?**
The report remains a valid valuation basis for litigation. Where assessments are disputed, the court appoints a judicial expert — that is the binding determination.

**How quickly must I instruct an expert?**
Within 7 days of the accident, and in any event before repairs begin. Later instruction is possible, but accuracy suffers.

**What if the insurer's expert has already inspected the vehicle?**
You can still appoint your own expert. Your report carries equal weight — it is not a mere confirmation of the insurer's report.
