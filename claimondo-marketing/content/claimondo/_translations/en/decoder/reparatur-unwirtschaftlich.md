# "Repair Is Not Economically Viable" – Decoder

> **In brief:** Insurers are quick to label damage "not economically repairable" the moment repair costs approach the vehicle's replacement value. German case law, however, recognises the **130% rule** (BGH VI ZR 132/00 et seq.): if repair costs exceed the replacement value by up to 30%, you are entitled to have your vehicle repaired at your own garage — provided you keep the vehicle and have it repaired properly and professionally.

---

## What You Have Just Received — Verbatim

Typical variations:

> *"Repair is not economically viable. We are offering you the replacement value of €15,000 less a salvage value of €6,500."*

> *"Since repair costs exceed the replacement value, this constitutes a total economic loss."*

> *"The 130% threshold does not apply here — we consider this an uneconomical repair case."*

Three mechanisms almost always present:

- **Blanket "total loss" assertion** without precise calculation
- **Salvage value set unrealistically high** (often sourced from insurer-owned online platforms)
- **130% rule** simply declared "not applicable"

---

## What Is Really Going On

### 1. Three Levers to Reduce Your Payout

When damage is borderline total loss, the insurer has three adjustment points — and typically uses all three:

1. **Set the replacement value low** (your equivalent vehicle would supposedly cost less).
2. **Set the salvage value high** (via insurer-affiliated disposal platforms).
3. **Define away the 130% rule.**

Each lever on its own can shift the outcome by several thousand euros. Together they determine whether you end up with a repair or a lump-sum payout.

### 2. The Salvage-Platform Trick

Insurers work with specialist online salvage exchanges that generate offers from **commercial buyers** for your damaged vehicle — offers you as a private individual **cannot actually realise** without considerable effort and risk. The BGH (Germany's Federal Court of Justice) has made clear: what counts is the **regionally realistic achievable** salvage value on the open market — not a theoretical platform figure.

### 3. Blocking Repair at Your Own Garage

If the insurer forces a replacement-value settlement, it avoids **all the friction** of authorised-dealer disputes, UPE mark-ups, and OEM parts. A lump-sum payout is the path of least resistance for the insurer.

---

## What Case Law Says

### The 130% Rule — BGH VI ZR 132/00 (Leading Judgment)

An injured party may claim reimbursement of repair costs **even where those costs exceed the replacement value by up to 30%** — subject to three conditions:

1. **Integrity interest** — you intend to keep the vehicle (no sale immediately after repair).
2. **Proper repair** — carried out professionally, in line with the expert's specification.
3. **6-month retention period** — you continue to use the vehicle for at least 6 months after repair (BGH line on continued use).

### Worked Example

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Replacement value (per expert) | €15,000 |
| 130% threshold (replacement value × 1.30) | **€19,500** |
| Repair costs per expert report | €18,000 |
| → **Repair costs within the 130% threshold** | ✓ |
| → Entitlement to full repair reimbursement | €18,000 |

Counter-example with repair costs of €20,000: 130% threshold exceeded → only replacement value minus salvage value is reimbursable.

### Salvage Value — BGH Line

What counts is the **regionally realistic achievable** salvage value on the general market — not special offers from specialist disposal buyers. The regional salvage value established by **your own expert** is the evidentiary basis. If the insurer demands that you sell the vehicle through its platform offers, you are in principle **under no obligation to do so** — you are free to sell it yourself or retain it.

### Right to an Authorised Dealership Repair

For vehicles less than 3 years old or with an unbroken authorised-dealership service history, BGH case law gives you the right to have repairs carried out at an **authorised dealership**. You are not required to accept a "recommended" partner garage nominated by the insurer (details: [Werkstattbindung-Decoder](/decoder/werkstatt-netz)).

## What to Do Now — Concretely

### 💡 The Single Most Important Step
Commission your own expert report — **before** you respond to the insurer's letter. The expert's costs are borne by the insurer (in a non-fault accident).

### 🛠 Four-Step Strategy

**1. Commission an expert report**
Choose a BVSK-member expert freely. Your expert establishes the replacement value, repair costs, salvage value (regional, realistic), and Wertminderung (diminished value). The report also includes the 130% assessment.

**2. Run the 130% calculation**
Replacement value × 1.30 = threshold. Are repair costs below that figure? → You are entitled to repair. Marginally above? → Detailed discussion with your expert.

**3. State your integrity interest in writing**
"I wish to keep my vehicle" — and give a commitment to retain it for 6 months. Both points must appear explicitly in your reply letter.

**4. Challenge the platform salvage value**
Confront the insurer's platform figure with your expert's value. Where the gap exceeds €500: cite the BGH line on regional achievability in writing.

## What the Insurer Is Likely to Do Next

### Response A — "130% Rule Not Applicable"
> *"The 130% rule applies only under certain conditions that are not met here."*

**Your reply:** Which condition is specifically said to be absent? Integrity interest: yes. 6-month retention: committed. Proper professional repair: yes. Three yesses: the rule applies.

### Response B — Salvage Value Dispute
> *"Our platform salvage value is €8,500 — higher than your expert's assessment."*

**Your reply:** BGH line on regional achievability. Platform offers are not a standard market valuation. Your own expert report is authoritative.

### Response C — Authorised Dealership Refused
> *"Repair at an authorised dealership is not required — a partner garage is sufficient."*

**Your reply:** For vehicles under 3 years old or with an unbroken authorised-dealership service history, the right to authorised-dealership repair applies (see [Werkstattbindung-Decoder](/decoder/werkstatt-netz)).

### Response D — Settlement Pressure
> *"Accept our replacement-value settlement — it means peace of mind and a quick payout."*

**Your reply:** Speed of payment is not an argument against legally substantiated claims. If you intend to have the vehicle repaired, do not accept this — keep your options open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if repair costs come to exactly 130.5%?**
Just over the threshold → in principle only replacement value applies. However, expert valuations have tolerances. Speak to your expert — positions can often be reorganised so the 130% limit is maintained without compromising the quality of repair.

**Do I really have to keep the vehicle for 6 months?**
That is the BGH line. Selling before the 6-month period expires can trigger an **obligation to repay** the amount received above the replacement value. Factor this in from the outset.

**What if I do not want to have the vehicle repaired?**
You are then entitled only to the **replacement value minus salvage value** — even where the 130% rule would in principle apply. The choice is yours.

**Salvage value — must I sell to the insurer's buyer?**
No. You are free to keep the vehicle, sell it privately, or scrap it. The salvage value relevant to the insurer's reimbursement is the regionally normal figure, not any specific offer.

**What if I do not commission my own expert?**
You take on a significant risk. Insurer-commissioned reports tend towards higher salvage values and lower replacement values — shifting the reimbursement by potentially several thousand euros to your disadvantage.
