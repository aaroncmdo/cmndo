# Audit Service Providers (controlExpert, carexpert & Co.) – How Insurers Cut Your Bills

> **Quick summary:** Audit service providers such as controlExpert or carexpert review expert reports and repair estimates on behalf of insurers — and frequently reduce them. What matters for you: you do not have to accept these reductions. The Bundesgerichtshof (Federal Court of Justice) has ruled clearly in favour of injured parties on the so-called Sachverständigenrisiko (expert cost risk) (BGH VI ZR 280/22).

---

## What This Is About

Before an insurer pays a damage invoice, many companies first route it through an **audit service provider**. The largest is **controlExpert** (based in Langenfeld), in which Allianz holds a stake; **carexpert** is a consortium of several motor insurers. These providers review expert fees and repair line items largely through automated processes and produce a **Kürzungsbericht** (reduction report) — listing positions deemed "non-reimbursable."

The underlying audit algorithms are not publicly available. That is precisely why blanket reductions without specific justification are legally contestable.

## Typical Reductions — and the Arguments Against Them

| Reduced Item | Why It Does Not Hold Up |
|---|---|
| Expert fee "excessive" | The BVSK-Honorartabelle is a BGH-recognised basis for estimation (**VI ZR 357/13**). |
| Franchised workshop hourly rate | Reimbursable where a franchised workshop claim exists (**VI ZR 91/09**). |
| Blending coat "not required" | Part of professionally sound repair with modern paints (**VI ZR 174/24**). |
| UPE surcharges / delivery costs | Reimbursable (**VI ZR 65/18**, **VI ZR 211/03**). |
| Wertminderung (diminished value) "wrong methodology" | Choice of method is unrestricted under § 287 ZPO. |

## The Decisive BGH Ruling

**BGH VI ZR 280/22 (12.03.2024)** settles the Sachverständigenrisiko: a party who was entitled to rely on a market-standard fee schedule does not bear the risk of allegedly excessive costs. An invoice that has already been settled additionally carries evidential weight (**VI ZR 225/13**). Blanket reductions without concrete grounds consistently fail to withstand this line of case law.

## What You Should Do If Your Claim Is Reduced

1. Request the **Kürzungsschreiben** (reduction letter) and the underlying **Prüfbericht** (audit report).
2. Have each reduction reviewed **line by line** — do not accept it as a whole.
3. Engage **legal support early**. If the accident was not your fault, the opposing insurer bears the legal costs too (§ 249 BGB).
