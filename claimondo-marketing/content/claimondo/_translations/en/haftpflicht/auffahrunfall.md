# Rear-End Collision – Who Is Liable and What Is the Fault Split?

> **In brief:** In a classic rear-end collision, the **prima facie evidence rule** (Anscheinsbeweis) establishes **100% liability on the part of the rear driver** – they failed to maintain the required following distance (§ 4 StVO) or were not paying attention. The front driver bears **0% contributory negligence** – even if they braked (except in the provable case of a sudden, unprovoked emergency stop designed purely to cause harm). Insurance attempts to push through a shared-fault split are unsustainable in 95% of cases.

## Also Known As

Rear-end collision · Rear impact · Tailgating collision · Following-too-closely scenario

---

## Explained in One Sentence

In a rear-end collision, the prima facie evidence rule (Anscheinsbeweis) operates against the rear driver – they maintained insufficient following distance or were inattentive.

## Explained in Three Sentences

A rear-end collision is the clearest prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) scenario in German traffic law – the driver who rear-ends another vehicle bears **full fault** in 95% of cases, because they failed to maintain the required following distance under § 4 StVO or lost attention. The front driver **does not need to prove anything** – the Anscheinsbeweis rules out their contributory negligence as a matter of principle, even if they braked (braking is permitted in road traffic and must be anticipated). The rear driver can rebut the Anscheinsbeweis only in rare exceptional cases: a **sudden and wholly unprovoked emergency stop** by the front driver designed purely to cause harm or without any comprehensible reason, technical defects caused by a third party, or other atypical constellations.

## The Five Key Figures

- **100 : 0** — standard fault split against the rear driver
- **§ 4 StVO** — statutory duty to maintain a safe following distance
- **Anscheinsbeweis** — prima facie evidence rule operating in favour of the front driver
- **95%** — typical success rate for the front driver
- **0% rebuttal rate** — without specific atypical facts

---

## What Happens Legally in a Rear-End Collision

A rear-end collision is the classic case for the **Anscheinsbeweis** – the court draws an **inference based on established experience** that the rear driver was at fault, without the front driver having to prove fault. The rationale: a driver who maintains the required following distance (rule of thumb: **half the speedometer reading in metres** for passenger cars outside built-up areas) and remains attentive can react to any braking action by the vehicle ahead.

### Three Legal Anchors
1. **§ 4 StVO** – duty to maintain adequate following distance
2. **§ 1 StVO** – general duty of care
3. **Anscheinsbeweis** – prima facie evidence rule in favour of the front driver

→ What matters is not that the front driver braked, but whether the **rear driver was able to react**.

## Fault Split by Typical Scenario

| Scenario | Rear Driver : Front Driver |
|---|---|
| Standard rear-end collision | **100 : 0** |
| Front driver brakes due to an obstacle (pedestrian, start of traffic jam) | 100 : 0 |
| Front driver brakes due to a traffic rule (red light, road sign) | 100 : 0 |
| Front driver brakes for "no reason / pure provocation" (hard to prove) | 80 : 20 or 70 : 30 |
| Front driver changes lane and brakes immediately | 70 : 30 or 50 : 50 |
| Rear driver demonstrably distracted (phone, sat-nav) | 100 : 0 |
| Both vehicles travelling well in excess of the speed limit | 80 : 20 |

→ As a rule: **100 : 0**. Rebuttal only in exceptional scenarios with concrete evidence.

## Insurer Tactics in Rear-End Collision Cases

> *"We consider the front driver to bear 25% contributory negligence because they braked suddenly."*

Standard argument. Do not accept it: braking in road traffic is **always permissible** and foreseeable. The Anscheinsbeweis must be rebutted by concrete evidence.

> *"Our policyholder reports that you braked without reason."*

The rear driver's perception is not a rebuttal fact. Objective evidence is required.

> *"There may have been a technical defect – and therefore an unavoidable event."*

Technical defects are in principle the vehicle owner's risk under § 7 StVG. Force majeure applies only where the defect was caused by an external third party (e.g. "storm" → tree falling on the vehicle).

## What to Do Now – Concretely

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Use the Anscheinsbeweis to your advantage. You need to prove **nothing** regarding fault – it is the insurer who must rebut it.

### 🛠 Three Steps

**1. Document the facts clearly**
Police report, witness contact details, photos of the final vehicle positions, skid marks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is at fault in a rear-end collision?**
As a rule, the rear driver – the Anscheinsbeweis applies because they failed to maintain following distance or adequate attention. Standard fault split: 100 : 0.

**What if the front driver braked?**
Braking is permitted and foreseeable in road traffic. It does not relieve the rear driver of liability – they must be able to react.

**What if the front driver braked without reason?**
Only where a deliberate provocation stop is proven. The evidential threshold is high – rarely successful in practice.

**Can I claim Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering compensation) for a whiplash injury (HWS-Distorsion)?**
Yes. Ranges depending on severity: €800–€3,500 (Grade I/II), up to €25,000 (Grade III, chronic). See [Schmerzensgeld § 253 BGB](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253).

**What if I braked suddenly?**
Where braking was objectively justified (obstacle, traffic jam, red light): you are fully protected. Where it was a deliberate provocation stop: a contributory negligence split is possible.
