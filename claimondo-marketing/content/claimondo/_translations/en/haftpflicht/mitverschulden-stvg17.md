# § 17 StVG – Shared Liability Between Vehicles and Apportionment

> **In brief:** § 17 StVG governs **shared liability between multiple vehicle owners** when damage arises from the operation of several vehicles. Liability is apportioned according to each party's contribution to the accident — both **fault** on the part of each driver **and** the **operational risk (Betriebsgefahr)** of each vehicle are decisive. Even an innocent driver may be assigned a **Betriebsgefahr share** (typically 20–30%) — unless proof of an **unavoidable event** succeeds. Classic applications: rear-end collisions, intersection crashes, disputed lane changes.

## Also known as

§ 17 StVG · shared causation · liability apportionment · apportionment norm · Betriebsgefahr share

---

## Explained in one sentence

§ 17 StVG distributes damage between multiple vehicle owners according to their respective contributions — the fault and Betriebsgefahr (operational risk) of both vehicles feed into the apportionment.

## Explained in three sentences

Where **multiple motor vehicles** are involved in a road accident, liability does **not** automatically fall 100% on the at-fault party — § 17 StVG governs **apportionment** according to each party's contribution to the accident. Two factors are weighed: the **fault** of the driver (e.g., failure to yield, running a red light, tailgating) **and** the **Betriebsgefahr** of each vehicle (the inherent motorised risk that every car carries). The practical consequence: even where the other party bears clear primary fault, a **Betriebsgefahr share of 20–30%** frequently remains with the injured party's vehicle — unless the injured party establishes the **unavoidable-event defence** under § 17 Abs. 3 StVG.

## The five key figures

- **§ 17 StVG** — apportionment norm between vehicles
- **§ 17 Abs. 3 StVG** — unavoidable event as grounds for full exemption
- **20–30 %** — typical Betriebsgefahr share for mere involvement
- **0 %** — in prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) scenarios or where the unavoidable-event defence succeeds
- **§ 17 Abs. 1 StVG** — fault-based distribution

---

## When § 17 StVG applies

§ 17 StVG is the **specific provision** for accidents involving multiple vehicles. It applies where:

1. **Multiple motor vehicles** are involved in the accident
2. Both vehicles were **"in operation"** (within the meaning of § 7 StVG)
3. Damage occurred to one or both vehicles or to the persons involved

Where only **one** vehicle is involved alongside a non-vehicle party (pedestrian, cyclist), § 17 StVG does **not** apply — § 7 StVG and [§ 254 BGB (contributory negligence)](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-bgb254) govern instead.

## Apportionment factors

### 1. Driver fault
Did the driver breach a traffic rule? How serious was the breach?

- Failure to yield → typically 80–100%
- Running a red light → 100%
- Tailgating / rear-end collision → 100%
- Lane change without due care → 70–100%
- Opening a car door into traffic → 100%
- Excessive speed → 20–50% depending on the circumstances

### 2. Betriebsgefahr of the vehicle
Even absent driver fault, every motor vehicle carries a **Betriebsgefahr** — the general risk inherent in operating a motorised vehicle.

- **Standard passenger car:** 20–25%
- **Heavy goods vehicle (LKW):** 30–40% (higher Betriebsgefahr due to size/mass)
- **Motorcycle:** 25–35%
- **Special vehicle (articulated lorry, heavy transport):** higher still

→ This share persists **even without fault** on the part of the driver, unless the unavoidable-event defence under § 17 Abs. 3 StVG succeeds.

---

## Unavoidable event — § 17 Abs. 3 StVG

The owner of a vehicle involved in the accident can escape the Betriebsgefahr share only by proving that the accident

> *"could not have been avoided even taking all circumstances into account and was due to an unavoidable event."*

### Requirements
- The **"ideal driver" standard** — the accident would have occurred even with the utmost care
- Examples: sudden, entirely unexpected conduct by a third party; a technical defect with no detectable maintenance shortcoming
- In practice, this defence very rarely succeeds

### Examples from case law
- ✅ **Accepted:** A child jumps unexpectedly from between parked cars into the road — the driver had no opportunity to react
- ❌ **Not accepted:** Black ice (a standard road risk); the other party's failure to yield (speed could have been adjusted)

## Typical apportionment examples

| Scenario | Split (primary at-fault party : injured party) |
|---|---|
| Rear-end collision (classic) | 100 : 0 (Anscheinsbeweis) |
| Failure to yield with impact | 80 : 20 (Betriebsgefahr) |
| Running a red light | 100 : 0 or 80 : 20 |
| Lane-change collision (clear fault) | 80 : 20 |
| Left-turning driver vs. oncoming traffic | 70 : 30 or 80 : 20 |
| Intersection with equal right of way (right-before-left) | 0 : 100 or 50 : 50 |
| Neither party clearly at fault | 50 : 50 |
| Black-ice accident (no fault) | 50 : 50 |

→ In [Anscheinsbeweis](/haftpflicht/anscheinsbeweis) scenarios, the Betriebsgefahr is typically **displaced** in favour of full liability on the at-fault party.

## Apportionment — how it affects your reimbursement

With an 80 : 20 split against the other party:

| Head of claim | Full amount | At 80% reimbursement |
|---|---|---|
| Repair costs | €8,000 | €6,400 |
| Wertminderung (diminished value) | €1,500 | €1,200 |
| Nutzungsausfall (loss of use) | €700 | €560 |
| Pain and suffering | €3,000 | €2,400 |
| Expert report costs | €600 | €480 |
| **Total** | **€13,800** | **€11,040** |

→ €2,760 difference due to the 20% share.

## What to do now — concrete steps

### 💡 The most important rule
Do not silently accept blanket contributory-negligence or apportionment claims made by the insurer. § 17 StVG requires **concrete facts** establishing the distribution of causation — not generalisations.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Check whether Anscheinsbeweis applies**
In classic scenarios (rear-end collision, failure to yield), the Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence rule) operates in the injured party's favour — the insurer must rebut it.

**2. Assess your own Betriebsgefahr share**
Where the other party bears clear primary fault, a residual Betriebsgefahr share of 20–30% is realistic. However, in Anscheinsbeweis scenarios it is often 0%.

**3. Dispute over apportionment — instruct a lawyer**
The [Decoder "Mitverschulden 30 %"](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent) explains the insurer's typical blanket strategy and the appropriate counter-response.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is § 17 StVG?**
The apportionment norm between multiple vehicle owners. It distributes liability according to each party's contribution — fault and Betriebsgefahr of both vehicles.

**What is a Betriebsgefahr share?**
The portion of liability arising solely from the inherent motorised risk of a vehicle — even without driver fault. Typically 20–30% for a passenger car, higher for lorries or motorcycles.

**Can I bear partial liability even though I was not at fault?**
Yes, in the form of Betriebsgefahr. Exemption is available only by proving an unavoidable event (§ 17 Abs. 3 StVG) or in Anscheinsbeweis scenarios.

**Who decides on the apportionment?**
In out-of-court proceedings, the insurer (subject to negotiation). In litigation, the court — typically on the basis of ADAC apportionment catalogues and established judicial practice.

**When is the split 100 : 0?**
In clear Anscheinsbeweis scenarios (rear-end collision, running a red light, failure to yield). Also where the injured party successfully proves an unavoidable event.
