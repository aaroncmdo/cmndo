# Default (Verzug) under § 286 BGB in Claim Settlement

> **In brief:** Default under § 286 BGB (German Civil Code on debtor's default) occurs when the liability insurer fails to provide a payment that is due, despite a formal demand or after the expiry of a reasonable review period. Consequences: default interest at 5 percentage points above the base rate, full reimbursement of legal fees as default damages, and compensation for losses caused by the delay. Default is your most powerful tool against stalling tactics — and in many cases it occurs even **without a formal demand**.

## Also Known As

Schuldnerverzug · Leistungsverzug · Zahlungsverzug · Verzugseintritt · default by the insurer

---

## Explained in One Sentence

Default under § 286 BGB means that the insurer has not fulfilled a payment obligation on time and, as a result, bears interest, legal fees, and all further consequential costs as an independent claim item.

## Explained in Three Sentences

In motor liability claims, default (Verzug) classically arises through a formal demand with deadline (§ 286 para. 1 BGB), but it can also occur **automatically without a demand** — for instance, when a calendar-based deadline expires, when the insurer issues a serious and final refusal to pay, or when the reasonable review period established by BGH case law has elapsed. Once default exists, the insurer owes **default interest at 5 percentage points above the base rate** (§ 288 BGB), bears **legal fees in full** as default damages (BGH VI ZR 235/13), and is additionally liable for **all further losses caused by the delay** (§ 280 BGB). Default is therefore the sharpest escalation tool you have against delay tactics.

## The Five Key Figures

- **5 percentage points** above the base rate in default interest (§ 288 BGB)
- **14 days** — standard grace period stated in a formal demand
- **4 weeks** — reasonable review period where liability is clear (BGH case law)
- **0** additional costs for you in a no-fault accident (the insurer bears default damages)
- **§ 286 para. 1–4 BGB** — legal basis

---

## What Default Means in Legal Terms

Default (Verzug) is governed by the German Civil Code as a civil-law failure of performance by the **debtor** — in our context, the debtor is the opposing liability insurer who must compensate the road accident loss. § 286 BGB distinguishes two routes by which default arises: with a formal demand (para. 1) and without one (para. 2). Both are practically relevant in claim settlement.

### Default by Formal Demand (§ 286 para. 1 BGB)
The injured party sends a written formal demand with a specific deadline. If the deadline passes without result, the insurer is in default. Day one of default is the first day after the deadline expires.

### Default Without Formal Demand (§ 286 para. 2 BGB)
Four constellations trigger default automatically:
- **No. 1 — Calendar-based deadline:** A previously set deadline expires.
- **No. 2 — Time-contingent:** A deadline such as "within X days of Y" expires.
- **No. 3 — Serious and final refusal:** The insurer clearly rejects payment — no further demand required.
- **No. 4 — Special circumstances:** The surrounding circumstances indicate that a demand is unnecessary. BGH case law places the expiry of the **reasonable review period** in road accident claims under this heading.

### Requirements at a Glance

| Requirement | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Due date | The claim has arisen and is enforceable (§ 271 BGB in conjunction with § 14 VVG). |
| Demand or exception | Written demand with deadline — or one of the four statutory exceptions. |
| Fault | The insurer must be responsible for the delay — this is presumed by law. |

## Legal Consequences of Default

### 1. Default Interest
From day one of default, the insurer owes **5 percentage points above the base rate** on the entire justified claim — accruing continuously until payment. Details: [Default interest under § 288 BGB](/haftpflicht/verzugszinsen-bgb288).

### 2. Legal Fees as Default Damages
Once default exists, legal fees become an **independent claim item** and are fully reimbursable — even if you instruct a lawyer **after default has occurred**. This BGH line (VI ZR 235/13) removes a common counterargument from the insurer. Details: [Legal fee reimbursement](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung).

### 3. Compensation for Delay-Caused Loss (§ 280 BGB)
All further losses arising from the delay are additionally recoverable. Typical examples in road accident claims:
- Extended rental car periods because repair authorisation was withheld
- Delayed medical treatment with resulting deterioration
- Diminished value of a vehicle ready for sale due to prolonged standstill
- Pre-financing costs

### 4. Negotiating Leverage
Default effectively compels the insurer to act, because every further delay **costs money**. Statistically observable: settlement offers made after default occurs average **20–40% higher** than those made before default.

## What You Should Do Now — Concretely

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Default does not arise automatically. It must be **created** — through a clear deadline and, where necessary, a formal demand. Anyone who simply waits will eventually have default after the reasonable review period — but will find it **difficult to prove**.

### 🛠 Three Steps to Robust Default

**1. Claim letter with deadline**
Set a 4-week deadline in accordance with [BGH case law](/haftpflicht/4-wochen-frist), sent by registered post with return receipt. Archive the receipt.

**2. Formal demand with 14-day deadline**
Once the claim deadline has passed, send a formal demand — in writing, with an explicit reference to default. Also by registered post.

**3. Initiate court order or legal action**
If the insurer remains passive, a court payment order (Mahnbescheid) or a lawsuit is the next step. The insurer pays the legal fees.

### 📝 Demand Letter Template

```
Formal Demand — Claim Reference [Number]

Dear Sir or Madam,

With reference to the accident of [date] and my notice of claim
of [date], I hereby formally demand payment of the outstanding
amount of [amount] €.

The 4-week deadline set in my letter of [date] expired without
result on [date]. If payment is not made within 14 days of
receipt of this demand, the following default consequences
will arise:

- Default interest at 5 percentage points above the base rate
  (§ 288 BGB)
- Full reimbursement of legal fees (BGH VI ZR 235/13)
- Compensation for losses caused by delay (§ 280 BGB), e.g. for
  extended rental car costs or delayed medical treatment

In the event of non-payment I will initiate court proceedings
(payment order or lawsuit). All associated costs are borne by
the insurer.

Yours sincerely,
[Signature]
```

## What the Insurer Typically Claims

> *"We did not receive the demand — therefore no default."*

Prove receipt via the registered-post return receipt. Email demands are permitted under BGH case law but carry greater evidential risk. **Registered post with return receipt is the standard.**

> *"We are still reviewing — no default where legitimate clarification is needed."*

What specific clarification is needed? Where liability is clear, a blanket appeal to "ongoing review" is insufficient under BGH case law. Ask concretely what is being reviewed.

> *"The delay was beyond our control — we are not responsible for it."*

Responsibility for the delay is **presumed by law** (§ 286 para. 4 BGB). The burden of proof lies with the insurer, not with you. Standard delays fall within the insurer's risk sphere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I really have to send a formal demand?**
Not always. Where liability is clear, default arises after the reasonable review period even **without** a demand (§ 286 para. 2 No. 4 BGB by analogy). In practice, a formal demand is still advisable — it makes the precise moment of default **provable** and forecloses legal argument.

**When does the 14-day period start running?**
From receipt of the demand by the insurer — not from the date of sending. Hence: registered post with return receipt.

**What if the insurer responds to the demand with a small partial acknowledgement?**
A partial acknowledgement restarts the limitation period (§ 212 BGB). The [limitation period](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195) starts afresh for the acknowledged items. For still-outstanding items the formal demand continues to run — default exists for the remaining amount.

**What if the insurer rejects part of the claim?**
A partial rejection may be treated as a **serious and final refusal** within the meaning of § 286 para. 2 No. 3 BGB — default for the rejected portion from the date of rejection, **without** any further demand being required.

**Can an email count as a formal demand?**
Yes, formally. But: registered post with return receipt is the gold standard for proving receipt. If you send a demand by email, at a minimum request a read receipt and archive the send log.

**Can default interest actually be claimed against the insurer in court?**
Yes. It is an independent claim alongside the main claim. It is included as an ancillary claim in every court payment order and every lawsuit — without additional court fees.
