# Unauthorized Vehicle Use & Car Theft – Liability for Joyriding

> **In brief:** "Schwarzfahrt" (joyriding / unauthorized vehicle use) = using a vehicle without the registered keeper's permission. If an accident is caused by an unauthorized driver, the keeper's liability under § 7 Abs. 3 StVG is extinguished — **provided** the keeper neither enabled nor facilitated the unauthorized use. The unauthorized driver is personally liable under § 18 StVG / § 823 BGB. Injured parties who cannot recover from an insolvent or unidentified unauthorized driver may turn to the **Verkehrsopferhilfe** (German Motor Insurers' Bureau).

## Also Known As

§ 7 Abs. 3 StVG · Schwarzfahrt · Kfz-Diebstahl-Konstellation · unauthorized vehicle use

## Three Typical Scenarios

### 1. Stolen Vehicle
- Keeper bears no fault → § 7 Abs. 3 StVG, **no keeper liability**
- The injured party's recourse: the unauthorized driver personally (often insolvent) or the **Verkehrsopferhilfe** ([VOH](/haftpflicht/unversicherte-voh))

### 2. Joyriding by a Family Member or Acquaintance
- Keeper **enabled** the unauthorized use (keys left accessible, prior tolerance, inadequate care)
- **Keeper liability remains** under § 7 Abs. 1 StVG — the keeper's insurer pays
- Insurer recovers against the unauthorized driver via subrogation (Regress)

### 3. Joyriding by an Employee
- Company vehicle: joyriding typically falls outside the scope of employment
- Keeper liability applies where authorized key storage was in place
- Employer's right of recourse against the employee

## Liability Diagram

```
Joyriding + keeper without fault  → no keeper liability
                                  → unauthorized driver liable under § 18 StVG / § 823 BGB
                                  → VOH protection if driver is insolvent or unknown

Joyriding + keeper at fault       → keeper liable under § 7 Abs. 1 StVG
                                  → keeper's insurer pays
                                  → recourse (Regress) against unauthorized driver
```

## What the Keeper Must Prove

To qualify for the exemption under § 7 Abs. 3 StVG, the keeper must demonstrate:
- Keys were stored with due care
- Vehicle was properly parked and locked
- No indication that unauthorized use was imminent
- Immediate police report upon discovery

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who pays if a stolen vehicle causes an accident?**
The unauthorized driver personally under § 18 StVG / § 823 BGB — if the driver is insolvent or unidentified: the Verkehrsopferhilfe steps in.

**What if a family member takes the vehicle without permission?**
Where the keeper is at fault for how keys were stored, liability remains under § 7 Abs. 1 StVG — the insurer pays but exercises Regress against the unauthorized driver.

**What must I do as keeper if my vehicle is stolen?**
File a police report immediately. Where theft is clearly established, an exemption under § 7 Abs. 3 StVG is available.

**Can I claim compensation from the VOH in a joyriding case?**
Yes — where the unauthorized driver is insolvent or cannot be identified, the Verkehrsopferhilfe steps in.
