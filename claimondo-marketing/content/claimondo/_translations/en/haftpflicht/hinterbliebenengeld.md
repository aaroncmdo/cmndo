# Bereavement Compensation under § 844 Abs. 3 BGB

> **In brief:** Bereavement compensation (*Hinterbliebenengeld*) is a **lump-sum payment** for the emotional loss suffered by close relatives following a fatal road accident — introduced in 2017 under § 844 Abs. 3 BGB. It is awarded **independently** of any medically diagnosed condition and is granted in addition to [shock damage claims](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich). Typical court-awarded ranges: **€5,000–15,000 per close relative**, and higher where the relationship was particularly close or the circumstances particularly severe.

## Also known as

§ 844 Abs. 3 BGB · Lump-sum compensation for bereaved relatives · Grief compensation · Bereavement pain and suffering

---

## In one sentence

Bereavement compensation (*Hinterbliebenengeld*) is a lump-sum payment to close relatives for the emotional loss of a family member in a fatal accident — regardless of whether the claimant has suffered a medically recognised condition of their own.

## In three sentences

The Act introducing bereavement compensation (2017) created § 844 Abs. 3 BGB, entitling close relatives to a lump-sum payment for their emotional suffering following a fatal road accident, **without requiring proof of a medically recognised condition of their own**. This fills the legislative gap between ordinary grief (no legal claim) and shock damage (requiring a diagnosable psychiatric condition) — the loss is acknowledged without a medical diagnosis. Court awards have settled in the range of **€5,000–15,000 per close relative**, and go higher where the relationship was particularly close (e.g. young parents of a killed child).

## The five key figures

- **§ 844 Abs. 3 BGB** — the legal basis for the claim
- **2017** — year the bereavement compensation right was introduced
- **€5,000–15,000** — typical court-awarded range per relative
- **Lump-sum** — no medical diagnosis required
- **Claimable in parallel with [shock damage](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich)**

---

## Who is entitled to bereavement compensation

§ 844 Abs. 3 BGB names:
- **Spouses / registered civil partners**
- **Parents and children**
- **Extended in individual cases** to other close relatives at judicial discretion (siblings, fiancés, long-term partners)

→ Not every relative qualifies automatically — what counts is the **close personal relationship**.

---

## Amount — court sentencing practice

| Scenario | Range |
|---|---|
| Loss of an adult family member (standard) | €5,000 – 10,000 |
| Loss of young parents / young spouses | €10,000 – 15,000 |
| Loss of a minor child | €12,000 – 20,000 |
| Particularly severe circumstances (e.g. witnessing the accident) | up to €25,000 |

→ Figures are oriented to Hacks/Wellner and established court practice. In litigation, the court decides on a case-by-case basis.

---

## Bereavement compensation vs. shock damage — differences and overlap

| Aspect | Bereavement compensation | Shock damage |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | § 844 Abs. 3 BGB | § 823 BGB |
| **Requirements** | Close relative + fatal accident | Close relative + own medically recognised condition |
| **Diagnosis required** | No | Yes (clinical threshold) |
| **Amount** | Lump sum €5,000–15,000 | Individual assessment €3,000–50,000+ |
| **Proof required** | Family relationship + death | + Diagnosis + causation |

→ Both claims are **cumulative** — a bereaved relative who has also developed PTSD can claim both bereavement compensation (~€10,000) and shock damage (~€15,000–25,000).

## What to do now — step by step

### 💡 The key rule
Bereavement compensation is a lump-sum entitlement — the claim exists **automatically** upon proof of a close family relationship and a fatal road accident. No diagnosis required.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Document the family relationship**
Civil registry certificates, family record book.

**2. Assert the claim**
In writing to the insurer — even if other claims are running in parallel.

**3. Check shock damage in parallel**
If you have suffered a psychiatric condition of your own, a shock damage claim may apply in addition — both claims are cumulative.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much is bereavement compensation?**
The standard court range is €5,000–15,000 per close relative. In exceptional circumstances (death of a child, young family) the amount can be higher.

**What is the difference from shock damage?**
Bereavement compensation is a lump sum that requires no medical diagnosis. Shock damage requires a medically recognised psychiatric condition of the claimant's own — and is assessed individually, typically at higher amounts.

**Can I claim both?**
Yes, the two claims are cumulative — bereavement compensation as a lump sum alongside an individual shock damage award.

**Who is entitled?**
Spouses, registered civil partners, parents, children. Extended in individual cases to siblings, fiancés, and long-term partners.

**What counts as a "close relative"?**
Both the legal relationship and the actual closeness of the bond matter. Spouses and parent-child relationships are the standard; other constellations are assessed case by case.

**What if death occurs later?**
Bereavement compensation only applies where the accident was fatal. Where a relative suffers severe permanent injury but survives, this claim does not apply — but a shock damage claim may.
