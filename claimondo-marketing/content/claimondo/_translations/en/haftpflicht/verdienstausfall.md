# Loss of Earnings After a Road Accident – Calculation, Evidence, Special Cases

> **In brief:** Loss of earnings (Verdienstausfall) is the reimbursement of income lost due to accident-related incapacity to work – for employees typically **net income minus sick pay**, for the self-employed **lost profit**. Legal basis: § 252 BGB in conjunction with § 249 BGB. Fully reimbursable by the opposing liability insurer; for longer-term earning capacity reduction, an additional **reduced earning capacity pension** under § 843 BGB may apply. Where statutory sick pay (Lohnfortzahlung) is received, claims transfer under § 6 EFZG / § 116 SGB X.

## Also Known As

Wage loss · Income loss · Earning capacity damage · Lost profit

---

## Explained in One Sentence

Loss of earnings (Verdienstausfall) is reimbursement of the net income you were unable to earn due to accident-related incapacity to work – minus any income-replacement benefits received.

## Explained in Three Sentences

Where an accident results in a sick note or incapacity to work, you are entitled to the **difference** between what you would have earned without the accident and what you actually received (statutory sick pay, health insurance sick pay). For employees, **net income** is the reference figure – the gross amount is only relevant for the internal calculation; tax and social security advantages are offset. For the self-employed, **lost profit** under § 252 BGB is the reference figure – calculated using prior-year profits, industry benchmarks, and expert estimates; special assessment rules apply for marginally employed workers and homemakers.

## The Five Key Figures

- **§ 252 BGB** – Lost profit as the legal basis
- **§ 6 EFZG** – Claims transfer upon statutory sick pay
- **§ 116 SGB X** – Claims transfer to the health insurer
- **§ 843 BGB** – Reduced earning capacity pension for permanent damage
- **Net income** as the standard reimbursement benchmark

---

## Who Is Entitled – and What Is Reimbursed

### Employees

**Phase 1 – the first 6 weeks:**
- **Statutory sick pay (Lohnfortzahlung)** by the employer (§ 3 EFZG) – you receive your full net pay
- Your direct claim against the insurer transfers in this phase to the employer under **§ 6 EFZG**
- You have no personal reimbursement claim for this period

**Phase 2 – from week 7 onwards (health insurance sick pay):**
- **Health insurance sick pay (Krankengeld)** from the health insurer (~70 % of gross pay, max. 90 % of net pay)
- The **difference** between health insurance sick pay and your actual net income is **your own claim** against the insurer
- The health insurer's portion transfers to the health insurer under **§ 116 SGB X** (subrogation)

### Self-Employed

- **Lost profit** under § 252 BGB
- Calculated using the average of the last 3 years plus industry analysis and seasonal adjustment
- Expert report (Sachverständigen-Gutachten) often required
- BGH position: reduced evidential standard under § 287 ZPO

### Special Cases

- **Marginally employed workers:** pro-rata entitlement
- **Homemakers:** no Verdienstausfall, but [household management loss (Haushaltsführungsschaden)](/haftpflicht/haushaltsfuehrungsschaden)
- **Apprentices:** training allowance
- **Civil servants:** difference from official salary
- **Retirees:** no classic Verdienstausfall, but potentially additional costs / supplementary needs

## How Calculation Works – Practical Scheme

### Employee Difference Method

```
Loss of earnings = (net income before accident × days on sick note)
                 − health insurance sick pay
                 − other benefits received
```

### Self-Employed Estimation Method

```
Lost profit = average prior-year profits / days
            × days of incapacity
            × industry / seasonal factor
```

### Employee Example

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net income before accident | €3,000/month |
| Period on sick note | 12 weeks |
| Statutory sick pay weeks 1–6 | full (no loss for you) |
| Health insurance sick pay weeks 7–12 (6 weeks) | approx. €2,250/month |
| Difference from net income | €750/month |
| **Loss of earnings for 1.5 months** | **approx. €1,125** |

## Evidence Requirements

| Group | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Employees | Employer's earnings certificate, health insurer's sick pay notice, sick notes (AU-Bescheinigungen) |
| Self-employed | Tax assessments for the last 3 years, management accounts (BWA), contracts/invoices, expert report if required |
| Homemakers | [Haushaltsführungsschaden](/haftpflicht/haushaltsfuehrungsschaden) framework |
| Civil servants | Salary statements, medical records |

§ 287 ZPO permits a **reduced evidential standard** – for the self-employed, precise accounting detail down to the last figure is not required.

---

## Reduced Earning Capacity Pension for Permanent Damage – § 843 BGB

Where incapacity to work is permanent (e.g. following polytrauma, chronic PTSD, or severe traumatic brain injury), reimbursement is no longer calculated as a lump-sum Verdienstausfall but as a **ongoing monetary pension** under § 843 BGB:

- Reference figure: difference between projected lifetime income and actual income
- Payable for life or until retirement age
- Capitalisation possible (lump-sum payment instead of pension)
- Complex calculation involving actuarial methods → specialist lawyer required

→ With permanent earning capacity reduction, total claims can amount to **several hundred thousand euros**.

## What to Do Now – Concretely

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Collect all income records from day one. Even while you are still incapacitated – the documentation obligation begins immediately.

### 🛠 Four Steps

**1. Archive all sick notes without gaps**
Original sick notes (AU-Bescheinigungen) from your GP and any specialist.

**2. Request an earnings certificate**
From your employer for the 12 months prior to the accident (gross, net, period of statutory sick pay).

**3. Secure the health insurer's notice**
Obtain written confirmation of sick pay (Krankengeld) receipt. The health insurer will later pursue its own subrogation claim – you can only claim the difference.

**4. For the self-employed: expert estimation**
Tax adviser or expert report on lost profit. Coordination by a lawyer is advisable.

## What the Insurer Typically Claims

> *"The statutory sick pay covers that – no claim exists."*

This is only true for the first 6 weeks. From week 7 onwards (health insurance sick pay), the difference claim arises for you.

> *"Loss of earnings not sufficiently evidenced – you could have continued working."*

The medical sick note (AU-Bescheinigung) is the basis. The insurer's assertions about working capacity must be substantiated.

> *"For the self-employed, tax assessments are not sufficient evidence."*

Incorrect. § 287 ZPO permits a reduced evidential standard. Tax assessments, management accounts (BWA), and the order situation are an established basis for estimation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who pays for wage loss after an accident?**
For the first 6 weeks, the employer pays through statutory sick pay (Lohnfortzahlung) – the claim transfers to the employer under § 6 EFZG. From week 7 onwards, you claim the difference between health insurance sick pay and net income directly from the insurer.

**How is loss of earnings calculated?**
Net income before the accident minus income-replacement benefits received (statutory sick pay, health insurance sick pay) – multiplied by the duration of incapacity. For the self-employed, lost profit under § 252 BGB.

**Am I entitled to loss of earnings as a self-employed person?**
Yes. The reference figure is lost profit – calculated from prior-year data, industry comparisons, and where necessary expert estimation. § 287 ZPO reduces the evidential burden.

**How much is health insurance sick pay?**
Approximately 70 % of gross pay, maximum 90 % of net pay. The difference from your net income is your claim against the insurer.

**What if I have a permanent reduction in earning capacity?**
§ 843 BGB applies – a reduced earning capacity pension (Erwerbsminderungs-Rente) payable until retirement or for life. Highly complex calculation; engage a specialist lawyer.

**Am I also entitled to loss of earnings for overtime and bonuses?**
Yes, if provable (pay slips from prior years). Special payments and 13th-month salary are also claimable.
