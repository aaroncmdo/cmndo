# Pain and Suffering Compensation under § 253 BGB in Traffic Accidents – Claims, Amounts, Practice

> **In brief:** Pain and suffering compensation (Schmerzensgeld) under § 253 BGB (German Civil Code) is the monetary award for **non-material damages** (pain, suffering, psychological consequences, impairment of quality of life) following a traffic accident. Assessment is individualised based on severity, duration, intensity, and consequences – guided by the **Hacks/Wellner-Schmerzensgeldtabelle** (German pain and suffering reference table). Ranges: €250 (minor whiplash) to €100,000+ (polytrauma, chronic PTSD). Insurers' initial offers typically fall at **20–40%** of what is legally warranted.

## Also Known As

§ 253 BGB claim · Non-material damages · Genugtuung (satisfaction) · Pain and suffering assessment

---

## Explained in One Sentence

Pain and suffering compensation (Schmerzensgeld) is the monetary award for pain and impairment of quality of life following a traffic accident – assessed according to severity, duration, and consequences in line with the Hacks/Wellner-Schmerzensgeldtabelle.

## Explained in Three Sentences

§ 253 Abs. 2 BGB grants a claim to fair monetary compensation for injury to the body, health, freedom, or sexual self-determination – in traffic accidents this covers physical and psychological injuries. Under established BGH case law, the assessment serves two functions: the **compensation function** (material compensation for pain, suffering, and impairment of daily life) and the **Genugtuungs-Funktion** (symbolic acknowledgement of the wrong suffered). The reference standard is the **Hacks/Wellner-Schmerzensgeldtabelle** (published annually by C.H. Beck Verlag) – it compiles German pain and suffering judgments, categorised by injury type, with ranges from €250 to €100,000+ depending on severity.

## The Five Key Figures

- **§ 253 Abs. 2 BGB** as the legal basis for the claim
- **§ 11 StVG** – pain and suffering compensation under strict liability
- **Hacks/Wellner-Tabelle** as the standard judicial reference
- **2 to 5 times** – typical gap between insurer's initial offer and what is legally warranted
- **3-year limitation period** from knowledge of the claim (§ 195 BGB) – up to 30 years for late-onset injuries

---

## Conditions for a Claim

§ 253 Abs. 2 BGB identifies four protected legal interests, the violation of which triggers a Schmerzensgeld claim:

1. **Body** – injuries to physical integrity (whiplash/HWS-Distorsion, fractures, haematomas, surgery)
2. **Health** – somatic and psychological impairments (PTSD, adjustment disorder, chronic pain, tinnitus)
3. **Freedom** – restrictions on freedom of movement (confinement to bed, limited mobility)
4. **Sexual self-determination** – rarely relevant in traffic accident cases

In addition: § 11 StVG extends the Schmerzensgeld claim to strict liability (i.e., even where the vehicle keeper is not at fault).

---

## The Two Functions – BGH Doctrine

### Compensation Function
Material compensation for pain, suffering, impairment of daily life, and permanent consequences. What cannot be replaced with money – what can money make more bearable?

### Genugtuungs-Funktion (Satisfaction Function)
Symbolic acknowledgement of the wrong suffered by the at-fault party. Where conduct is grossly negligent or intentional, awards tend to be higher.

---

## Assessment Criteria (BGH Established Case Law)

Assessment is carried out **individually** based on all circumstances of the specific case:

| Criterion | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Severity of injury** | ICD diagnosis, clinical findings, degree of severity |
| **Duration of treatment** | Acute phase, recovery, outpatient or inpatient |
| **Intensity of pain** | Pain scale, need for pain medication |
| **Permanent consequences** | Loss of function, chronification, scarring |
| **Surgical procedures** | Number, severity, complications |
| **Psychological consequences** | PTSD, adjustment disorder, driving anxiety, depression |
| **Occupational impact** | Sick leave, inability to work, career change |
| **Social consequences** | Social isolation, loss of social activities |
| **Degree of fault** | Gross negligence vs. ordinary negligence (increases award) |
| **Financial circumstances** | Very rarely relevant – only in exceptional cases |

## Hacks/Wellner-Schmerzensgeldtabelle – the Practical Benchmark

The annually updated Hacks/Wellner-Tabelle (C.H. Beck Verlag) compiles German pain and suffering judgments and organises them by injury type. It is the **reference source used by German courts** – insurers know it too and negotiate against it **when the injured party cites it**.

### Typical Ranges (for guidance)

| Injury | Range |
|---|---|
| HWS-Distorsion Grade I (minor whiplash) | €250 – €1,500 |
| HWS-Distorsion Grade II | €800 – €3,500 |
| HWS-Distorsion Grade III | €3,000 – €10,000 |
| Bone fracture (healed) | €2,000 – €8,000 |
| Shoulder surgery (rotator cuff) | €5,000 – €15,000 |
| Traumatic disc herniation | €15,000 – €50,000 |
| Commotio cerebri (mild traumatic brain injury) | €500 – €5,000 |
| Severe traumatic brain injury with late sequelae | €25,000 – €100,000+ |
| Polytrauma (ISS > 16) | €30,000 – €250,000+ |
| PTSD (treatable) | €10,000 – €30,000 |
| Chronic PTSD | €25,000 – €80,000 |
| Adjustment disorder | €2,000 – €10,000 |
| Driving anxiety (clinically diagnosed) | €2,000 – €8,000 |
| Tinnitus (airbag noise, chronic) | €5,000 – €25,000 |
| Facial scarring (cosmetic) | €3,000 – €15,000 |

→ These ranges reflect judicial practice and are for guidance only – **not** absolute values. In cases of exceptional severity or multiple combined injuries, awards may be significantly higher.

## Trend Towards Higher Awards

Since the 2010s, German case law has recognised **higher Schmerzensgeld ranges**, in particular for:

- Psychological sequelae (PTSD, chronic depression)
- Chronic pain syndromes (ICD-11 MG30, since 2022)
- Polytrauma with late-onset consequences
- Complex trauma (complex PTSD / kPTBS, ICD-11 6B41)

This trend matters: earlier judgments from the 2000s no longer reflect current assessment practice.

---

## Evidence Requirements

For Schmerzensgeld assessment, you will need:

1. **ICD diagnosis** from your GP or specialist
2. **Unbroken chain of treatment** (emergency department → GP → specialist → rehabilitation → therapy)
3. **Ongoing documentation** (symptom diary, therapy reports, rehabilitation discharge letters)
4. **Evidence of functional impairment** (restricted mobility, rehabilitation assessments)
5. **For psychological consequences:** diagnosis from a licensed psychotherapist or psychiatrist
6. **Occupational impact** (sick leave certificates, employer declarations)

§ 287 ZPO (German Code of Civil Procedure) permits **a lower evidential threshold** for establishing the amount of loss – the judge assesses based on free conviction; perfect documentation is not strictly required but substantially strengthens the case.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much Schmerzensgeld will I receive after a traffic accident?**
Assessment is individual – guided by the Hacks/Wellner-Tabelle. Ranges: €250 (minor whiplash) to €100,000+ (polytrauma, chronic PTSD). For mid-severity injuries, typically €2,000–€10,000.

**How is the exact amount calculated?**
Based on severity, duration, intensity, consequences, surgical procedures, psychological impact, occupational consequences, and the degree of fault of the at-fault party. Comparable judgments from the Hacks/Wellner-Tabelle serve as the reference point.

**Can I claim Schmerzensgeld for psychological consequences?**
Yes. PTSD, adjustment disorders, driving anxiety, and depression are independently compensable. Ranges: €2,000–€80,000 depending on severity and chronification.

**How long do I have to bring a Schmerzensgeld claim?**
3 years from the end of the year in which you became aware of the damage and the identity of the at-fault party (§ 195 BGB). For late-onset injuries, the absolute maximum is 30 years (§ 199 Abs. 2 BGB).

**What if my injury is not listed in the Hacks/Wellner-Tabelle?**
A lawyer will identify analogous judgments – the table is a guide, not an exhaustive list.

**Can I receive Schmerzensgeld if I was partly at fault?**
Contributory negligence results in a proportional reduction – 30% contributory negligence means 70% Schmerzensgeld. Blanket percentage reductions without supporting evidence must be challenged.
