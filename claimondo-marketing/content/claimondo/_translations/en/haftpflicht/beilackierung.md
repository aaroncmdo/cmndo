# Adjacent Panel Repainting After an Accident – When the Insurance Company Must Cover It

> **Quick summary:** Adjacent panel repainting (Beilackierung) means the body shop repaints neighbouring, **undamaged** panels so the repaired section doesn't stand out in colour. With modern metallic and effect paints, this is technically unavoidable in many cases. The Bundesgerichtshof (Federal Court of Justice) confirmed it as reimbursable in **VI ZR 174/24**. Despite this, it is one of the most frequently cut line items – usually struck out wholesale in an audit report, without anyone having seen your vehicle.

## Also Known As

Colour-match blending · blend-in repainting · Beilack · repainting of adjacent panels

## Explained in One Sentence

Beilackierung is the repainting of undamaged neighbouring panels to ensure a seamless colour match after the repair – and under § 249 BGB (German Civil Code, principle of full restitution) it forms part of a professionally executed restoration whenever it is technically required.

## Explained in Three Sentences

Modern paints – particularly metallic, pearl-effect and multi-layer systems – can almost never be mixed to match so precisely that a single repainted panel remains invisible in natural light. To prevent the repair from ending at a visible colour boundary, the paint gradient is "blended out" into the adjacent panels; that is exactly what Beilackierung means. With **VI ZR 174/24** the Bundesgerichtshof has clarified that this line item is reimbursable for modern paints – so the question is no longer *whether* it qualifies, but only whether it was technically required in the specific case.

## The Five Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | § 249 Abs. 2 BGB – restitution in kind |
| **Leading decision** | BGH VI ZR 174/24 – reimbursable for modern paints |
| **Who assesses the necessity** | the expert appraiser at the vehicle |
| **Who strikes the line item** | the audit service on the basis of the file alone |
| **Typically affected** | metallic, pearl-effect and multi-layer paints |

## Why Adjacent Panel Repainting Is Technically Necessary

A paint finish is not simply a colour code. Metallic and effect paints contain particles that align during spraying – depending on nozzle, distance, humidity, coat thickness and the age of the existing paint. Two panels with an **identical colour code** can therefore look noticeably different as soon as light strikes them at an angle.

Ageing compounds the problem: a six-year-old factory finish has shifted in tone through UV exposure relative to freshly mixed material carrying the same code. If only the damaged panel is repainted, a visible step appears at the panel boundary.

Beilackierung resolves this by blending the new paint out into the adjacent panel – the transition then runs across a surface area rather than sitting at a hard edge.

### When It Is Routinely Required

- **Metallic and pearl-effect paints** – the finer the effect, the more visible any deviation
- **Large, flat surfaces** side by side (door next to sill panel, wing next to bonnet)
- **Older vehicles** with an aged factory finish
- **Multi-layer systems** where coverage depends on coat thickness

### When It Does Not Apply

- **Solid (uni) paints** in simple colours can often be matched exactly
- **Small panels with a pressed seam or hard edge** that forms a natural break – the colour gradient ends there anyway
- Where the expert appraiser has **not** included it in the estimate because it is not required in the specific case

## What the BGH Decided

With **VI ZR 174/24** the Bundesgerichtshof confirmed that Beilackierung is a reimbursable line item for modern paint finishes. This settles the long-running fundamental dispute over whether it constitutes "anyway costs" or unnecessary expenditure.

Industry bodies such as the **ZKF** (Zentralverband Karosserie- und Fahrzeugtechnik) and the **IFL** have held this position for some time; the BGH ruling supports it. This matters in practice: if your expert appraiser has included Beilackierung in the estimate and the insurance company strikes it, your side is not standing alone with a minority view.

> **Important:** The BGH does not say that Beilackierung is *always* payable. It says it is reimbursable **when technically required**. That assessment belongs to the expert appraiser – not to an audit report compiled on the basis of the file alone.

## The Typical Reduction – and How to Recognise It

The cut rarely comes with a technical justification. Typical formulations in the audit report:

| What the letter says | What it means |
|---|---|
| "Adjacent panel repainting not required" | Blanket reduction without vehicle inspection |
| "Only applicable upon actual repair" | Attempt to strike the item for notional (fiktiv) settlements |
| "Line item does not correspond to the damage pattern" | Contradiction of the expert report – without a counter-report |
| "Colour-match blending is an anyway cost" | A claim the BGH does not support in this form |

The pattern is always the same: an audit service assesses your expert appraiser's estimate **at a desk**, without having seen the vehicle. How these audit reports are produced and what weight they carry is explained in detail under [Expert Costs](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten).

## Beilackierung in Notional (Fiktiv) Settlements

If you settle **on a notional basis** – that is, on the basis of the expert report without actually having the repair carried out – insurers attempt to strike this line item particularly often: no painting is taking place, they argue, so no cost is incurred.

This argument misunderstands the logic of notional settlement. What is reimbursed there is the **objectively required repair expenditure**, not the cost actually incurred. That is precisely why you do not receive VAT under a notional settlement, but do receive the calculated labour values. What belongs to the required expenditure is set out in the expert report – and if Beilackierung is included there with a reasoned calculation, it forms part of that expenditure.

The fundamentals of both settlement approaches are set out under [Repair Costs](/haftpflicht/reparaturkosten).

## What to Do Now – Concretely

1. **Place the audit report next to the expert report.** Compare line by line. A struck Beilackierung entry almost always lacks any technical justification.
2. **Involve your expert appraiser.** They have seen the vehicle and can substantiate the necessity – a written statement on the reduced line item is the most effective step.
3. **Do not negotiate yourself.** Accepting the reduction "as a goodwill gesture" forfeits the amount permanently.
4. **Set a deadline.** If the insurance company does not respond, the standard settlement periods apply – see [4-Week Deadline](/haftpflicht/4-wochen-frist).

### 💡 The Most Important Rule

A struck Beilackierung entry is **not a technical verdict** – it is, initially, an assertion. Only someone who has examined the paint finish on the vehicle can assess it technically. That is precisely the point at which your expert report carries more weight than an audit report compiled from the file alone.
