# Original, Identical (Ident) or Used – Which Replacement Parts You Are Entitled To

> **Quick overview:** Not every part that fits is equivalent. **Original replacement parts** come with manufacturer approval and branding; **ident parts** often come from the same supplier but without manufacturer approval; **used parts** have an unknown history. The Bundesgerichtshof (Federal Court of Justice) addressed the entitlement to original parts in **VI ZR 302/08** and the authorised-repairer entitlement in **VI ZR 53/09**. The insurer may refer you to cheaper parts – but cannot unilaterally decide the matter.

## Also known as

OEM parts · original parts · ident parts · remanufactured parts · aftermarket parts · used parts · accessory parts

## Explained in one sentence

Which part quality belongs in your vehicle is determined by the repair method set out in the expert report (Gutachten) – not by the opposing insurer's proposed deductions.

## Explained in three sentences

Original parts, ident parts and used parts differ in terms of manufacturer approval, warranty and documentation – differences that affect resale value and future claims. The expert (Sachverständiger) calculates the part quality required for the proper restoration of your specific vehicle: for a young, full-service-history car this means something different than for a twelve-year-old used vehicle. The insurer may **propose** a cheaper alternative, but must justify it; it cannot impose one unilaterally.

## The five key figures

| | |
|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | § 249 Abs. 2 BGB (German Civil Code) – restoration of the prior condition |
| **Leading ruling on original parts** | BGH VI ZR 302/08 (22.06.2010) |
| **Leading ruling on authorised repairers** | BGH VI ZR 53/09 |
| **Who determines part quality** | the expert (Sachverständiger) in the repair specification |
| **What the insurer may do** | refer with justification – not impose unilaterally |

## The three quality tiers compared

| | Original replacement part | Ident part / remanufactured part | Used part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | vehicle manufacturer | often the same supplier | donor or accident vehicle |
| **Manufacturer approval** | yes | no | no |
| **Logo / part number** | yes | no manufacturer logo | original part with history |
| **Warranty** | new-part warranty | varies by supplier | limited or none |
| **Prior history** | none | none | typically **unknown** |
| **Documentable** | fully | limited | difficult |

**Ident parts** are the most common point of dispute, because the insurer's argument seems plausible at first: if the same factory produces the part, surely it is the same part. The difference lies in **approval** – the manufacturer tests and documents the part for your exact model. That verification chain is absent with an ident part, and it is precisely why manufacturer warranties and certain service entitlements may depend on it.

**Used parts** are particularly problematic for bodywork and safety-critical components: a part from a donor vehicle may carry its own prior damage that is not visible. For load-bearing structural components or sensor-equipped parts, this is a risk in its own right – see [ADAS calibration](/haftpflicht/adas-kalibrierung).

## When you are entitled to original parts

The entitlement follows the logic of § 249 BGB: the condition to be restored is the condition **before** the accident. If a vehicle was maintained exclusively at an authorised repairer, that history is part of its prior condition – and cannot be seamlessly continued with parts that lack manufacturer approval.

The BGH addressed the authorised-repairer question in **VI ZR 53/09** (entitlement to an authorised repairer for young vehicles with a full service history) and the original replacement part question in **VI ZR 302/08**. In practice, three factors interact:

- **Vehicle age** – the younger the vehicle, the stronger the case for original parts
- **Service and workshop history** – unbroken authorised-repairer maintenance carries significant weight
- **Type of component** – the standard is stricter for safety and structural parts than for a trim strip

> **Important:** There is no fixed age threshold. Anyone telling you that after five years you are automatically entitled only to ident parts is reducing the legal position to a rule of thumb – the same oversimplification that applies to [Wertminderung (diminished value)](/haftpflicht/wertminderung).

## What the insurer may do – and what it may not

It may **refer** you to a cheaper repair option. However, that referral is subject to conditions: it must be specific, reasonable and equivalent – and it must be **justified**, not merely asserted.

| What the assessment letter states | What needs to be examined |
|---|---|
| "equivalent replacement parts sufficient" | Equivalence must be demonstrated, not asserted |
| "ident parts qualitatively comparable" | Comparable in what respect? Approval and warranty are absent |
| "used part available on the market" | Availability does not substitute a reasonableness assessment |
| "original parts not required" | Contradicts the expert's repair specification without a counter-report |

What lies behind a referral to a partner network is explained in the decoder [Werkstattnetz (repairer network)](/decoder/werkstatt-netz).

## The downstream costs nobody mentions

Part quality has an impact beyond the repair itself:

- **Wertminderung (diminished value)** – in the used-car market, repair quality is a pricing factor; documented original parts are viewed more favourably than "parts of unknown origin"
- **Resale** – when disclosing accident damage, part quality is a relevant factor
- **Manufacturer warranty and goodwill** – manufacturer entitlements may be tied to approved parts

The question is therefore not only whether the car drives again, but what it was repaired with.

## What to do now – concretely

1. **Review the repair specification.** The Gutachten (expert report) specifies the calculated part quality. That is your benchmark, not the insurer's deduction letter.
2. **Request justification if ident or used parts are proposed** – specifically: which part, which supplier, what constitutes equivalence.
3. **Do not sign anything that transfers the choice of parts to the insurer.** That decision belongs to the repair process, not to the claims settlement.
4. **Have all installed parts documented** – part numbers on the invoice are worth real money when you sell the vehicle.

### 💡 The most important rule

"Equivalent" is not a fact – it is a **claim that must be substantiated**. Whoever makes it must specify: equivalent in what respect – fit, approval, warranty, or price alone?
