# Acknowledgement of Liability (§ 212 BGB) vs. Settlement (§ 779 BGB) – the decisive difference

> **In brief:** A **settlement** (§ 779 BGB) resolves the dispute definitively — once payment is made, all claims are satisfied, including future ones (unless an explicit late-consequences reservation is agreed). An **acknowledgement of liability** (§ 212 BGB) merely confirms that the claim exists in principle, leaves the amount open, and restarts the limitation period from scratch. Insurers routinely dress up settlement offers as "acknowledgements" — the legal consequences are dramatically different.

## Also known as

Acknowledgement of debt · § 212 BGB restart of limitation period · settlement discharge · full-and-final clause · final settlement

---

## In one sentence

An acknowledgement of liability under § 212 BGB preserves your claim and restarts the limitation period — a settlement under § 779 BGB disposes of the claim for good.

## In three sentences

Insurers frequently draft settlement offers and acknowledgements in ways that blur the distinction — yet the gap between the two is enormous: an acknowledgement is a unilateral declaration by the insurer that the claim exists in principle, keeps the precise amount fully negotiable, and triggers a **complete restart of the limitation period** under § 212 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BGB. A settlement under § 779 BGB, by contrast, is a binding contract — once payment is made, all covered claims are discharged, including future late consequences, **unless an explicit reservation clause** has been agreed. The practical takeaway: accept an acknowledgement; only sign a settlement after legal review and with a late-consequences reservation in place.

## The five key figures

- **§ 212 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BGB** — restart of limitation period triggered by acknowledgement
- **§ 779 BGB** — settlement requiring mutual concessions
- **3 years** — new limitation period running from the date of acknowledgement
- **0** — late consequences recoverable after a settlement without reservation
- **Standard clause** "All claims are satisfied upon payment" — the most consequential clause you will ever sign

## Settlement versus acknowledgement — overview table

| Aspect | Settlement (§ 779 BGB) | Acknowledgement (§ 212 BGB) |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal nature** | Bilateral contract | Unilateral declaration |
| **Requirement** | Mutual concessions | Confirmation that claim exists in principle |
| **Effect** | Dispute definitively resolved | Claim preserved |
| **Amount** | Specifically agreed | Remains open |
| **Late consequences** | Discharged — unless reserved | Not discharged |
| **Limitation period** | Claim extinguished | 3-year period restarts |
| **Binding effect** | Full, in both directions | Only as to the existence of the claim |
| **Risk for injured parties** | High | Low — almost always advantageous |

## What an acknowledgement of liability means in practice

### Typical insurer formulations
> *"We acknowledge liability in principle."*
> *"Our policyholder is in principle liable."*
> *"We will settle the claim."*

### Three legal consequences
1. **Restart of limitation period** under § 212 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BGB — 3 years from the date of acknowledgement. This immediately defuses any [limitation-period concern](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195).
2. **Amount remains negotiable** — you retain full room to negotiate every individual head of damage.
3. **Burden of proof shifts** — a party that has acknowledged the basis of a claim cannot later contest it in general terms.

### Implied acknowledgement (konkludentes Anerkenntnis)
Even without explicit words, an insurer can acknowledge liability through conduct. Typical scenarios:
- **Interim payment** made without reservation
- **Appointment of its own expert** at the insurer's expense
- **Detailed enquiries** about specific heads of damage

→ The legal assessment is a matter for a lawyer in every case. Injured parties should, however, actively seek acknowledgements and have them confirmed in writing.

## What a settlement means in practice

### The standard clause — the most consequential clause you will ever sign

> *"This payment satisfies all claims arising from the loss event of [date]."*

Once this clause is fulfilled:
- All current claims are discharged
- **All future late consequences are discharged** — even those that were unforeseeable at the time
- No further claims can be made
- Rescission is only possible in extreme exceptional circumstances (e.g. fraudulent misrepresentation)

### When a settlement may make sense for you
- When the damage is fully settled and all consequences are medically consolidated
- When a legally drafted late-consequences reservation is included
- When the offered amount is demonstrably fair (settlement tables, BGH case law)

### When a settlement is problematic
- In personal injury cases within the first 24 months — late consequences often emerge only later
- When no medical certificate of consolidation has been obtained
- When you are suffering from negotiation fatigue
- When the insurer is pressing for a quick decision ("special offer", "year-end promotion")

## Late-consequences reservation — standard wording

This clause **must** appear in every settlement involving personal injury — without it, late consequences are lost forever.

```
LATE-CONSEQUENCES RESERVATION

Payment of the agreed amount satisfies all known claims
arising from the loss event of [date].

RESERVATION: Unforeseeable late consequences and all heads
of damage resulting from such late consequences (treatment
costs, loss of earnings, increases in pain-and-suffering
awards, care needs and additional requirements, increased
needs) are expressly EXCLUDED from this settlement and may
be asserted as and when they manifest.
```

Without this reservation, all future consequences you cannot yet anticipate today — such as chronic pain syndrome, post-traumatic stress disorder, or late-onset osteoarthritis — are covered by the settlement and permanently waived.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Insurer sends a settlement offer → **do not sign until a lawyer has reviewed it**. Insurer issues an acknowledgement → have it confirmed in writing and file it. These two steps protect you from the costliest mistakes in claims law.

### 🛠 Four steps when you receive a settlement offer

**1. Immediate response: do not accept**
Do not make a quick decision. A standard reply: *"Thank you for the offer. I will review it and get back to you."*

**2. Instruct a lawyer**
A specialist road-traffic lawyer will assess whether the offered amount aligns with the BGH case law and pain-and-suffering tables — and whether a late-consequences reservation must be included.

**3. Complete documentation of all damage**
For personal injury: medical certificate confirming consolidation. For property damage: all receipts collected.

**4. Negotiate the late-consequences reservation**
Mandatory for personal injury; advisable for concealed property-damage consequences.

### 🛠 When you receive an acknowledgement

**Request written confirmation.** Insurer acknowledgements are often made by telephone or vaguely worded in letters. You need a concrete written record for your file:

```
Dear Sir or Madam,

Thank you for your communication of [date]. I note that you
acknowledge liability in principle.

Please confirm this acknowledgement in writing so that it can
be documented in my file as an acknowledgement within the
meaning of § 212 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 BGB.

I will set out the specific heads of damage separately.

Yours faithfully,
[Signature]
```

## What insurers typically claim

> *"This payment satisfies all claims — including future ones."*

For personal injury without a late-consequences reservation: legally precarious. The BGH applies strict construction to full-and-final clauses — but the risk rests with the injured party. Legal advice is essential.

> *"Act quickly — otherwise you may receive less."*

Classic scarcity pressure. Where an acknowledgement has been given, there is **no urgency**, because the limitation period has restarted.

> *"We are not formally acknowledging anything — we are still reviewing."*

A possible delay tactic. Document implied acknowledgement conduct (part-payment, appointment of own expert) regardless — it carries the same legal weight.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I accept a settlement offer from the insurer?**
Not without legal review. First offers are statistically 40–60 % below what can legitimately be claimed. Furthermore: without a late-consequences reservation, future consequences are permanently lost.

**What does an acknowledgement of liability by the insurer mean?**
Very good news. The claim is acknowledged in principle, the limitation period restarts — you can negotiate without time pressure.

**Are late consequences still recoverable after a settlement?**
Only if an explicit late-consequences reservation appears in the settlement text. Without it: all late consequences are discharged.

**Can I undo a settlement?**
Only in extreme exceptional cases — if it is contrary to public policy (§ 138 BGB), obtained by fraudulent misrepresentation (§ 123 BGB), or affected by a mistake in declaration (§ 119 BGB). Rarely successful in practice.

**What is an implied acknowledgement (konkludentes Anerkenntnis)?**
An acknowledgement expressed through conduct rather than explicit words. Classic example: an interim payment by the insurer. It triggers the same legal consequence — the restart of the limitation period.

**Personal injury claims involving children — what do I need to consider?**
Settlements on behalf of minor injured parties frequently require approval by the family court (§ 1822 BGB). A late-consequences reservation is even more essential in these cases.
