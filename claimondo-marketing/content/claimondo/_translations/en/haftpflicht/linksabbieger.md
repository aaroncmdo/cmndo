# Left-Turn Accident – Liability in Collision with Oncoming Traffic

> **In brief:** § 9 StVO (German Road Traffic Regulations) imposes a **dual duty of care** on drivers turning left — they must yield to oncoming traffic AND exercise particular caution while turning. In a collision with oncoming traffic, the prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) typically places **70:30 to 100:0** liability on the turning driver; only provable gross negligence by the other party (significant speeding, crossing the centre line) can reduce that share.

## Also Known As

§ 9 StVO left turn · turning-vehicle collision · oncoming-traffic collision

## Liability Split by Scenario

| Scenario | Left-turning driver : Oncoming driver |
|---|---|
| Standard left turn without right of way | **100 : 0** |
| Left-turning driver + oncoming driver speeding | 70 : 30 |
| Left-turning driver + oncoming driver overtaking | 60 : 40 |
| Left turn on green arrow signal + oncoming driver runs red light | 0 : 100 |
| Both turning left simultaneously | 50 : 50 |

## Legal Framework

§ 9 Abs. 3 StVO: Any driver intending to turn must yield to oncoming vehicles and behave in a manner that rules out endangering other road users.

**Consequence:** The Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie presumption) operates against the turning driver. Even where the oncoming vehicle was apparently close, the duty of care rests with the driver making the turn.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is liable in a left-turn accident?**
As a rule, the left-turning driver — the Anscheinsbeweis applies against them. Standard split: 100:0.

**What if the oncoming driver was speeding?**
Where significant speeding is proven, a contributory share of 20–40 % may be attributed to the oncoming driver.

**What if there is a green left-turn arrow?**
A green arrow signal grants you right of way. If the oncoming driver runs a red light: 0:100 against them.
