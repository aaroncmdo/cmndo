# Failure to Observe Right-of-Way Signs – Liability at Stop Signs, "Yield" Signs, and Priority Roads

> **In brief:** Anyone who fails to observe a **stop sign (Z 206)** or a **yield sign (Z 205)** and is involved in an accident bears **full liability (100 : 0)** under the prima facie evidence rule (Anscheinsbeweis). On a priority road (Z 306), you as the right-of-way driver are protected — the standard split when the other party violates your right of way is likewise 100 : 0.

## Also known as

§ 8 StVO signs · stop sign violation · right-of-way violation · Z 205/206/306

---

## In one sentence

Anyone who fails to observe a stop sign or yield sign and collides bears full liability — standard split: 100 : 0.

## Split by scenario

| Situation | At-fault party : Injured party |
|---|---|
| Stop sign ignored | **100 : 0** |
| Yield sign ignored | **100 : 0** |
| Right-of-way driver travelling too fast | 80 : 20 |
| Right-of-way driver travelling significantly too fast | 70 : 30 |
| Both vehicles from minor road | 50 : 50 |

## Sign overview

- **Z 206 Stop** – complete stop before the line is mandatory
- **Z 205 Yield** – stop only if necessary; right of way must be clearly observed
- **Z 306 Priority road** – you have right of way on the yellow-bordered road
- **Z 301 Right of way** – individual intersection with right-of-way sign

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is liable when a stop sign is ignored?**
As a rule, the person who ignored the sign — the Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence rule) applies against them: 100 : 0.

**Can I as the right-of-way driver be assigned a share of liability?**
Only where significantly excessive speed is demonstrably proven (20–30 %). At normal speed you are fully protected.

**What if the sign was obscured?**
If the sign was objectively not recognisable, the Anscheinsbeweis may potentially be rebutted. The evidential requirements are high.
