# Turning on the Road – Liability in a Collision

> **In brief:** Anyone performing a U-turn bears the **highest duty of care** under § 9 Abs. 5 StVO (German Road Traffic Regulations) – they must conduct the manoeuvre in a way that rules out any risk to other road users. In a collision during a U-turn, the turning driver almost always bears **full liability (100 : 0)** under the prima facie evidence rule (Anscheinsbeweis).

## Also known as

§ 9 Abs. 5 StVO Wenden · Wendemanöver · U-Turn

## Fault split by scenario

| Scenario | Turning driver : Other party |
|---|---|
| U-turn on road, collision with moving traffic | **100 : 0** |
| U-turn + other party speeding | 80 : 20 |
| U-turn in dead end or ambiguous traffic situation | case-by-case |
| Reversing turn (often = reversing) | 100 : 0 |

## Legal framework

§ 9 Abs. 5 StVO: *"Anyone performing a U-turn must conduct themselves in a way that rules out any risk to other road users; if necessary, they must have themselves guided."*

**Consequence:** The turning driver bears responsibility towards all moving traffic. The Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence rule) applies in every collision that occurs during a U-turn.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is at fault in a U-turn accident?**
As a rule, the turning driver – the Anscheinsbeweis applies, standard split 100 : 0.

**What if the other party was speeding?**
Where a significant speed violation can be proven, a contributory share of 20 % is possible.

**Can I turn around anywhere?**
Not everywhere – at "no U-turn" signs and at locations with limited visibility, performing a U-turn may constitute a traffic offence (Ordnungswidrigkeit). In the event of a collision, this adds an additional element of fault.
