# Loss of Maintenance under § 844 Abs. 2 BGB – Dependency Benefits for Surviving Relatives

> **In brief:** Anyone who loses their maintenance provider (spouse, parent) in a fatal road accident is entitled to compensation for **lost maintenance** – either as an ongoing annuity or as a lump-sum capitalisation. Legal basis: § 844 Abs. 2 BGB. Calculation: hypothetical family income without the accident minus actual family income after the accident, multiplied by the surviving person's maintenance share. For widows/widowers: for life or until remarriage; for children: until economic independence.

## Also known as

§ 844 Abs. 2 BGB · Hinterbliebenenrente · Unterhaltsrente · Widow's/Orphan's liability pension

---

## Explained in one sentence

Unterhaltsschaden (loss of maintenance) is compensation for lost financial support for surviving dependants after a fatal road accident – payable as a lifelong annuity or as a lump sum.

## Explained in three sentences

When a person loses their maintenance-obligated family member (spouse, biological parent) in a fatal road accident, § 844 Abs. 2 BGB creates an independent claim for **compensation of lost maintenance**. The reference figure is the **hypothetical maintenance the deceased would have provided without the accident** – typically calculated from projected employment income and the family-typical maintenance share (spouse: ~40% of net family income; children: graduated by age and Düsseldorfer Tabelle). The claim runs **for life** or until the maintenance obligation ceases (remarriage, legal adulthood plus economic independence of children).

## The five key figures

- **§ 844 Abs. 2 BGB** as the legal basis
- **~40% of net family income** typical spousal share (aligned with Düsseldorfer Tabelle)
- **For life or until remarriage** duration for widows/widowers
- **Up to age 18/21/25** duration for children (depending on stage of education)
- **Six figures** typical total claims where a young parent is killed

---

## Who is entitled to claim

§ 844 Abs. 2 BGB protects:

- **Spouses / registered civil partners**
- **Parents** (only in very limited circumstances, where the deceased adult child had a concrete maintenance obligation)
- **Children** of the deceased
- **Unborn children** at the time of the accident – also recoverable after birth (Nasciturus protection)

→ Prerequisite: at the time of the accident, the deceased was **legally** obligated to provide maintenance to the surviving dependant – or would foreseeably have been so obligated (young parents, children still in education).

---

## Calculation framework

### Widow's/widower's annuity

```
Maintenance claim (widow/widower) = (Hypothetical income of deceased
                                     − actual income of surviving spouse)
                                   × maintenance share (typically 40%)
```

### Child maintenance

Calculated according to the Düsseldorfer Tabelle, graduated by:
- Age of the child
- Employment income of the person with the maintenance obligation
- Number of persons with maintenance obligations

→ Duration: until age 18; extended to age 21 or 25 if education continues.

---

## Duration and termination

### Widow's/widower's claim
- **For life** as the default rule
- **Ceases upon remarriage** – § 844 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BGB (unless otherwise agreed)
- **Adjusted** if the surviving spouse's own income increases significantly

### Children's claim
- Until **legal adulthood** (standard)
- Extended during school, university or vocational training (to approximately age 25)
- Ends upon economic independence

### Parents' claim
- Rare; only where there is concrete need and the deceased had an established maintenance obligation

## Evidence requirements

| Point | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Family relationship | Registry office certificate, family record book |
| Maintenance obligation | Family law assessment |
| Hypothetical income | Prior-year data, career projection, expert report |
| Actual income of surviving dependant | Tax assessments, pay slips |
| Düsseldorfer Tabelle reference | Ages of children at relevant dates |

---

## Annuity vs. capitalisation

| Form | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| **Ongoing annuity** | Adjustable to real-life changes | Risk of insurer insolvency |
| **Capitalisation** | Immediately available, plannable | Inflation risk, harder to vary later |

In practice, capitalisation is frequently agreed in settlement – requiring complex actuarial calculation.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much is the maintenance claim for a widow?**
Typically ~40% of the deceased's net family income, minus the widow's own income. Payable for life or until remarriage – total claims quickly reach six figures.

**Do children receive maintenance?**
Yes, graduated by age under the Düsseldorfer Tabelle. Paid until legal adulthood; extended to approximately age 25 during education.

**What happens if the widow remarries?**
The claim ceases as a matter of law (§ 844 Abs. 2 Satz 2 BGB) – unless otherwise agreed.

**Can I receive maintenance compensation and Hinterbliebenengeld at the same time?**
Yes. Hinterbliebenengeld is a flat-rate recognition of bereavement (€5,000–€15,000); Unterhaltsschaden is specific income replacement. Both are cumulative.

**What if the deceased was still a student?**
A claim exists if a maintenance obligation would foreseeably have arisen. A career projection is required.
