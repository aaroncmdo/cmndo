# Calibrating Driver Assistance Systems After an Accident – Why This Is Not an Optional Extra

> **In brief:** Emergency braking assistants, lane-keeping systems and adaptive cruise control read the road through cameras and radar sensors. Their alignment must be accurate to fractions of a degree. After work on the windscreen, bumper, wing mirrors or suspension, manufacturers prescribe **calibration**. It is part of a proper repair under §249 BGB (German Civil Code on full restoration of the pre-accident condition) – and yet it is one of the line items most readily struck from the assessor's report.

## Also known as

ADAS calibration · driver assistance system alignment · camera initialisation · sensor alignment · system calibration

## Explained in one sentence

If a repair involves moving, replacing or realigning a component that carries an assistance sensor, that sensor must be recalibrated afterwards – otherwise it operates on a system that no longer knows where it is pointing.

## Explained in three sentences

Modern vehicles carry their assistance sensors at precisely the spots that are hit first in an accident: the front camera behind the windscreen, the radar in the bumper, ultrasonic sensors in the valances, surround-view cameras in the wing mirrors. Even a misalignment of a few millimetres or one degree of angular deviation shifts the detection zone by several metres at a distance of 50 metres. Calibration restores the manufacturer-specified target state – making it not a comfort extra but the prerequisite for the systems to function reliably after the repair.

## The five key facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | §249 Abs. 2 BGB – necessary cost of restoration |
| **Standard of necessity** | the vehicle manufacturer's repair specification |
| **Typical triggers** | windscreen, bumper, mirror and suspension work |
| **Who assesses it** | the expert (Sachverständiger) and the repair shop, at the vehicle |
| **Who strikes it** | the assessor's service reviewing paperwork only |

## When calibration is required

The systems do not depend solely on their own sensor – they depend on the **geometry of the entire vehicle**. That is why calibration is not triggered only by replacing the sensor itself:

| Work on … | system affected |
|---|---|
| Windscreen (replacement or removal) | Front camera – lane-keeping, traffic sign recognition, emergency braking |
| Front bumper / radiator grille | Front radar – adaptive cruise control, emergency braking assistant |
| Wing mirrors | Surround-view camera – 360° view, blind-spot warning |
| Tailgate / rear bumper | Reversing camera, cross-traffic alert |
| Suspension, wheel alignment, steering | All direction-dependent systems |

The last point is overlooked most often: if wheel alignment changes the vehicle's thrust axis, a correctly mounted camera will still point away from its target zone. That is why manufacturers' specifications place calibration **after** suspension work – not before.

### Static or dynamic

- **Static** – carried out in the workshop using calibration targets at a defined distance, on a level surface and in controlled lighting. Requires space and specialist equipment.
- **Dynamic** – carried out during a test drive following the manufacturer's protocol (speed, road markings, weather conditions).
- **Combined** – many manufacturers require both in sequence.

Which method applies is determined by the manufacturer, not the repair shop – and certainly not the assessor's service.

## Why this is a safety issue

For most line items that are reduced, the dispute is about money. Here it is not only that.

A misaligned emergency braking assistant has two failure modes, and both are dangerous: it may react **too late or not at all**, because the obstacle is outside its detection zone. Or it triggers **without cause**, because it mistakes a roadside sign for a stationary vehicle – a full emergency stop for no reason, with traffic behind you.

What makes this insidious: neither failure is noticeable in everyday driving. The systems intervene rarely, and when they do, that is precisely the moment you rely on them. A warning symbol appears in the cockpit only if the sensor recognises itself as faulty – an intact but **misaligned** camera reports no error; it simply measures past reality.

> **Calibration is therefore not a line item to be negotiated away.** The repair shop is liable for carrying out the repair to the required standard. If the insurance strikes the item, that does not change the manufacturer's specification – it only changes who initially bears the cost.

## The typical reduction

| What the assessor's report says | What counters it |
|---|---|
| "Calibration not required" | The requirement follows from the manufacturer's specification for the specific repair procedure |
| "Included in the hourly rate" | Calibration is a separate process requiring dedicated equipment, not a step done in passing |
| "Only necessary when replacing the sensor itself" | Removal, suspension work and bodywork also trigger it |
| "Line item not comprehensible" | Comprehensible via manufacturer documentation and the workshop's calibration report |

How these assessor's reports come about – compiled from paperwork, without the vehicle being examined – is explained under [Expert costs](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten). The same mechanism affects [blending/adjacent panel respraying](/haftpflicht/beilackierung) (Beilackierung).

## What to do now – in concrete terms

1. **Request the calibration report.** Reputable workshops document the procedure, target values and result. This is the strongest counter-argument against a blanket reduction.
2. **Have the manufacturer's specification cited.** The workshop can show which repair step triggers the calibration requirement – this overrides the claim that it was "not required".
3. **Do not waive the item to avoid conflict.** A calibration that was not carried out saves no money; it moves a safety risk into your everyday driving.
4. **If the item is reduced, involve the expert (Sachverständiger)** – whether calibration was necessary is a technical question, not a commercial one.

### 💡 The most important rule

Whether calibration was required is stated in the **manufacturer's repair specification** – not in the insurer's reduction letter. Anyone striking this item is contradicting the company that built the vehicle.
