# Expert Report Costs After a Car Accident – Who Pays and How Much

> **Quick summary:** Expert report costs are an independent damage item under § 249 BGB (German Civil Code on damage compensation) and must be **fully reimbursed** by the opposing liability insurer (BGH VI ZR 67/06). Under established BGH case law, you have **free choice of expert**. Fees are based on the **BVSK-Honorartabelle** (BVSK fee schedule) — typically €300–€1,200 depending on the extent of damage. If the accident was not your fault, you effectively pay nothing.

## Also known as

SV-Kosten · Gutachterkosten · BVSK-Honorar · Sachverständigen-Honorar

---

## Explained in one sentence

Expert report costs are the fee charged by an independent automotive appraiser — in the event of a no-fault accident, they are borne entirely by the opposing liability insurer.

## Explained in three sentences

After a no-fault road accident, you are entitled to commission **your own independent automotive expert** (BVSK member or equivalent) — you place the instruction, and the opposing liability insurer bears the costs as a standalone damage item under § 249 BGB (BGH VI ZR 67/06). The fee level is based on the **BVSK-Honorartabelle**, with brackets tied to the extent of damage (HB I through HB V) — typical fees range from €300 to €1,200 for standard cases. Insurer reductions are unfounded under the BGH line of case law as long as the fee falls within the BVSK table.

## The five key figures

- **§ 249 BGB** — expert report costs as a damage item
- **BGH VI ZR 67/06** — the reimbursement principle
- **€300–€1,200** — typical fee range (standard cases)
- **5 fee levels (HB I–V)** in the BVSK-Honorartabelle
- **€0** — your cost in a no-fault accident

---

## Who can commission an expert

For property damage of roughly €750–€1,000 or more, the injured party is entitled to commission their own independent expert. Key points:

- **You** place the instruction — not the insurer
- Choice is **free** — a BVSK member is recommended
- Commission **before repairs begin** (preserving evidence!)
- Costs are borne by the opposing liability insurer (see below)

→ You are **not** required to accept an insurer's proposed "approved expert" — see [Decoder "Unser Sachverständiger"](/decoder/unser-sachverstaendiger).

---

## Fee levels under the BVSK-Honorartabelle

The BVSK-Honorartabelle assigns expert fees in steps based on the extent of damage:

| Fee level | Damage amount (repair costs or replacement value) | Indicative fee range |
|---|---|---|
| HB I | up to €750 | approx. €200–€280 |
| HB II | €750–€1,500 | approx. €280–€400 |
| HB III | €1,500–€5,000 | approx. €400–€700 |
| HB IV | €5,000–€15,000 | approx. €600–€1,200 |
| HB V | over €15,000 | individual, often €1,000–€2,500 |

→ These figures are indicative. Actual fees are determined by the current BVSK-Honorartabelle plus expenses (photographs, travel, drafting fee, VAT).

→ Full detail on all levels and typical insurer reduction wording: [Kfz-Gutachter Kosten-Tabelle (BVSK)](/decoder/kfz-gutachter-kosten-tabelle).

## What the case law says

### The reimbursement principle under BGH VI ZR 67/06
In established case law the BGH has confirmed: expert report costs are **necessary restoration costs** within the meaning of § 249 BGB. They are reimbursable regardless of whether the report ultimately leads to litigation — commissioning an expert in a damage situation is itself a necessary step.

### Fee level — BVSK-Honorartabelle as the benchmark
The BVSK-Honorartabelle is the recognised **basis for estimation**. Fees within the table are generally reimbursable — even where the insurer claims the fee is "excessive".

### The workshop-risk principle applies here too
If the expert charges too much, that is the **at-fault party's risk** (by analogy with the Werkstatt-Risiko principle). You are not required to monitor costs or choose the cheapest expert.

## What to do now — in concrete terms

### 💡 The most important rule
Commission your own expert before repairs begin. Without an expert report there is no reliable damage figure — and without a reliable damage figure, your negotiating position is weak.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Find a BVSK expert**
BVSK website (bvsk.de) → postcode search. Alternatively: recommendation from your workshop or lawyer.

**2. Written instruction within 7 days**
Clear scope: repair costs, replacement value (Wiederbeschaffungswert), residual value (Restwert, local market), diminished value (Wertminderung).

**3. Fee based on the BVSK-Honorartabelle**
The fee agreement should reference BVSK. This secures reimbursability.

**4. If the insurer reduces the fee: table and BGH line**
Refer to the BVSK bracket and BGH VI ZR 67/06. If there is a shortfall, involve a lawyer.

## What the insurer typically claims

> *"The expert costs are excessive — we will only reimburse [lower amount]."*

The BVSK-Honorartabelle is the reference. Fees within the table are reimbursable under the BGH line of case law.

> *"Commissioning your own expert was not necessary — our expert would have sufficed."*

Incorrect. Free choice of expert under the BGH line of case law. An insurer-nominated expert is a suggestion, not an obligation.

> *"We are reimbursing proportionally because the report covers other items too."*

Blanket reductions are unfounded. An expert report is a single, complete service.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who pays for the automotive expert after an accident?**
In a no-fault accident, the opposing liability insurer pays, as a standalone damage item (§ 249 BGB, BGH VI ZR 67/06). You do not pay upfront — the expert typically settles directly with the insurer.

**What does an automotive expert report cost?**
Fees follow the BVSK-Honorartabelle, depending on the extent of damage. Typical fees: €300–€1,200 (standard cases), €1,000–€2,500 for larger losses. Plus expenses and VAT.

**Can I choose my own expert?**
Yes. Free choice of expert under established BGH case law. You are not required to accept the insurer's recommendation.

**What is the BVSK-Honorartabelle?**
The standard fee schedule of the Bundesverband freiberuflicher und unabhängiger Sachverständiger (BVSK). It is the recognised basis for estimating expert fees in and out of court.

**From what damage amount is an expert worthwhile?**
From approximately €750–€1,000 in damage. For minor damage (Bagatell) a workshop cost estimate often suffices.

**What if the expert overcharges?**
Under the BGH Werkstatt-Risiko principle: that is the at-fault party's risk, not yours. You are not required to monitor it.
