# Burden of Proof in Traffic Accidents – Who Must Prove What

> **In brief:** In civil proceedings, each party must prove the facts that support its own position. For your claim settlement: you prove the accident and the extent of the damage – the insurer proves any alleged contributory negligence. In prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) situations, the burden of proof is even shifted **in your favour**. Thorough evidence preservation in the first 48 hours after the accident often decides the outcome of any later dispute.

## Also known as

Beweispflicht · Beweisverteilung · Onus probandi · Beweisrisiko

---

## Explained in one sentence

Burden of proof means: whoever asserts a fact in their own favour must also prove it – otherwise the court must assume the opposite to be true.

## Explained in three sentences

In a damages claim following a traffic accident, each party bears the burden of proof for the facts favourable to its position: the injured party proves the accident itself and the extent of the damage, while the insurer proves any alleged contributory negligence or violations of the duty to mitigate loss. In standardised situations ([Anscheinsbeweis](/haftpflicht/anscheinsbeweis) – prima facie evidence), the burden is reversed – you need not prove anything; the opposing party must specifically rebut the inference. In practice, the **quality of early evidence preservation** (police report, witnesses, photographs, medical certificates) often determines who prevails – well-documented facts are disputed far less frequently in out-of-court correspondence.

## The five key figures

- **3 pillars** of your burden of proof: accident + extent of damage + causation
- **3 pillars** of the insurer's burden of proof: contributory negligence + failure to mitigate + grounds for exoneration
- **§ 286 ZPO** – standard of proof: full judicial conviction
- **§ 287 ZPO** – reduced standard of proof for the quantum of damage
- **48 hours** – the critical window for proper evidence preservation

---

## Who must prove what – the basic structure

| Position | Burden of proof | Typical means of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The accident took place | Injured party | Police report, witnesses, photographs |
| Extent of damage | Injured party | Expert report (Sachverständigen-Gutachten), invoices, medical certificates |
| Causation (accident → damage) | Injured party | Expert report, medical findings |
| Contributory negligence of the injured party | Insurer | Police report, witnesses, expert report |
| Violation of duty to mitigate loss | Insurer | Medical opinions, comparative values |
| Force majeure / exoneration | Insurer | Expert witnesses, weather reports |
| Prima facie evidence / Anscheinsbeweis (against insurer) | Insurer must rebut | Concrete atypical facts |

## The two standards of proof – § 286 vs. § 287 ZPO

### § 286 ZPO – Full conviction
For facts that establish the claim (e.g. did the other party cause the accident?), the judge must be fully convinced – no reasonable doubt may remain.

### § 287 ZPO – Reduced standard for the quantum of damage
For the **amount** of the damage, a reduced standard applies: the judge estimates on the basis of free assessment. What this means in practice: even without millimetre-precise calculation, a claim for damages can be awarded – this is particularly important for psychological consequences, chronic conditions, and Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering compensation).

→ This § 287 ZPO rule defeats the insurer's standard boilerplate of *"not objectively verifiable"* in almost every case.

---

## What your burden of proof as the injured party covers

### 1. The accident itself
Who was involved, when, where, and how?

**Means of evidence:**
- Police report (if one was taken)
- Witnesses (secure names and addresses!)
- Your own record (notes, sketch)
- Photographs of the accident scene
- Dashcam footage (if available)

### 2. Extent of damage
What damage was caused, and to what amount?

**Evidence for property damage:**
- Expert report (Sachverständigen-Gutachten) from a BVSK-Sachverständiger
- Workshop invoice
- Receipts for rental car (Mietwagen), taxi, towing

**Evidence for personal injury:**
- Medical certificates (from day one)
- Sick notes
- Treatment invoices
- For psychological consequences: diagnosis from a qualified psychotherapist

### 3. Causation
Did the accident cause the damage claimed?

**Means of evidence:**
- Unbroken chain of treatment (GP → specialist → rehabilitation if applicable)
- Expert report (for disputed attribution of damage)
- Before/after comparison (e.g. herniated disc – recent STIR sequence in MRI)

---

## What the insurer must prove

### 1. Contributory negligence (§ 254 BGB)
If the insurer seeks to enforce a contributory negligence quota (Mitverschuldens-Quote), it must prove three things:
- A specific traffic violation
- Factual evidence of that violation
- A concrete causal link to the specific damage

If any one of these is missing, the quota is unfounded.

### 2. Violation of the duty to mitigate loss (§ 254 Abs. 2 BGB)
If the insurer claims you could have reduced the damage (e.g. renting a car for too long, neglecting therapy), it bears the burden of proof – what was the reasonable alternative, and why did you not use it.

### 3. Grounds for exoneration (e.g. force majeure under § 7 Abs. 2 StVG)
Rarely successful – wild animal collisions or unavoidable weather conditions are interpreted narrowly under traffic law.

---

## What you should do now – in concrete terms

### 💡 The most important rule
Evidence loses probative force over time. The first 48 hours after the accident are critical – anything you fail to document then is difficult to reconstruct later.

### 🛠 Four steps to thorough evidence preservation

**1. At the scene (within minutes)**
- Call the police where required (injury, significant damage, disputed facts)
- Photographs: damage, final positions of vehicles, skid marks, traffic signs, lighting conditions, the other party's personal details
- Approach witnesses – names, phone numbers, addresses
- Accident sketch

**2. On the same day (within hours)**
- Go to A&E or your GP, **even if you feel fine** – adrenaline masks pain
- File a damage notification with your own insurer
- Start a first symptom diary

**3. Within the first 7 days**
- Commission an expert report (Sachverständigen-Gutachten) from a BVSK-Sachverständiger
- Plan the workshop repair
- Request the police report (available approximately 2–4 weeks after it was taken)

**4. Ongoing**
- Archive all receipts and documents
- Continue the symptom diary
- For all correspondence with the insurer: use registered mail and archive the delivery receipts

## What insurers typically claim

> *"You need to prove that – we assume that…"*

In an Anscheinsbeweis situation: the burden of proof lies with the insurer, not with you. Point this out in writing.

> *"Without proof, no reimbursement."*

The reduced standard of proof under § 287 ZPO applies to the quantum of damage. Reject blanket statements of this kind.

> *"Your medical certificates are not sufficiently meaningful."*

Ask which specific findings the insurer considers to be missing. Do not accept blanket criticism – request a precise list of what is required, or take the dispute to court (where a court-appointed expert will decide).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who must prove who is at fault?**
As a general rule, the injured party bears the burden of proving the other party's fault. In Anscheinsbeweis situations (rear-end collision, right of way, red-light violation), the burden is reversed – the other party must rebut the inference.

**What do I need to provide as evidence?**
Police report, expert report (Sachverständigen-Gutachten), medical certificates, photographs, witness details. For personal injury claims, also sick notes and treatment records.

**Do I have to prove that I am not at fault?**
No. Contributory negligence (Mitverschulden) must be proven by the insurer – you do not have to prove your own freedom from fault.

**What is the reduced standard of proof under § 287 ZPO?**
For the **amount** of the damage, an estimate by the judge is sufficient – the full standard of proof required for establishing that the damage **arose** does not apply. This is particularly important for chronic pain, psychological consequences, and Schmerzensgeld.

**How long do I have to preserve evidence?**
Evidence continuously loses probative force. The first 48 hours are critical; the first 4 weeks are very important. Later reconstruction is possible but more difficult.

**Can dashcam footage be used as evidence?**
Yes – the BGH (VI ZR 233/17) has recognised dashcam recordings as admissible in court, provided they are justifiable under data protection law (event-triggered recording, not continuous full recording).
