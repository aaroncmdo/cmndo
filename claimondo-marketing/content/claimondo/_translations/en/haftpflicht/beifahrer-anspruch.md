# Passenger Claims in Road Traffic Accidents

> **Quick summary:** Passengers and co-riders have **independent, fully enforceable claims** in road traffic accidents — regardless of which vehicle was at fault. If your own vehicle was at fault: claims against the **driver's own liability insurance**. If the other vehicle was at fault: claims against that vehicle's liability insurance. Seat belt obligations apply — a violation can lead to [contributory negligence](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-bgb254) (typically 20–30 %).

## Also known as

Co-rider claim · Occupant claim · Passenger claims

---

## Explained in one sentence

Passengers have full, independent claims for Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering compensation), medical treatment costs, and all further personal injury damages — regardless of which vehicle was at fault.

## Explained in three sentences

Passengers and co-riders are **primary injured parties** (Geschädigte) under German damages law with independent claims — their position is even more favourable than the driver's in one respect: they always have full claims, regardless of which vehicle was at fault. If the other vehicle was at fault: claims against its liability insurance (§ 115 VVG); if the **driver's own** vehicle was at fault: claims against the **driver's own** liability insurance — meaning the relative's or friend's insurer pays. Failure to wear a seat belt can lead to [contributory negligence](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-bgb254); otherwise, full reimbursement of all personal injury heads of damage applies.

## The five key figures

- **§ 7 StVG, § 18 StVG, § 823 BGB** as legal bases for claims
- **§ 115 VVG** — direct claim against the insurer
- **§ 21a StVO** — seat belt obligation (contributory negligence if violated)
- **20–30 %** — typical reduction for not wearing a seat belt where injuries result
- **0 %** contributory negligence when seat belt is worn

---

## Passenger scenarios

### 1. Accident caused by the other vehicle
- The passenger has full claims **against the opposing liability insurer**
- Split 100 : 0 in your favour (provided no contributory negligence on your part)
- Legal costs are paid by the opposing insurer

### 2. Accident caused by your own vehicle
- The passenger has full claims **against the driver's own liability insurer**
- Even if the driver is a family member or friend — the insurer pays regardless of the personal relationship
- Important: the co-rider's damages do **not** affect the driver's no-claims bonus (Schadenfreiheitsklasse, SF-Klasse) under liability insurance (unlike comprehensive cover)

### 3. Shared fault
- The passenger has claims **against both liability insurers** (joint and several liability)
- You may choose which insurer to pursue first — the insurers settle the internal allocation between themselves (§ 426 BGB)

---

## Seat belt — the contributory negligence question

§ 21a StVO requires all occupants to wear a seat belt. Anyone who was **not buckled up when injuries result** risks a contributory negligence reduction — if the insurer proves that the specific injury would have been **prevented or reduced** by the belt.

| Situation | Contributory negligence |
|---|---|
| Seat belt worn | 0 % |
| No seat belt, no injury | 0 % (de minimis) |
| No seat belt, minor whiplash | 0–20 % |
| No seat belt, serious head/facial injury | 20–40 % |
| Child without car seat where injuries result | 30–50 % (parental contributory negligence) |

→ Reject blanket insurer assertions — demand a concrete causal justification.

## What claims a passenger has

Identical to other primary injured parties (see [Geschädigte primär](/haftpflicht/geschaedigte-primaer)):

- **[Schmerzensgeld § 253 BGB](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253)** — amount assessed under Hacks/Wellner
- **[Medical treatment costs](/haftpflicht/heilbehandlungskosten)** — in full
- **[Loss of earnings](/haftpflicht/verdienstausfall)** — for employed persons
- **[Haushaltsführungsschaden (household impairment damages)](/haftpflicht/haushaltsfuehrungsschaden)** — where household capacity is affected
- **Property damages** — personal items damaged in the accident (glasses, laptop, phone)
- **[Vermehrte Bedürfnisse (increased needs)](/haftpflicht/vermehrte-beduerfnisse)** — for lasting consequences
- **[Loss of earning capacity](/haftpflicht/erwerbsminderungsschaden)** — for permanent reduction in earning capacity

## Special case: passenger in a vehicle driven negligently by a family member

A common real-world scenario — a family member drives negligently and a passenger is injured. Key points:

- **The driver-relative's own liability insurer** pays — no "family discount", no waiver
- Even a child can bring a claim against the parent's liability insurer (provided the child is over 10 years old; below that age, no contributory fault applies in any event)
- The passenger and driver may remain in an ongoing close relationship after the accident — the insurer still pays objectively

→ This is therefore not a "brother against brother" conflict — the claim runs legally against the insurance company, not personally against the family member.

---

## What insurers typically claim

> *"Passengers have limited claims."*

Incorrect. Passengers are full primary injured parties with all associated claims.

> *"You should have worn your seat belt — full reduction."*

Even without a seat belt the reduction is capped (20–40 %), not total.

> *"No claim exists due to the family relationship."*

Incorrect. The claim exists objectively against the insurer, independent of the relationship with the driver.

---

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Passenger claims are fully enforceable — regardless of which vehicle was at fault. A family relationship is no bar.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Document injuries medically**
Within 24 hours — even for seemingly minor complaints.

**2. Identify the correct respondent**
If the driver was at fault: their insurer. If the other vehicle was at fault: that vehicle's insurer.

**3. Engage a lawyer**
At no cost to you (BGH VI ZR 235/13). A family relationship is no obstacle.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have a claim as a passenger?**
Yes, in full. Passengers are primary injured parties with independent claims — regardless of which vehicle was at fault.

**What if my own driver was at fault?**
Claims go against the driver's own liability insurer. The relative's or friend's insurer pays — objectively, without personal conflict.

**Does not wearing a seat belt reduce my claim?**
Possibly. Where injuries result, a contributory negligence reduction of typically 20–30 % applies, if the insurer proves the belt would have prevented the injury.

**Do I have to sue my own brother/father/friend?**
No. The action is brought against the liability insurer, not personally against the family member.

**What if both drivers were at fault?**
The passenger has claims against both liability insurers — jointly and severally. You choose which to approach first.
