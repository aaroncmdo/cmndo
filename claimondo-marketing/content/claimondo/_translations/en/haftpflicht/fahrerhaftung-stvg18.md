# Driver Liability under § 18 StVG – Presumption of Fault and Exculpatory Evidence

> **In brief:** § 18 StVG establishes the **personal liability of the vehicle driver** in addition to the holder's liability (§ 7 StVG). Unlike the holder, the driver is **presumed to be at fault** — they must actively prove that they bear **no fault** (exculpatory burden under § 18 Abs. 1 Satz 2 StVG). In practice, at least one of these provisions almost always applies in favour of the injured party — § 7 StVG (holder) and § 18 StVG (driver) combined create a strong evidentiary position in your favour.

## Also known as

§ 18 StVG · Driver fault liability · Operator liability

---

## Explained in one sentence

Under § 18 StVG, the driver is personally liable for traffic accident damages unless they actively prove that they bear no fault — a presumed fault liability.

## Explained in three sentences

While § 7 StVG holds the **vehicle holder** liable regardless of fault, § 18 StVG governs the personal **fault liability** of the driver — with the critical difference that **fault is presumed**. To exonerate themselves, the driver must positively prove that they observed the **standard of care required in traffic** — otherwise their liability applies alongside the holder's liability towards the injured party. Since holder and driver are often, but not always, the same person (rental car, company car, family vehicle), this dual structure gives the injured party (Geschädigter) a powerful lever in virtually every traffic accident scenario.

## The five key figures

- **§ 18 StVG** as the legal basis for claims against the driver
- **Presumption of fault** — burden of proof reversed
- **§ 18 Abs. 1 Satz 2** — exculpatory proof required
- **§ 7 StVG (holder) + § 18 StVG (driver)** — parallel liability
- **§ 115 VVG** — direct claim against the liability insurer

---

## What § 18 StVG means in legal terms

Driver liability supplements holder liability — both run in parallel, giving the injured party two liable parties (plus the liability insurer as a direct debtor).

### Three requirements

1. **Vehicle driver** — the person operating the vehicle at the time of the accident
2. **"During operation"** — functional connection with the vehicle
3. **Injury to life, body, health, or property**

### Fault — presumed
The **key difference** from § 823 BGB: § 823 BGB requires the injured party to **prove fault**. § 18 StVG reverses this — fault is **presumed**, and the driver must prove to the contrary that they observed the **standard of care required in traffic**.

---

## Exculpatory proof under § 18 Abs. 1 Satz 2 StVG

The driver can only exonerate themselves by positively proving:

- The accident was **unavoidable** despite all care required in traffic
- OR: The accident resulted from an **inevitable event**

In practice, exculpatory proof is **difficult to establish** — court statistics show success rates below 20%. Insurers nonetheless invoke it, particularly in ambiguous situations.

### What does **not** constitute exculpatory proof
- "I didn't see anything" — not due care
- "It was fate" — too vague
- "The other party made a mistake" — does not automatically exonerate

### What **can** constitute exculpatory proof
- Technical defect with no identifiable maintenance gap
- Sudden, unexpected conduct by a third party without prior warning
- Black ice situation despite appropriately reduced speed

→ In classic prima facie evidence scenarios (rear-end collision, right of way, red light) exculpatory proof is practically excluded.

---

## Holder and driver — who is liable and how

### Same person
When holder and driver are the same person (the most common case), § 7 StVG **and** § 18 StVG apply simultaneously in your favour — you have a dual legal basis for your claim.

### Different persons
Where they are separate persons (rental car, family vehicle, hire car), liability is as follows:
- The **holder** is liable regardless of fault under § 7 StVG
- The **driver** is liable under presumed fault under § 18 StVG
- Both are jointly and severally liable (§ 421 BGB) — you can claim the full amount from either

### Practical consequence
The simplest approach is to proceed directly against the **holder's liability insurer** (§ 115 VVG) — this covers both holder and driver liability.

---

## Relationship to § 7 StVG, § 823 BGB, and § 17 StVG

| Provision | Basis of liability | Fault |
|---|---|---|
| **§ 7 StVG** | Holder liability | No (operational risk) |
| **§ 18 StVG** | Driver liability | Yes, but presumed |
| **§ 823 BGB** | General tort | Yes, to be proved by the injured party |
| **§ 17 StVG** | Contributory liability between vehicles | Proportional to causation |

In practice, the first two provisions regularly apply in parallel — this is the system of **dual liability basis** in favour of the injured party (Geschädigter).

---

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
You do **not** need to prove driver fault. § 18 StVG reverses the burden of proof — the driver must exonerate themselves.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Identify holder and driver**
The police report contains both parties' details. Reference both in any correspondence with the insurer.

**2. Direct claim against the insurer**
§ 115 VVG. The holder's liability insurance also covers driver-related damages.

**3. If exculpatory proof is invoked: consult a lawyer**
The insurer's argument of "no driver fault" is usually unsustainable — a specialist lawyer (Fachanwalt) will clarify.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the driver liable for the accident?**
Yes — under § 18 StVG. Fault is presumed; the driver must positively prove that they observed the required standard of care to exonerate themselves.

**What is the difference between holder and driver?**
Holder = the person who operates the vehicle on their own account (pays insurance, tax). Driver = the person operating it at the time of the accident. Often the same person, often not. The holder is liable regardless of fault (§ 7 StVG); the driver is liable under presumed fault (§ 18 StVG).

**What if the driver borrowed the vehicle?**
The holder is liable under § 7 StVG. The driver is additionally liable under § 18 StVG. Both are jointly and severally liable.

**Can the driver exonerate themselves?**
Theoretically yes, through exculpatory proof under § 18 Abs. 1 Satz 2 StVG. In practice, this rarely succeeds — court success rates are below 20%.

**Do I need to sue both?**
No. § 115 VVG allows a direct claim against the holder's liability insurer, which covers all liabilities.
