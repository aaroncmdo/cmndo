# Accident Abroad – Claim Settlement via the Green Card System

> **In brief:** For road accidents **abroad** (EU + Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway), claim settlement is handled through the **Green Card system** and the German **"Büro Grüne Karte"** (Green Card Bureau) — you do not need to litigate abroad. For accidents **in Germany** involving a foreign vehicle, the **Deutscher Verkehrsversicherer e.V. (DVS)** is the entry point for settlement. Proceedings are complex, processing times are longer (3–6 months), and a specialist lawyer is strongly recommended.

## Also Known As

Green Card system · IB procedure · ZB Grüne Karte · international claim settlement

## Two Main Scenarios

### 1. You Are Abroad, the At-Fault Party Is Foreign
- Call the police and obtain an accident report in the country where the accident occurred
- Complete a **European Accident Statement** (available in multiple languages)
- Locate the at-fault party's insurer via the Green Card or their claims representative in Germany
- File your claim with the German **claims representative** of the foreign insurer — or with the German Büro Grüne Karte

### 2. A Foreign Driver in Germany Is the At-Fault Party
- Deutscher Verkehrsversicherer e.V. (DVS) handles the case like a German insurer would
- Proceedings are considerably simpler than pursuing a claim abroad

## EU vs. Non-EU

**EU + EEA + Switzerland** (Green Card system):
- Insurers settle claims across borders
- Every EU insurer is required to have a German claims representative

**Non-EU** (e.g. Turkey, Russia, USA):
- More complex, slower proceedings
- Local legal assistance often required
- Risk: invalid or unenforceable insurance coverage

## Applicable Law

**Rome II Regulation** (for EU countries): in principle, the law of the **country where the accident occurred** applies, with limited scope for choice of law in specific constellations.

→ In practice: pain-and-suffering compensation (Schmerzensgeld) levels can vary significantly — Italy, for example, uses a tariff-based scale that typically falls well below German levels, while France is sometimes higher. Legal strategy: securing jurisdiction in Germany wherever possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Green Card?**
An international proof of insurance. It is no longer mandatory to carry it in the EU, EEA, or Switzerland (number plate recognition suffices), but it remains the procedural foundation of the system.

**Who handles the claim for an accident abroad?**
The at-fault party's insurer — or their German claims representative. For an accident in France involving a French at-fault party: the German representative of the French insurer handles settlement in Germany.

**How much Schmerzensgeld (pain-and-suffering compensation) is awarded for an accident abroad?**
It depends on the law of the country where the accident occurred. Italy is typically lower, France sometimes higher than Germany. Legal advice is essential.

**What applies for a non-EU accident?**
Expect longer proceedings, often requiring local lawyers. Travel legal expenses insurance is worthwhile.
