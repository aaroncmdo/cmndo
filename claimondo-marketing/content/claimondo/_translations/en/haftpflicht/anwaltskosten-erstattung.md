# Legal Fees in Road Accident Claims – Who Pays and How They Are Calculated

> **In brief:** When a road accident is not your fault, legal fees are an **independent damage item** that the opposing party's liability insurer must **reimburse in full** (BGH, judgment of 08.07.2014, VI ZR 235/13). Calculation follows the Rechtsanwaltsvergütungsgesetz (RVG – German Lawyers' Remuneration Act): the business fee (Geschäftsgebühr) is typically **1.3** (standard) to **2.3** (for cases of particular complexity), applied to the claim value (Streitwert, i.e. the total loss amount). In practice, this means you pay €0 for legal representation – regardless of how complicated the matter becomes.

## Also Known As

Anwaltsgebühren-Erstattung · RVG-Erstattung · Geschäftsgebühr · Außergerichtliche Vertretungskosten · Schwellengebühr

---

## In One Sentence

When liability is clear, legal fees in road accident claims are borne in full by the opposing party's liability insurer – as an independent damage item under BGH VI ZR 235/13.

## In Three Sentences

Reimbursement of legal fees is one of the most important BGH principles for injured parties: with its judgment of 08.07.2014 (VI ZR 235/13), the Bundesgerichtshof (Federal Court of Justice) confirmed that instructing a lawyer is **necessary** within the meaning of § 249 BGB (German Civil Code on damage compensation) – even in straightforward cases – and that the costs are therefore fully reimbursable. Calculation follows the RVG: the standard Geschäftsgebühr is 1.3 applied to the Streitwert (= total loss amount); in particularly complex cases (personal injury, late-onset consequences, multiple parties involved) up to 2.3. Proportionally reduced where contributory negligence applies, fully covered where liability is clear: you take on no cost risk by instructing a lawyer now.

## The Five Key Figures

- **§ 249 BGB** – legal fees as an independent damage item
- **BGH VI ZR 235/13** – judgment of 08.07.2014 (landmark decision)
- **1.3** – standard Geschäftsgebühr (Schwellengebühr, i.e. threshold fee)
- **2.3** – maximum Geschäftsgebühr for cases of particular complexity
- **€0** – your cost where the accident is not your fault

---

## The Legal Basis for Reimbursement

Under § 249 BGB, the party responsible for the damage owes Naturalrestitution (restitution in kind) – they must restore you to the position you would have been in without the accident. Legal fees are included where instructing a lawyer was **necessary**.

### The BGH Standard – "Necessary" Is Interpreted Broadly

With its judgment of 08.07.2014 (VI ZR 235/13), the BGH clarified that necessity is **to be interpreted broadly**. Even in straightforward cases, the injured party may instruct a lawyer – not least because insurers routinely deploy complex tactics (["We are reviewing the matter"](/decoder/wir-pruefen-sachverhalt), [blanket contributory negligence quotas](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent), Restwert (salvage value) disputes).

This removes from the insurer its common counter-argument *"You could have handled the claim yourself"* – even in ostensibly clear-cut cases, instructing a lawyer is justified.

---

## How Legal Fees Are Calculated Under the RVG

Lawyers' remuneration is governed by the Rechtsanwaltsvergütungsgesetz (RVG). Three components combine to produce the total invoice:

### 1. Geschäftsgebühr (Primary Component)
Applied to the **Streitwert** (= total loss amount). The multiplier (factor) depends on complexity and scope:

| Factor | Application |
|---|---|
| **0.5–1.3** | Simple matters – rare |
| **1.3 (Schwellengebühr)** | Standard in road accident cases |
| **1.5–1.8** | Personal injury with Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering) calculation |
| **2.3 (maximum fee)** | Complex matters, late-onset consequences, multiple parties |

### 2. Flat Expense Allowance
€20 flat rate for postage and telecommunications (RVG VV No. 7002).

### 3. VAT
19% on the fee and the flat rate.

### Worked Example – Loss of €15,000

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Geschäftsgebühr 1.3 on €15,000 (RVG schedule) | approx. €970 |
| Flat rate postage/telecommunications | €20 |
| Subtotal | €990 |
| 19% VAT | approx. €188 |
| **Total** | **approx. €1,178** |

Where liability is clear, the insurer bears this amount **in full**.

## When a Higher Fee (1.5–2.3) Is Justified

The BGH recognises higher factors in the following situations:

- **Personal injury** with Schmerzensgeld calculation (typically 1.5–1.8)
- **Risk of late-onset consequences** and settlement negotiations with reservation of rights
- **Multiple parties** (collision involving 3+ vehicles)
- **Disputed contributory negligence** (cf. [Mitverschulden 30%](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent))
- **Complex medical late-onset consequences** (PTSD, chronic pain syndrome)

In these cases, an experienced road traffic lawyer bills a **1.5 to 2.3 fee** – and the insurer must justify why it should not be reimbursed.

## What to Do Now – Concretely

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Where the accident is not your fault: **instructing a lawyer costs you nothing**. You take on no financial risk – the insurer pays. Failing to use this right means forfeiting, on average, 20–40% of legitimate claims.

### 🛠 When Legal Representation Is Essential
- For **every personal injury claim** – even apparently minor injuries (risk of late-onset consequences!)
- For **property damage exceeding €1,000**
- Where **liability is disputed** or contributory negligence is alleged
- When the **insurer deploys tactics** (delays, blanket reductions, Restwert disputes)
- When the **insurer makes a settlement offer**

### 🛠 How to Find a Lawyer

- **Fachanwalt für Verkehrsrecht** (specialist road traffic lawyer) – the recognised specialist qualification in this field
- **ARGE Verkehrsrecht** within the Deutschen Anwaltverein (DAV – German Bar Association) – recognised association of specialists
- **Recommendation from your expert appraiser** – KFZ-Sachverständige (motor vehicle expert appraisers) know the experienced firms in their region
- **Online search** – anwaltverein.de, anwalt.de or directly verkehrsrecht.de

### 🛠 First Steps with Your Lawyer

1. **Sign the Vollmacht (power of attorney)** – work can begin
2. **Hand over the file** – accident report, damage notification, Gutachten (expert report), medical certificates
3. **Lawyer sends formal letter of claim** – with a four-week deadline
4. **Direct billing** lawyer ↔ insurer – you do not need to front the costs

## What the Insurer Typically Claims

> *"Legal representation is not necessary – you could have handled the claim yourself."*

Incorrect under BGH VI ZR 235/13. Necessity is to be interpreted broadly – a lawyer may be instructed even in straightforward cases. Particularly where the insurer itself is deploying tactics (blanket reductions, delays), legal representation is in fact required.

> *"A Geschäftsgebühr of 1.0 is sufficient for this simple matter."*

The Schwellengebühr of 1.3 is the standard in road accident cases. Lower factors apply only in exceptionally simple constellations – where there is an insurance dispute (blanket quota, delay), higher factors are more likely to be justified.

> *"Legal fees are only reimbursable where a lawyer was actually instructed."*

Correct – but that is not an argument against instructing one. Once a lawyer is engaged, the reimbursement entitlement exists. There is no such thing as notional billing of legal fees.

> *"Where contributory negligence applies, reimbursement is proportional."*

Where a quota has actually been established: correct. However, a blanket contributory negligence allegation by the insurer does not entitle it to unilaterally reduce reimbursement – the quota must be legally established.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who pays the lawyer in a road accident claim?**
Where the accident is not your fault, the opposing party's liability insurer pays – as an independent damage item under § 249 BGB and BGH VI ZR 235/13. You take on no cost risk.

**How much are the legal fees?**
Calculated under the RVG: Geschäftsgebühr 1.3 (standard) to 2.3 (complex cases) on the Streitwert, plus a €20 flat rate and 19% VAT. For a loss of €15,000: approx. €1,178 total.

**Do I have to instruct a lawyer?**
There is no obligation to do so. In practice, however, it is strongly recommended: without a lawyer, statistically 20–40% of legitimate claims are lost. And the costs are borne by the insurer regardless.

**What if I am partly at fault?**
Where a contributory negligence quota has been established, reimbursement is proportional. Example: at 30% contributory negligence, the insurer bears 70% of the legal fees. Where liability falls entirely on the at-fault party: 100%.

**Are litigation costs also reimbursable?**
Yes. Where a claim succeeds in court, the insurer bears the court costs and legal fees for both instances. Where success is partial, costs are apportioned in proportion to the amounts claimed (§ 92 ZPO).

**What if I have legal expenses insurance?**
Where you hold a Verkehrs-Rechtsschutzversicherung (road traffic legal expenses insurance): your insurer initially covers the legal fees and later recovers them from the opposing party's liability insurer (subrogation, § 86 VVG by analogy). The practical effect for you is the same: no cost risk.
