# Black Ice and Aquaplaning – Who Is Liable for Weather-Related Accidents

> **In brief:** Black ice, slush, and aquaplaning are **not** classified as acts of God — they are **foreseeable traffic risks** that every driver must manage through **appropriate speed** (§ 3 StVO) and adapted driving behaviour. A driver who loses control on an icy or wet road and causes an accident typically bears **full or predominant liability** (50 : 50 to 100 : 0). Invoking "sudden black ice" as a defence only succeeds in demonstrably **unexpected** changes in road conditions.

## Also Known As

§ 3 StVO speed requirement · weather-related accident · skidding accident · aquaplaning scenario

## Liability Split by Scenario

| Scenario | Split |
|---|---|
| Skidding on standard black ice | **100 : 0** against the skidding driver |
| Skidding on suddenly emerging black ice (e.g. bridge, shaded section) | 50 : 50 (shared liability) |
| Aquaplaning at a demonstrably excessive speed | 100 : 0 |
| Aquaplaning at appropriate speed + sudden road dip | 50 : 50 |
| Rear-ending a skidding vehicle | 0 : 100 (prima facie evidence against the rear driver) |

## Legal Framework

§ 3 StVO: *"Speed must be adapted in particular to road, traffic, visibility, and weather conditions."*

**Consequence:** Adapting to weather conditions is a statutory duty. Acts of God under § 7 Abs. 2 StVG do **not** apply to standard weather scenarios — the driver is required to control the risk through appropriate speed.

## What the Insurance Typically Claims

> *"Act of God due to sudden black ice — no liability."*

A standard defence argument that almost never holds up. Acts of God are reserved for truly extraordinary circumstances — routine winter weather does not qualify.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is liable in a black-ice accident?**
As a rule, the skidding driver — appropriate speed was not maintained. Standard split: 100 : 0.

**What if the black ice appeared suddenly (bridge, shaded section)?**
Where a demonstrably unexpected change in road surface can be proven, a shared liability of 50 % is possible.

**Is black ice an act of God?**
No. Acts of God are limited to truly extraordinary circumstances such as storm damage or natural disasters.

**What about aquaplaning?**
The same applies — appropriate speed was mandatory. Where an unexpectedly flooded road surface can be demonstrated, shared liability may apply; otherwise 100 : 0.
