# Overtaking Accident – Who Is Liable in an Overtaking Manoeuvre

> **In brief:** Overtaking is governed by § 5 StVO (German Road Traffic Regulations), which imposes a **heightened duty of care** — overtaking is only permitted with clear visibility, without obstructing oncoming traffic, and with sufficient lateral clearance. Anyone who causes an accident while overtaking typically bears **80:20 to 100:0** fault. Significant shared liability of the overtaken vehicle only applies where that driver committed a demonstrable violation (sudden acceleration, left turn without indicating).

## Also Known As

§ 5 StVO overtaking · overtaking collision · cut-in accident

## Fault Split by Scenario

| Scenario | Overtaker : Overtaken |
|---|---|
| Overtaking without clear visibility, head-on collision with oncoming traffic | **100:0** against overtaker |
| Overtaking with insufficient lateral clearance | 100:0 |
| Cutting back in too early | 100:0 |
| Overtaking + overtaken vehicle turning left without indicating | 50:50 |
| Overtaking in a no-overtaking zone | 100:0 |
| Overtaking + overtaken vehicle suddenly accelerates | 70:30 |

## Legal Framework

§ 5 StVO:
- Overtaking is only permitted where it is clearly foreseeable that no other road user will be obstructed
- Sufficient lateral clearance must be maintained
- Clear visibility conditions are required
- Oncoming traffic must not be impeded

**Consequence:** The highest standard of care applies when overtaking; the prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) operates against the overtaking driver.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is liable in an overtaking accident?**
As a rule, the overtaking driver — the Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie presumption of fault) applies against them. Standard split: 100:0.

**What if the overtaken driver suddenly accelerates?**
A shared liability of up to 30% is possible, provided the sudden acceleration can be proven.

**What applies when overtaking is prohibited?**
Violating a no-overtaking prohibition constitutes fault — the overtaking driver bears full liability.
