# Default Interest Under § 288 BGB in Claim Settlement

> **In brief:** When a liability insurer is in default on claim settlement, it owes **5 percentage points of default interest above the base rate** (§ 288 para. 1 BGB). With a base rate typically between 0–4 %, that translates to **5–9 % default interest per year**. Interest runs from the first day of default, constitutes an independent claim, and is calculated on the full legitimate claim amount. It is your additional lever in claim settlement.

## Also known as

Default interest · Debtor interest · Default interest rate · Default damage interest

---

## Explained in one sentence

Default interest is the statutory minimum loss you incur when the liability insurer is in default — 5 percentage points above the base rate on the entire legitimate claim amount (§ 288 para. 1 BGB).

## Explained in three sentences

Default interest supplements the principal claim with a statutory interest component as soon as [default](/haftpflicht/verzug-bgb286) occurs — typically 4 weeks after a complete claim notification has been received by the insurer where liability is clear. The rate is **5 percentage points above the base rate** of the Deutsche Bundesbank (§ 288 para. 1 BGB) and is adjusted every six months. Interest accrues until actual payment; it stands independently alongside the principal claim and must be stated separately as an ancillary claim in every payment order or court action.

## The five key figures

- **5 percentage points** above the base rate (§ 288 para. 1 BGB)
- **Semi-annual** adjustment of the base rate (on 1 January and 1 July each year, Deutsche Bundesbank)
- **From day one** of default — not from a reminder
- **Independent ancillary claim** — can be claimed separately
- **No additional court costs** for asserting the claim in a payment order or court action

---

## How the amount is calculated

The default interest rate consists of two components:

```
Default interest rate = Base rate + 5 percentage points
```

### Base rate over time

The base rate is set twice yearly (on 1 January and 1 July) by the Deutsche Bundesbank and published at [bundesbank.de](https://www.bundesbank.de). Historical examples:

| Period | Base rate | Default interest rate |
|---|---|---|
| 01.07.2022 – 31.12.2022 | −0.88 % | 4.12 % |
| 01.01.2023 – 30.06.2023 | 1.62 % | 6.62 % |
| 01.07.2023 – 31.12.2023 | 3.12 % | 8.12 % |
| 01.01.2024 – 30.06.2024 | 3.62 % | 8.62 % |
| 01.07.2024 – 31.12.2024 | 3.37 % | 8.37 % |

What matters for your case: the **currently applicable** base rate on the day for which interest is being calculated — the rate may change during an ongoing default period.

### Worked example

Claim: **€8,000** repair costs + €1,200 Wertminderung (diminished value) + €400 expert fees = **€9,600 total claim**.
Default since: 1 July 2024 (base rate 3.37 %, default interest rate 8.37 % p.a.).
Default period: 90 days until actual payment.

```
Interest = €9,600 × 8.37 % × (90 / 365)
         = €9,600 × 0.0837 × 0.2466
         ≈ €198
```

→ Additional claim of **€198** on top of the principal. In lengthy proceedings (1+ year) interest quickly adds up to **several hundred to several thousand euros**.

---

## Which amounts attract interest

What counts is the **legitimate claim** — i.e. the amount ultimately awarded to you. Key points:

- **All legitimate damage items** attract interest: repair costs, Wertminderung (diminished value), Nutzungsausfall (loss of use), rental car costs, pain and suffering, loss of earnings, expert fees, medical treatment costs.
- **No interest on legal fees as such** — legal fees constitute an independent default loss (see [legal fee reimbursement](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung)).
- **On partial payment**, interest continues to run **only on the outstanding balance**.

---

## When the interest period begins

Default interest runs from **day one of default** — not from a reminder or the filing of a claim. Three typical scenarios in road accident cases:

| Scenario | Start of default |
|---|---|
| Claim letter with 4-week deadline, deadline expired | Day after deadline expires |
| No deadline set, but 4 weeks have passed since complete claim notification | Day after the 4-week BGH threshold expires |
| Insurer has expressly and finally refused | Day of refusal (§ 286 para. 2 no. 3 BGB) |

In disputed scenarios the court decides — in practice, however, it usually follows the date of complete claim notification plus 4 weeks closely.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
**Always include default interest in any legal action.** It is a cost-free additional claim — the amount in dispute (for court costs) is based on the principal claim; the interest comes on top.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Document the start of default clearly**
Claim letter sent by registered post, reminder sent by registered post. Both dates in your file.

**2. Keep the interest amount updated**
Recalculate daily. Online calculators such as [Verzugszinsrechner](https://www.basiszinssatz.de) help. Enter the figure as of the date of the court action.

**3. State as an ancillary claim in the payment order or court action**
Standard wording: *"plus interest at 5 percentage points above the base rate from [date]"*.

### 📝 Wording in the payment order (Mahnbescheid)

```
Principal claim:         €9,600.00
Ancillary claim:         Interest at 5 percentage points
                         above the base rate
                         from 1 July 2024
```

---

## What the insurer typically claims

> *"Interest will be taken into account on settlement — no dispute necessary."*

In practice, interest is frequently "forgotten" if you do not explicitly demand it. Always state the claim explicitly in writing.

> *"Interest only runs from a formal reminder."*

Incorrect under the BGH line on reasonable review periods. Default arises automatically after the review period expires — interest runs from that day, not from a reminder.

> *"We will offset against an alleged contributory fault."*

Attempts to reduce interest by applying a blanket [contributory fault percentage](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent) are impermissible as long as that percentage has not been finally determined by a court.

## Frequently asked questions

**How high is default interest currently?**
The base rate is currently between 3 and 4 % — giving a default interest rate of **8 to 9 % p.a.** Current figures at [bundesbank.de](https://www.bundesbank.de/de/statistiken/geld-und-kapitalmaerkte/zinssaetze-und-renditen/basiszinssatz-nach-247-bgb-616266).

**Can I claim compound interest?**
Only to a limited extent. No compound interest accrues on default interest itself (§ 289 BGB). However: from the point at which proceedings become pending (Rechtshängigkeit, i.e. the filing of the claim), a separate entitlement to **litigation interest** (Prozesszinsen) arises alongside the default interest — which is effectively a doubling.

**Is default interest taxable?**
Default interest is generally treated as **investment income** and is subject to income tax if the allowance is exceeded. Pain and suffering awards remain tax-free — the interest portion attributable to them is typically tax-neutral under BFH case law as well, but this must be assessed individually.

**Can the insurer simply reduce the interest if it pays as "goodwill"?**
No. Interest is provided for by statute and is non-negotiable where default is established. A blanket reduction without legal basis must be rejected.

**Is it worth the effort for €100–200 in interest?**
Yes. First, interest accumulates quickly to larger amounts in lengthy proceedings. Second, the lawyer and the enforcement cost you nothing (the insurer pays legal fees). Third, the interest increases pressure for swift settlement — it works **preventively** against delay.
