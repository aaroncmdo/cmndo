# Heirs and Legal Succession Following a Fatal Traffic Accident

> **In brief:** When an injured party dies as a result of a traffic accident, any compensation claims that had already arisen pass to the heirs **as part of the estate** (§ 1922 BGB – universal succession). Since a landmark BGH ruling in 1985, claims for pain and suffering (Schmerzensgeld) are **heritable** – even a claim that arose in connection with the dying process is now accessible to the heirs. In addition, surviving dependants have **their own** independent claims ([Hinterbliebenengeld](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld), [Unterhaltsschaden](/haftpflicht/unterhaltsschaden), [Beerdigungskosten](/haftpflicht/beerdigungskosten)).

## Also known as

§ 1922 BGB · Gesamtrechtsnachfolge (universal succession) · succession · transfer of claims to heirs

---

## Explained in one sentence

In a fatal accident, any compensation claims the deceased had accrued up to the time of death pass to the heirs as part of the estate – Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering damages) is heritable.

## Explained in three sentences

When a traffic accident victim dies, the compensation claims that had arisen up to the moment of death do **not lapse** – instead, they pass to the heirs under § 1922 BGB by way of universal succession (Gesamtrechtsnachfolge), who can then pursue them against the at-fault party's liability insurer. Since the BGH's 1985 ruling, **Schmerzensgeld is heritable** – even if the injured party dies after the accident but before the claim has been formally asserted, the entitlement passes to the heirs. In addition, surviving dependants have **independent** claims of their own (Hinterbliebenengeld, Unterhaltsschaden, Schockschaden, Beerdigungskosten) – heirs' claims and dependants' claims are **legally separate** and can run in parallel.

## Five key figures

- **§ 1922 BGB** – Gesamtrechtsnachfolge (universal succession)
- **1985** – BGH ruling establishing the heritability of Schmerzensgeld
- **3-year limitation period** – § 195 BGB continues to run for the heirs
- **Proof of heirship** required to assert claims
- **Dependants' claims** run in parallel – independently

---

## What passes to the heirs

### Financial losses up to the time of death
- [Repair costs](/haftpflicht/reparaturkosten) – already incurred
- [Expert report costs](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten) (Sachverständigen-Kosten)
- [Loss of earnings](/haftpflicht/verdienstausfall) (Verdienstausfall) – up to the time of death
- [Medical treatment costs](/haftpflicht/heilbehandlungskosten) (Heilbehandlungskosten) – already incurred

### Personal injury – Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering damages)
- **Suffering endured up to death** – heritable as an independent claim
- **Even in cases of immediate death** from the accident (without delay) – the BGH recognises a "moment-of-death claim"
- Amount is low under BGH case law where death was immediate (typically only a few thousand euros) – higher where the deceased suffered before death

### Claims that do NOT pass to heirs
- **Claims arising from the heirs' own legal relationships** (e.g. the heirs' own medical treatment)
- **Strictly personal rights** that extinguish upon death

## Heirs' claims vs. dependants' claims

| Claim | Who? | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Schmerzensgeld up to death | Heirs | § 1922 BGB |
| Loss of earnings up to death | Heirs | § 1922 BGB |
| Repair costs / property damage | Heirs | § 1922 BGB |
| [Hinterbliebenengeld](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld) | Surviving dependants | § 844 Abs. 3 BGB |
| [Unterhaltsschaden](/haftpflicht/unterhaltsschaden) | Dependants entitled to maintenance | § 844 Abs. 2 BGB |
| [Beerdigungskosten](/haftpflicht/beerdigungskosten) | Whoever bore the costs | § 844 Abs. 1 BGB |
| [Schockschaden](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich) | Dependants who suffered a recognised condition | § 823 BGB |

→ Heirs and surviving dependants are **often the same people**, but the claims are **legally distinct**.

## Who are the heirs?

### Intestate succession (§§ 1924–1936 BGB)
- **First order**: children and spouse / registered civil partner
- **Second order**: parents and siblings
- **Third order**: grandparents and their descendants
- Spouse separately (share of 1/4 or 1/2 depending on matrimonial property regime)

### Testamentary succession
- Where a will or inheritance contract exists – takes precedence over intestate succession
- Compulsory share (Pflichtteil) remains protected

---

## Limitation periods – an important pitfall

The [limitation period](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195) **runs continuously**:

- If the 3-year period had already begun running during the deceased's lifetime, it continues to run for the heirs
- It may be suspended if negotiations are ongoing
- Where heirs discover late-onset consequences: a fresh period may apply

→ In practice: heirs must **act promptly** to establish whether claims still exist and whether they are at risk of becoming time-barred.

---

## What to do now – in concrete terms

### 💡 The most important rule
Check **both** heirs' claims and dependants' claims – they are independent and can be pursued cumulatively.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Apply for a certificate of inheritance (Erbschein)**
Through the probate court or a notary. Required to assert claims against the insurer.

**2. Instruct a lawyer**
The combination of heirs' claims and dependants' claims is complex. Legal fees are borne by the opposing insurer.

**3. Check the limitation period**
If the succession has been open for some time: verify the deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can heirs receive compensation following a fatal accident?**
Yes. All claims that had arisen up to the time of death pass to the heirs under § 1922 BGB – including Schmerzensgeld (heritable since BGH 1985).

**Is Schmerzensgeld heritable?**
Yes. Since the BGH's 1985 ruling, Schmerzensgeld is fully heritable – even where the injured party survived only briefly after the accident.

**What is the difference between heirs and surviving dependants?**
Heirs step into the deceased's legal position and take over the estate (including compensation claims). Surviving dependants have **their own** claims (Hinterbliebenengeld, Unterhaltsschaden, Schockschaden, Beerdigungskosten). In practice, they are often the same persons.

**What is Gesamtrechtsnachfolge (universal succession)?**
The heirs step into the complete legal position of the deceased, taking over all assets – including claims arising from a traffic accident.

**Who are the heirs?**
Where a will exists: those named in it. Without a will: intestate succession applies – children and spouse take first priority.
