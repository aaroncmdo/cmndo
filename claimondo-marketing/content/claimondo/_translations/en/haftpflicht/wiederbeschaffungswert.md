# Replacement Value (Wiederbeschaffungswert) After a Road Accident – What It Is and How It Is Calculated

> **In brief:** The Wiederbeschaffungswert (WBW – replacement value) is the **market value of an equivalent vehicle** at the time of the accident – the amount you need to purchase a comparable car on the open market. In the event of an economic total loss (repair costs exceed 130 % of the WBW), the third-party liability insurer reimburses the **net replacement cost** (WBW minus salvage value). For private individuals, the **gross WBW including VAT** applies as a rule; the salvage value is determined on the basis of what is **realistically achievable locally** – not via insurer-operated online platforms.

## Also Known As

WBW · pre-accident market value · replacement price · gross WBW · net WBW

---

## Explained in One Sentence

The Wiederbeschaffungswert is the market price of an equivalent replacement vehicle at the time of the accident – the basis for reimbursement in cases of economic total loss.

## Explained in Three Sentences

In the event of an economic total loss – where repair costs exceed the Wiederbeschaffungswert by more than 30 % – the net replacement cost is reimbursed instead of repair: that is the **Wiederbeschaffungswert minus salvage value (Restwert)**. The Wiederbeschaffungswert is based on the actual market price of a comparable vehicle (same make, model, age, mileage, equipment and pre-accident condition) and is established by an expert report (Gutachten) – for private individuals, this is calculated on a gross basis, i.e. inclusive of VAT. The **Restwert (salvage value)** is determined in accordance with BGH case law on the basis of the price realistically achievable on the local market – insurer-operated online platforms with above-average offers do not oblige you to sell to those buyers.

## The Five Key Figures

- **130 %** – the threshold between repair and replacement (BGH standard)
- **Gross WBW** for private individuals – inclusive of VAT
- **14 days** – typical replacement period for rental car (Mietwagen) entitlement
- **Salvage value regionally realistic** – not determined by insurer platform
- **§ 249 Abs. 2 BGB** as the legal basis

---

## How the Wiederbeschaffungswert Is Determined

The Wiederbeschaffungswert is **not** a statically calculated figure. It refers to the **specific market price** of a comparable vehicle at the time of the accident. A qualified automotive expert (Kfz-Sachverständiger; BVSK member or equivalent) combines three sources:

### 1. Market Analysis Databases
Provide statistical reference values drawn from actual market transactions, segmented by make, model, year of manufacture and equipment. The most important standard.

### 2. Schwacke-Liste (Eurotax)
A second data source used in parallel to the market analysis – diverging values allow for a plausibility check.

### 3. Market Research
Current listings on mobile.de, autoscout24 and dealer-direct at the time of valuation. The expert compares 5–10 specific listings for comparable vehicles.

The final WBW in the Gutachten is the **averaged market value** supported by the expert's reasoning – not a single database figure.

---

## Gross vs. Net – the Private Individual Rule

Private individuals are reimbursed on a **gross** basis, i.e. inclusive of VAT. The reason: private individuals cannot claim input tax deductions when purchasing a replacement – they pay the full gross price on the market. If the insurer were to reimburse only the net amount, the claim settlement would be **incomplete** and the injured party (Geschädigter) would have to cover the difference out of their own pocket.

### When the Net WBW Applies
- For businesses entitled to input tax deduction
- Where differential taxation (Differenzbesteuerung) applies to the dealer: proportional VAT consideration
- In cases of purely notional billing without an actual replacement purchase (special case – seek legal advice)

---

## Net Replacement Cost – the Formula

In the event of an economic total loss, the insurer pays **not** the full WBW but the **net replacement cost (Wiederbeschaffungsaufwand)**:

```
Wiederbeschaffungsaufwand = WBW (gross) − Restwert (salvage value)
```

### Concrete Example

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Wiederbeschaffungswert (gross, per expert) | €15,000 |
| Restwert (regionally realistic, per expert) | €3,500 |
| **Wiederbeschaffungsaufwand (net replacement cost)** | **€11,500** |

The Restwert reduces the reimbursement because you can still sell the wreck. However: you are **not** obliged to sell it – you may keep it, continue using it or have it scrapped. The relevant Restwert is the market value, not any specific sale offer.

---

## Restwert – Where Disputes Begin

The most common dispute in economic total loss cases concerns the Restwert. Insurers use their own online disposal platforms, which typically aggregate offers from commercial buyers for your accident-damaged vehicle – and those offers are usually **significantly above** the regionally realistic salvage value.

### What Case Law Says

The BGH has made clear: the decisive figure is the **Restwert achievable on the general local market**, not a specialist buyer's offer. The Geschädigter is **not obliged** to sell the vehicle to a buyer sourced via the insurer's platform.

### Example Difference

| Source | Restwert |
|---|---|
| Insurer online platform offer | €5,500 |
| Own expert report (regional) | €3,500 |
| **Difference in favour of the injured party** | **€2,000** |

This difference arises in every second total loss case – and this is precisely where your own Gutachten pays off.

## Replacement Period and Follow-On Claims

While you search for a replacement vehicle, you are entitled to:

- **Rental car costs (Mietwagen-Kosten)** (if a rental car is actually used) – details: [Mietwagen reimbursement](/haftpflicht/mietwagen)
- OR **loss-of-use compensation (Nutzungsausfall-Entschädigung)** (Sanden-Danner table) – details: [Nutzungsausfall](/haftpflicht/nutzungsausfall)

Typical period under BGH standards: **14 days** – time for market research, test drives, purchase and registration. For specialist or collector vehicles, the period may be longer where supported by an expert or market research.

---

## What You Should Do Now – Concretely

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Commissioning your own expert (Sachverständiger) is essential. Insurer-commissioned reports tend to set the WBW lower and the Restwert higher – the difference can amount to several thousand euros.

### 🛠 Four Steps

**1. Commission your own Sachverständiger**
BVSK member, freely chosen. Expert costs are paid by the insurer (for accidents where you are not at fault, per BGH standards on free choice of expert).

**2. Compare WBW and Restwert**
Your own expert vs. the insurer's report. If the difference exceeds €1,000 (WBW) or €500 (Restwert): seek legal review.

**3. Reject platform Restwert figures in writing**
Argue on the basis of the BGH standard on regional achievability. The insurer may not compel you to sell to platform buyers.

**4. Claim Mietwagen / Nutzungsausfall for the replacement period**
14 days as standard; longer if circumstances warrant.

---

## What the Insurance Company Typically Argues

> *"Comparable vehicles are available more cheaply – the WBW is lower."*

Which specific comparison vehicles, with what equipment, mileage and condition? Counter with your own Gutachten. If disputed, file a claim – a court-appointed expert will decide.

> *"Restwert per our platform: [high figure]."*

BGH standard on regional achievability. Platform offers are not a regular market valuation – they are special offers from specialist buyers, often subject to conditions that private individuals cannot meet.

> *"We reimburse the net WBW."*

For private individuals without input tax deduction entitlement: legally untenable. BGH standard on gross WBW for private individuals.

> *"Replacement is possible within 7 days."*

14 days is the standard for market search, test drive, purchase and registration. Do not accept a blanket reduction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Wiederbeschaffungswert?**
The market value of a comparable vehicle at the time of the accident. The basis for reimbursement in cases of economic total loss (repair costs > 130 % WBW).

**How is the Wiederbeschaffungswert calculated?**
By an automotive expert (Kfz-Sachverständiger) using market analysis, the Schwacke-Liste and concrete market research (mobile.de, autoscout24). The expert takes into account make, model, age, mileage, equipment and pre-accident condition.

**Does the insurer reimburse gross or net?**
For private individuals: gross (inclusive of VAT), per BGH standards. The reason: private individuals pay the full gross price on the market, without any input tax deduction.

**Who determines the Restwert?**
The decisive figure is the Restwert realistically achievable on the local market, per BGH standards. Determined by your own Sachverständiger. Insurer online platforms with above-average offers do not oblige you to sell to those specialist buyers.

**What is the Wiederbeschaffungsaufwand?**
The amount you receive from the insurer when you do not repair: Wiederbeschaffungswert minus Restwert. Example: €15,000 WBW − €3,500 Restwert = €11,500 reimbursement.

**How long do I have to find a replacement?**
14 days as standard per BGH standards. During this period: Mietwagen or Nutzungsausfall. For specialist vehicles requiring a longer market search: correspondingly longer.
