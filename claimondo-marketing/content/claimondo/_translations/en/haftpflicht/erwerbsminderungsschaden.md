# Reduced Earning Capacity (Erwerbsminderungs-Schaden) under § 843 BGB – Annuity for Permanent Consequences

> **In brief:** If an accident permanently reduces your earning capacity, § 843 BGB (German Civil Code) entitles you to a **monetary annuity** for the loss of your earning ability — either as a recurring monthly payment or as a **capitalised lump sum**. The amount is calculated from the difference between projected lifetime income and actual post-accident income, multiplied by the estimated remaining working period. This is a highly complex actuarial calculation — total claims for seriously injured young claimants regularly run into seven figures.

## Also known as

§ 843 BGB Erwerbsminderung · Schadensrente · Erwerbsschaden-Rente · Berufsunfähigkeits-Schaden Haftpflicht · Verdienstausfall-Rente

---

## Explained in one sentence

Erwerbsminderungs-Schaden is the compensation — paid as an annuity or lump sum under § 843 BGB — for the accident-related loss of your earning capacity, covering you for life or until retirement age.

## Explained in three sentences

If a road accident permanently (or for several years) prevents you from working at your previous level, the standard [Verdienstausfall (loss of earnings)](/haftpflicht/verdienstausfall) transitions into an **Erwerbsminderungs-Schaden** under § 843 BGB — paid as a continuing monetary annuity until retirement age or for life, or settled as a capitalised lump sum. The calculation is based on the **difference** between projected lifetime income without the accident and actual post-accident income, **multiplied by the degree of reduced earning capacity (MdE-%)** and the estimated remaining working life. For young claimants with severe disabilities and long remaining working periods, **total claims in the seven-figure range are not unusual** — a specialist lawyer with actuarial support is essential.

## The five key figures

- **§ 843 BGB** — the legal basis
- **MdE-%** (Minderung der Erwerbsfähigkeit — degree of reduced earning capacity) as the assessment factor
- **Retirement age or lifetime** as the payment period
- **Capitalisation possible** (lump sum instead of annuity)
- **Seven figures** — typical total amounts for seriously injured young claimants

---

## Who is entitled

- Injured parties with **permanent reduced earning capacity** resulting from accident injuries
- Also applies to **partial reduction** (e.g. only able to manage 50 % workload)
- In cases of complete occupational disability
- Where a career change results in loss of income

---

## Calculation framework

### Difference method

```
Erwerbsminderungs-Rente = (Lifetime income without accident
                          − Actual post-accident income)
                        × MdE-%
                        × Remaining working life
                        / Pension period
```

### Capitalisation

For a lump-sum settlement: actuarial calculation incorporating:
- Life expectancy (mortality tables)
- Discount factor (interest rate trends)
- Inflation assumptions
- Occupation-specific working life

→ Must be prepared with legal and expert input.

---

## Example calculation

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Lifetime income without accident (30-year-old university graduate) | €65,000/year gross |
| Actual post-accident income (part-time, changed occupation) | €35,000/year |
| Difference | €30,000/year |
| MdE | 50 % |
| Attributable amount | €15,000/year |
| Remaining working life until retirement age | 35 years |
| **Projection** | **€15,000 × 35 = €525,000** |
| Capitalisation with actuarial adjustment | approx. €380,000–€450,000 |

→ Simplified example. An actual calculation accounts for career progression, inflation assumptions, and deductions for occupational disability insurance (BU-Rente).

## MdE assessment

The degree of reduced earning capacity is established in a **medical expert report** (Versorgungsmedizin-Recht or civil-law assessment):

| MdE | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 20 % | Minor impairment |
| 30 % | Moderate impairment; career change may be required |
| 50 % | Significant impairment |
| 70 % | Severe disability |
| 100 % | Complete inability to work |

Unlike MdE assessments for a Schwerbehindertenausweis (severe disability card), the **civil-law** assessment is the decisive benchmark here — and the two can differ.

---

## Annuity vs. capitalisation — strategic considerations

| Form | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuing annuity** | Insurer security, paid for life | Insurer insolvency risk, ongoing administration |
| **Capitalisation** | Full control, immediate availability | Inflation risk, management burden |

In practice: where long-term consequences are difficult to predict, a continuing annuity is often preferred; for clearly defined losses, capitalisation with a reservation for late-emerging consequences is common.

## Your next steps — concrete

### 💡 The most important rule
Erwerbsminderungs-Schaden is **not a DIY matter**. A specialist road-traffic-law lawyer with actuarial support is essential.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Medical MdE assessment**
A Versorgungsmedizin or civil-law expert report to establish the degree of reduced earning capacity as a percentage.

**2. Occupational and income projection**
Projected career path without the accident, extrapolated over your working life. Tax adviser and lawyer work together.

**3. Actuarial calculation**
Expert report on capitalisation, incorporating life expectancy, interest rate trends, and inflation.

**4. Late-consequences reservation in any settlement**
If opting for capitalisation: insist on an express reservation covering future deterioration — see [Decoder: Pauschal-Abgeltung](/decoder/pauschal-abgeltung).

## What the insurance company typically argues

> *"The reduced earning capacity has not been sufficiently documented."*

A medical expert report plus occupational income projection is the standard evidence. The insurer's own assessment is not binding — a court-appointed expert has the final say.

> *"The MdE has been set too high — we calculate a lower figure."*

Counter-expert report and litigation. Where the facts are clear, the prospects are good.

> *"You could still work."*

Demand specifics: which occupation exactly? Which employer? What workload capacity?

## Frequently asked questions

**How high is the Erwerbsminderungsrente (reduced earning capacity annuity) after a road accident?**
It is calculated individually based on projected lifetime income, MdE-%, and remaining working life until retirement age. For seriously injured young claimants, total claims typically reach six to seven figures.

**What if I retain partial working capacity?**
A proportional annuity based on the MdE-%. At 50 % MdE, 50 % of the income difference loss is paid as an ongoing annuity.

**Who calculates the capitalisation?**
An actuary or specialist expert. In settlement negotiations, an expert estimate in out-of-court proceedings is also common.

**What if my condition deteriorates further later?**
With a continuing annuity: adjustment is possible. With capitalisation: only if an express late-consequences reservation was included in the settlement.

**Is an annuity or lump sum better?**
A strategic decision — where insurer insolvency is a concern: lump sum. Where life expectancy is uncertain: annuity.
