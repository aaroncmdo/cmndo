# Right of Way – Right Before Left: Liability and Typical Fault Apportionment

> **In brief:** At uncontrolled intersections, **§ 8 StVO** (German Road Traffic Regulations) establishes the "right before left" rule – the vehicle approaching from the right has the right of way. Whoever fails to yield bears primary fault (prima facie evidence applies). Typical apportionment: **70:30 to 100:0** against the party violating the right of way, depending on visibility, speed, and the specific circumstances. A residual operating-risk share (20–30 %) remains with the right-of-way holder only in cases of significantly excessive speed or demonstrable personal fault.

## Also Known As

§ 8 StVO · Right-before-left rule · Intersection right of way · Failure to yield

---

## In One Sentence

At uncontrolled intersections, the vehicle approaching from the right has the right of way – whoever disregards this bears primary fault for the accident.

## In Three Sentences

§ 8 StVO establishes that at intersections without yield signs, traffic lights, or police direction, the basic rule applies: **"right before left"** – the vehicle coming from the right has priority. Whoever fails to yield bears **primary fault** – prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) regularly operates **against the party violating the right of way**. Typical apportionment ranges between **70:30 and 100:0** against the at-fault party depending on the circumstances; a residual operating-risk share for the right-of-way holder only applies in cases of significantly excessive speed, inadequate observation of the intersection, or demonstrable traffic violations of their own.

## The Five Key Figures

- **§ 8 StVO** — right-before-left rule
- **70:30 to 100:0** — typical apportionment against the party violating the right of way
- **Anscheinsbeweis** (prima facie evidence) — operates against the vehicle approaching from the left
- **20–30 %** — operating-risk share where the right-of-way holder was travelling at excessive speed
- **"Half right-of-way" rule** — applicable in specific constellations

---

## When § 8 StVO Applies

The right-before-left rule applies at **all intersections and junctions without a special regulation**:
- No yield sign
- No traffic lights
- No police officer directing traffic
- No special residential-zone rule
- At car parks with road character

→ In **traffic-calmed zones** and in pure car park areas (without road character), the right-before-left rule does **not** apply automatically – mutual consideration and coordination are required there.

---

## Apportionment by Typical Constellation

| Constellation | At-fault party (from left) : Injured party (from right) |
|---|---|
| Standard failure to yield | **100:0** |
| Failure to yield + obstructed visibility | 100:0 |
| Failure to yield + right-of-way holder travelling too fast | 80:20 |
| Failure to yield + right-of-way holder travelling considerably too fast | 70:30 |
| Failure to yield + right-of-way holder drives through stop sign | 50:50 |
| Both from side street – mutual failure to yield | 50:50 or case-by-case |

→ The **standard apportionment is 100:0** – contributory negligence only applies where specific concrete facts are established.

---

## Insurance Company Tactics

> *"The right-of-way holder was travelling too fast – we assume 30 % contributory negligence."*

Demand the specific speed figure. At standard speed (e.g. 50 km/h in built-up areas), no contributory share applies.

> *"Both parties breached their duty of care."*

A blanket argument. The Anscheinsbeweis operates against the party who violated the right of way – rebuttal requires concrete facts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who has the right of way under right before left?**
The vehicle approaching **from the right** at the intersection has the right of way – § 8 StVO.

**What if both vehicles are coming from a side street?**
Mutual failure to yield – apportionment 50:50, depending on the specific constellation.

**Who bears fault where visibility is obstructed (parked cars, hedges)?**
Even with obstructed visibility: the Anscheinsbeweis operates against the party who violated the right of way. Where both parties had a mutual duty of caution, a minor contributory share may be possible.

**What if the right-of-way holder was speeding?**
Where speed was significantly excessive, a contributory share of 20–30 % may apply. At normal traffic speed, the right-of-way holder is fully protected.
