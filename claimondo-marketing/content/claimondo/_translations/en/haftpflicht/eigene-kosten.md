# Out-of-Pocket Expenses After a Road Accident – Taxi, Travel, Phone & Postage Costs

> **Quick summary:** Out-of-pocket expenses are all the small, accident-related costs you personally incur – taxi fares, travel costs, phone calls, postage, registered mail, photocopies. Reimbursable under § 249 BGB (German Civil Code on damage compensation); courts regularly recognise a **flat-rate expenses allowance of €25–30**, while higher actual costs can be claimed with receipts. Start collecting proof from day one – the total adds up faster than you'd expect.

## Also known as

General out-of-pocket costs · Flat-rate personal expenses · General disbursements · Additional mobility costs

---

## In one sentence

Out-of-pocket expenses are all accident-related minor costs – taxi, travel, phone and postage – reimbursable either as a flat rate or on an itemised basis.

## In three sentences

Over the course of a claim settlement, numerous **minor costs** arise that you have to cover yourself – trips to the workshop or doctor, taxi rides when your own vehicle is unavailable, registered letters with return receipt, document copies, phone calls with the insurer, lawyer and Sachverständiger (expert appraiser). Courts commonly recognise a **flat rate of €25–30** for these costs – alternatively, actual receipts can be submitted if total expenditure exceeds the flat rate (typical in cases of extended repairs or extensive correspondence). The legal basis for reimbursement is § 249 BGB; these costs should be claimed together with all other damage items.

## The five key figures

- **€25–30** typical flat-rate expenses allowance
- **§ 249 BGB** legal basis for the claim
- **€0.30/km** typical reimbursement rate for private vehicle travel
- **Keep receipts** – if costs exceed the flat rate
- **Flat rate or itemised** – your choice

## What counts as out-of-pocket expenses

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Travel costs | Trips to doctor, therapist, workshop or Sachverständiger (€0.30/km for your own vehicle) |
| Taxi costs | When you cannot drive yourself or have no replacement vehicle |
| Postage | Registered letters with return receipt, correspondence with the insurer |
| Phone calls | Insurer, lawyer, workshop, Sachverständiger |
| Photocopies | Damage report, police report, medical certificates, Gutachten (expert report) |
| Internet / printing costs | Online research, printouts |
| Parking fees | For workshop or medical appointments |

## Flat rate vs. itemised billing

### Flat rate (standard)
- €25–30 flat
- No receipts required
- Fast processing
- Recommended for minor claims

### Itemised receipts (for high expenditure)
- All receipts, copies and stamps required
- More administrative effort
- Higher total possible (often €60–150)
- Worth it for extended claim correspondence

---

## What the insurance company typically claims

> *"Personal expenses are covered by the standard settlement."*

Incorrect. The expenses allowance is a **standalone claim item** – it must be requested either as a flat rate or on an itemised basis.

> *"No receipts, no entitlement."*

The flat-rate entitlement exists even without receipts (established court practice).

---

## What to do now – step by step

### 💡 The most important rule
Always claim the flat rate – low threshold, no effort required. Switch to itemised receipts if your actual costs are higher.

### 🛠 Two steps

**1. List €25–30 flat-rate expenses as a separate line item in your claim letter**
Label the position clearly as "Auslagen-Pauschale" (expenses allowance).

**2. If costs are higher: collect all receipts**
Archive all receipts, calculate the total and submit them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who pays for taxis after an accident?**
If you are unable to drive due to the accident: the insurer covers this as part of out-of-pocket expenses or as additional mobility costs.

**How much travel reimbursement can I claim?**
€0.30/km for trips to treatment, the workshop or Sachverständiger using your own vehicle. Alternatively, taxi receipts are accepted where there is a medical necessity.

**What is an Auslagen-Pauschale (flat-rate expenses allowance)?**
A standard reimbursement amount for general disbursements (postage, phone calls, copies) – typically €25–30, reimbursable without receipts.

**Do I receive the flat rate automatically?**
No – you must actively request it in your claim letter. Insurers only settle what is explicitly claimed.

**Is itemised billing worth it instead of the flat rate?**
For extended claim correspondence with many receipts, yes – totals often reach €60–150.
