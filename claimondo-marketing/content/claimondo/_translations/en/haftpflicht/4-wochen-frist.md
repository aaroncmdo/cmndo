# The 4-Week Claims Settlement Period After a Road Accident

> **In brief:** The 4-week claims settlement period is an established BGH line of case law on the reasonable review period for motor liability insurers. With clear liability and complete documentation, 4 weeks suffice; for complex personal injury cases, 6–8 weeks. Once the deadline expires, default (Verzug) occurs automatically — with default interest and fully recoverable legal costs charged to the insurer.

---

## What This Is About

If you have been in a road accident and the opposing insurer is required to cover your loss, the question will sooner or later arise: *How long may the insurer actually "review" the claim before paying?*

There is no explicit statutory deadline. However, there is a clear BGH line of case law on the **reasonable review period** that insurers, lawyers and courts use as their reference point. This deadline is your most powerful lever — anyone who knows it and uses it stops negotiating from a defensive position.

---

## How Long Is "Reasonable"?

| Scenario | Typical deadline |
|---|---|
| Property damage with expert report on file, clear liability | **4 weeks** |
| Property damage including Wertminderung (diminished value), clear liability | 4–6 weeks |
| Personal injury with initial medical findings, clear liability | **6 weeks** |
| Personal injury with late-onset consequences or expert report required | 6–8 weeks |
| Major loss or multiple parties involved | 8–12 weeks |

Important: these deadlines are **guideline values drawn from BGH case law**, not rigid statutory maximum periods. With clear liability and complete documentation, a longer period is only permissible where there is a demonstrable, **specifically identified** need for further investigation.

---

## When the Clock Actually Starts

The deadline does **not** start from the date of the accident. It starts only once **all documents necessary for the review** have been received by the insurer. These typically include:

- Fully completed loss notification form
- Police report or accident diagram
- Expert report / Gutachten (for property damage)
- Medical certificates (for personal injury)
- Photographic evidence
- A concrete statement of the amount claimed

If you submit documents later, the deadline restarts — but only for **that** item. This matters when the insurer plays a salami-tactics game, demanding new documents in ever-smaller instalments.

---

## What Happens Once the Deadline Expires

Once the reasonable review period has elapsed, **default (Verzug) occurs automatically** (§ 286 Abs. 2 Nr. 4 BGB, applied by analogy) — without you having to send a formal demand. This legal nuance is important: the insurer cannot claim it was "never put on notice."

From the moment default occurs, three independent heads of loss arise — all payable by the insurer:

### 1. Default Interest
**5 percentage points above the base rate** on the full justified claim, from the date default commences. Details: [Default interest under § 288 BGB](/haftpflicht/verzugszinsen-bgb288).

### 2. Legal Costs
**Fully recoverable as an independent head of loss caused by default** — including where you instruct a lawyer only after default has commenced (BGH, judgment of 18 July 2017, VI ZR 235/13). You therefore take on no cost risk by escalating now. Details: [Recovery of legal costs](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung).

### 3. Reminder and Litigation Costs
All further escalation costs — formal demand, court proceedings, court fees — are likewise losses caused by default and are borne by the insurer, provided your claim is well-founded.

---

## What the Insurer Typically Argues

> *"Due to the complexity of the matter, the review requires more time."*

This blanket statement is **not** sufficient to extend the deadline. The insurer must specify exactly what it is still reviewing and precisely which documents it needs. Generic review activity without a particularised need for further investigation does not constitute a valid review under BGH case law.

> *"We are still awaiting a statement from our policyholder."*

That is an **internal matter** for the insurer and is no grounds for a delay in dealing with you. The insurer can settle without a statement from its policyholder — it merely needs to act vis-à-vis its policyholder in time; it cannot use that process to delay payment to **you**.

> *"A longer period applies in personal injury cases."*

This is correct in principle — but only to the extent that specific medical clarification is genuinely still outstanding. A blanket invocation of "personal injury" is insufficient. Once an initial diagnosis and a prognostic assessment are available, 6 weeks is the standard.

---

## What You Should Do Now

### 💡 The most important rule
The 4-week deadline only works if the insurer knows that **you** know it. Use it actively and in writing — otherwise the insurer will treat it as a myth to support its delay strategy.

### 🛠 Three steps to a clean deadline notice

**1. Send a complete notice of claim**
By recorded delivery with return receipt. Include all documents, a specific total sum claimed and a 4-week deadline from the date of receipt.

**2. Document the date of receipt**
Keep the return receipt — it is your proof of when the deadline began.

**3. Trigger default**
Once the deadline has passed: written demand with a 14-day deadline, then instruct a lawyer. The legal costs are paid by the insurer.

### 📝 Template letter for setting a deadline

```
Dear Sir or Madam,

With reference to the loss event of [accident date] under
claim reference [number], I hereby assert the following claims:

[Itemised list: repair costs / Wertminderung / Nutzungsausfall / …]

Total claimed: [amount] €

Documents enclosed: [list]

I request full settlement within 4 weeks of receipt of this letter.

Upon expiry of this period you will be in default pursuant to § 286 BGB.
Default interest (§ 288 BGB) and full legal costs
(BGH VI ZR 235/13) will be claimed.

Yours faithfully,

[Signature]
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the 4-week deadline also apply to my own comprehensive insurance (Kaskoversicherung)?**
No. The deadlines described here relate to the **opposing motor liability insurer** in a no-fault accident. Your own comprehensive cover is subject to the [contractual notification periods](/haftpflicht/kasko-versicherung), not the BGH line on review periods.

**What if the insurer requests more time?**
Ask for a specific justification. If there is a demonstrable need for further investigation, grant a maximum of 2 additional weeks in writing — and only **once**. If the insurer requests another extension: instruct a lawyer.

**Does the deadline restart when I submit additional documents?**
Only for the items newly submitted. If, for example, you claimed repair costs and Wertminderung and later provide a pain-and-suffering medical certificate, the original deadline for repair costs and Wertminderung continues to run — only for the pain-and-suffering element does a new deadline begin.

**What if I set too short a deadline?**
An excessively short deadline (e.g. 7 days instead of 4 weeks) is not automatically invalid — it is generally reinterpreted as a "reasonable deadline," i.e. treated as a 4-week period. That said: write 4 weeks to avoid any legal debate.

**What about accidents abroad?**
Accidents in other countries are governed by different mechanisms under the Green Card system. See the [entry on accidents abroad](/haftpflicht/auslandsunfall) for details.
