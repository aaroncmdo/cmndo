# Funeral Costs under § 844 Abs. 1 BGB

> **In brief:** Funeral costs are fully reimbursable under § 844 Abs. 1 BGB by the opposing party's liability insurer in the event of a fatal road accident — covering burial, funeral service, grave plot, headstone and expenses of a **fitting standard**. Who can claim: the person who **actually bore** the funeral costs (typically heirs or a surviving spouse). Typical ranges: €5,000–€15,000 (standard burial) up to €25,000+ (elaborate headstones, larger funeral services).

## Also known as

§ 844 Abs. 1 BGB · Bestattungskosten · funeral service reimbursement · grave cost reimbursement

---

## Explained in one sentence

Funeral costs (Beerdigungskosten) are the reimbursement of all expenses of a fitting standard for burial and funeral services following a fatal road accident, paid by the opposing party's liability insurer.

## Explained in three sentences

§ 844 Abs. 1 BGB obliges the opposing party's liability insurer to reimburse **funeral costs** (Beerdigungskosten) in the event of a fatal road accident — covering all **expenses of a fitting standard** for the burial, funeral service, grave plot, headstone and, where applicable, transportation of the deceased. The person entitled to claim is the one who **actually bore** the costs — typically the heirs or the surviving spouse. Typical ranges run from **€5,000–€15,000** (standard burial) to over **€25,000** in particular circumstances (elaborate headstone design, large funeral service, family vault) — the benchmark is the **socially customary standard** for the deceased's position, financial situation and family circumstances.

## The five key figures

- **§ 844 Abs. 1 BGB** — legal basis for the claim
- **€5,000–€15,000** — standard burial costs
- **€25,000+** — in particular circumstances
- **Fitting standard** — the guiding principle for assessment
- **Actually borne** — prerequisite for entitlement

---

## Which costs are reimbursable

### Direct burial costs
- Coffin / urn
- Burial (earth, cremation or sea burial)
- Funeral service
- Burial rite (religious or secular)
- Transportation costs
- Funeral stationery (cards, announcements)

### Grave and grave maintenance
- **Grave plot** — purchase, extension
- **Headstone** — purchase, inscription, installation
- **Planting** and grave flowers (initial arrangement)
- **Ongoing grave maintenance** for an **appropriate period** (typically the 25-year rest period) — partially disputed

### Other expenses
- Mourning attire in **appropriate scope**
- Catering for funeral guests (funeral reception / burial gathering)
- Death certificate, official fees

---

## "Fitting standard" — the boundary question

The BGH's line of case law refers to reimbursement of a "fitting standard" (standesgemäß). The benchmark is:

- **Financial and professional circumstances** of the deceased
- **Family standard** and regional custom
- **Religion / culture** (e.g. Orthodox vs. Protestant tradition)
- **Social position** of the deceased

→ For standard burials, €5,000–€15,000 is generally accepted. For elaborate burials, the fitting-standard character must be substantiated.

---

## Who holds the claim

The person entitled to claim is the one who **actually bore** the funeral costs:

- **Heirs** (typically under statutory inheritance law or a will)
- **Surviving spouse** (often the one bearing costs, even without heir status)
- **Multiple persons on a pro-rata basis** where costs were shared

→ Whoever paid the invoice holds the claim.

---

## What the insurer typically argues

> *"Funeral costs not fully reimbursable — we will only reimburse a flat-rate standard amount of €5,000."*

Do not accept blanket arguments. Present specific invoices — the insurer must justify why costs are "not of a fitting standard."

> *"25 years of grave maintenance is excessive."*

Apply the BGH's case law on the appropriate rest period. Under local cemetery regulations, 25 years frequently represents the standard.

> *"Funeral reception catering not necessary."*

Reimbursable within an appropriate scope. Check the fitting-standard criterion.

---

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Archive all receipts. Rebut blanket reductions with specific invoices.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Gather all receipts**
Funeral home, headstone, cemetery fees, funeral service, catering, stationery, transportation costs.

**2. Prepare the fitting-standard argument**
Financial circumstances of the deceased, family standard, tradition.

**3. Consult a lawyer for reductions exceeding €1,000**
Escalation is worthwhile in the case of blanket reductions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who pays for the funeral after a road accident?**
In the event of a fatal accident that was not your fault, the opposing party's liability insurer pays — as a standalone damage item under § 844 Abs. 1 BGB. Prerequisite: you bore the costs.

**How high are reimbursable funeral costs?**
Standard burial: €5,000–€15,000. In particular circumstances (elaborate headstone design, large funeral service) up to €25,000+.

**Are headstone costs fully reimbursable?**
Yes, within the fitting standard. Insurer reductions citing "excessive" costs are often unsustainable.

**Can I also recover catering costs for the funeral service?**
Within an appropriate scope — based on family size and tradition. Contest blanket refusals.

**Who can assert the claim?**
The person who **actually bore** the funeral costs. Typically the heirs or the surviving spouse.
