# Limitation of Traffic Accident Claims under § 195 BGB (German Civil Code)

> **In brief:** Compensation claims arising from a traffic accident are generally subject to a **3-year** limitation period under § 195 BGB (German Civil Code on limitation of claims) — starting at the **end of the year** in which the claim arose and you had knowledge of the damage and the at-fault party (§ 199 para. 1 BGB). For personal injury claims with late-onset consequences, a **30-year maximum period** applies; for concealed property damage, 10 years. If the insurance company deliberately delays, the clock runs against you — but negotiations, acknowledgement of liability, and litigation can suspend or restart the limitation period.

## Also known as

Anspruchsverjährung · Regelverjährung · 3-Jahres-Frist · limitation period for compensation claims

---

## Explained in one sentence

Limitation under § 195 BGB means: without actively asserting your claim, you lose the right to enforce it after 3 years.

## Explained in three sentences

The standard **3-year** limitation period under § 199 para. 1 BGB runs from the **end of the year** in which you became aware of both the damage and the at-fault party — not from the date of the accident. For personal injury with unforeseeable late-onset consequences, § 199 para. 2 BGB provides protection through a **30-year maximum period**; for concealed property damage, § 199 para. 3 BGB applies a **10-year** ceiling. Limitation can be suspended through negotiations and restarted entirely by an [acknowledgement of liability by the insurer (Anerkenntnis)](/haftpflicht/anerkenntnis-bgb212) — it is not a passive countdown, but a risk that can be actively managed.

## The five key figures

- **3 years** standard limitation period (§ 195 BGB)
- **31 December** of the year of knowledge as the start of the period — not the accident date
- **30 years** maximum period for personal injury claims (§ 199 para. 2 BGB)
- **10 years** maximum period for other damage (§ 199 para. 3 BGB)
- **6 months** recommended safety margin before the limitation deadline for initiating court proceedings

---

## How the 3-year period is calculated in practice

Three steps:

1. **Determine the year in which the claim arose and you gained knowledge.**
2. **End of that year** = start of the limitation period (always 31 December).
3. **+ 3 years** = end of the limitation period (always 31 December).

### Concrete examples

| Accident | Knowledge of damage / at-fault party | Period start | Limitation end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15.03.2026 | immediately on the day of the accident | 31.12.2026 | **31.12.2029** |
| 15.12.2026 | immediately | 31.12.2026 | **31.12.2029** |
| 15.03.2026 | not until June 2027 (late-onset consequence) | 31.12.2027 | **31.12.2030** |
| 15.03.2026 | not until March 2030 (chronic pain syndrome) | 31.12.2030 | **31.12.2033** |

→ Note the most important trap: the period does **not** begin with the accident but with the **end of the year** of the accident or the year of knowledge. An accident on 15 December and one on 15 March produce the same limitation end date (31 December).

---

## The two maximum periods for late-onset damage

While the standard 3-year period depends on **knowledge**, two absolute maximum periods run independently of it:

### 30-year maximum period (§ 199 para. 2 BGB) — personal injury
Where life, body, health or liberty is harmed, a claim is enforceable for a maximum of **30 years** from the damaging event. Most relevant scenario: severe traumatic brain injuries with late-onset consequences (dementia, chronic pain syndromes) that may not manifest until decades later.

### 10-year maximum period (§ 199 para. 3 BGB) — other damage
For property and financial damage, 10 years from the time the claim arose. Important: for concealed property damage identified late (body panel consequential damage, structural damage), the period ends no later than 10 years after the accident.

---

## What happens when a claim is time-barred

Under § 214 BGB, limitation operates as a **defence available to the debtor** — the claim formally remains in existence, but the insurer may permanently refuse to perform. In practice this means:

- The insurer raises the limitation defence in the proceedings
- The court dismisses the claim
- Total loss of the claim — even if it was substantively valid

Limitation is **not** examined by the court of its own motion — the insurer must actively raise it. In practice, however, it **always** does so when the opportunity arises.

---

## How to suspend or restart the limitation period

### Suspension through negotiations (§ 203 BGB)
For as long as you are in negotiations with the insurer, the limitation period is **suspended** — time stops running. After negotiations break down, you still have at least 3 further months to bring a claim. Practically: active correspondence with the insurer protects against limitation — silence does not.

### Suspension for minors (§ 207 BGB)
Where the injured party (Geschädigter) is a minor, the limitation period is suspended until they reach the age of 21. This is particularly relevant for children injured as passengers.

### Restart through acknowledgement of liability (§ 212 BGB)
If the insurer acknowledges the claim — even only in part — the limitation period **starts entirely afresh**: 3 years from the acknowledgement (Anerkenntnis). Detail: [acknowledgement under § 212 BGB](/haftpflicht/anerkenntnis-bgb212).

### Interruption through litigation or payment order
Filing a claim or obtaining a Mahnbescheid (payment order) interrupts the limitation period permanently. Following a final and binding judgment, a 30-year period applies under § 197 BGB.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Limitation runs whether or not you think about it. Insurers sometimes wait deliberately — anyone who lets the limitation period expire without acting loses their claim entirely.

### 🛠 Four steps to limitation security

**1. Establish the accident year and note the limitation end date**
Mark in your calendar: 31 December of the year + 3. Example: accident in 2026 → limitation expires 31.12.2029.

**2. Engage a lawyer 12 months before the deadline**
If full settlement has not been reached after 24 months, have a lawyer assess whether active negotiations (suspension) or court proceedings are required.

**3. Plan a payment order / litigation 6 months before limitation**
An online Mahnbescheid (payment order) typically costs €36–80 (depending on the amount in dispute) and interrupts the limitation period permanently.

**4. For late-onset consequences, engage a lawyer to secure a late-consequence reservation**
If you receive a settlement offer, a Spätfolge-Vorbehalt (reservation for future unknown consequences) can be included in the settlement — preserving the right to claim for any later unforeseen consequences.

## What the insurance company typically claims

> *"Your claim is time-barred."*

Check the dates carefully. Factor in any suspension periods (negotiations, minority) and the effect of any acknowledgement of liability. If in dispute: bring proceedings — the court decides.

> *"You should have acted sooner."*

The limitation period is 3 years and is guaranteed by statute. There is no additional obligation to act "in good time" within the period.

> *"We have an internal deadline."*

Internal insurer deadlines are not legally binding. The statutory limitation period under § 195/199 BGB is the applicable standard.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do my claims from a traffic accident become time-barred?**
As a rule, 3 years from the end of the year in which you had knowledge of the damage and the at-fault party (§ 195, § 199 para. 1 BGB). For personal injury with late-onset consequences, up to 30 years; for concealed property damage, up to 10 years.

**Does the period begin on the day of the accident?**
No. It begins on **31 December** of the year in which the claim arose and you were aware of it. An accident on 15 March and one on 15 December produce the same limitation end date.

**What if I only notice late-onset consequences years later?**
For personal injury, you are protected by the 30-year maximum period (§ 199 para. 2 BGB). The standard 3-year period does not begin until you have concrete knowledge of the late-onset consequence — reservation clauses in settlements are advisable.

**Does reporting the damage suspend the limitation period?**
The mere report does not. But **active negotiations** with the insurer suspend the limitation period under § 203 BGB — for as long as a "dialogue" is ongoing, time stops running.

**What does a payment order (Mahnbescheid) achieve shortly before limitation?**
A Mahnbescheid interrupts the limitation period permanently. In practice, it is the fastest and most cost-effective way to preserve the claim shortly before the deadline — available through the online payment order systems of the German federal states.

**How long do I have after negotiations break down?**
At least 3 months (§ 203 sentence 2 BGB), often longer depending on the duration of the suspension. Use this time to file a claim or obtain a Mahnbescheid without delay.
