# Contributory Negligence under § 254 BGB – What It Is and How Much It Actually Reduces Your Claim

> **In brief:** § 254 BGB (German Civil Code) provides that compensation is reduced when the injured party bears **partial responsibility** for the occurrence or aggravation of the damage. Classic cases: no seatbelt worn, excessive speed, inappropriate driving behaviour. **The burden of proving contributory negligence always lies with the insurer** – blanket percentages without concrete factual justification have no legal standing. Typical quotas range from 0 to 100 %, with 20 to 50 % being common.

## Also known as

Contributory responsibility · Co-causation · Contributory fault · Claimant's own fault · Attributable self-negligence

---

## Explained in one sentence

Contributory negligence under § 254 BGB is the proportion of self-caused contribution to the damage that proportionally reduces the at-fault party's obligation to compensate – and the burden of proof always lies with the insurer.

## Explained in three sentences

§ 254 BGB establishes that both the duty to compensate and the extent of compensation depend on the **shares of causation** contributed by each party – the damage is apportioned by quota. Classic contributory negligence scenarios in road accidents include failure to wear a seatbelt, excessive speed, or inappropriate driving behaviour; the quota is determined case by case and ranges from 0 % to 100 %. **The burden of proof for all facts and causation lies entirely with the insurer** – blanket assertions without concrete factual basis are invalid and must be rejected in line with BGH case law.

## The five key figures

- **§ 254 para. 1 BGB** – contribution to the occurrence of damage
- **§ 254 para. 2 BGB** – breach of the duty to mitigate loss
- **20–30 %** typical seatbelt quota when injuries result from not wearing one
- **0 %** contributory negligence in prima facie evidence scenarios (rear-end collision, right of way)
- **100 %** burden of proof on the insurer

---

## What § 254 BGB means in law

> *"If, in the event of damage, the fault of the injured party has contributed to the occurrence of the damage, the obligation to compensate and the extent of the compensation to be made depend on the circumstances, in particular on how far the damage has been caused mainly by the one or the other party."*
> – § 254 para. 1 BGB

This provision applies in two variants:

### Variant 1 – Contribution to the occurrence of damage (para. 1)
Example: You are driving above the speed limit on an urban road when another driver fails to see you while turning left. Primary fault lies with the turning driver – but if evidence exists that a compliant speed would have prevented the accident, a contributory negligence quota (e.g. 20 %) may be recognised.

### Variant 2 – Breach of the duty to mitigate loss (para. 2)
Example: After the accident, you forego a medically indicated treatment, causing the damage to worsen. The insurer is then not liable for that aggravation.

In both variants the legal consequence is the same: **proportional reduction** of the compensation in accordance with the established quota.

## Situations that typically give rise to contributory negligence

| Behaviour | Typical quota when injuries result | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Not wearing a seatbelt | **20–30 %** | Breach of § 21a StVO (Road Traffic Regulations); the additional injuries would have been avoidable with the belt |
| No helmet (motorcycle/e-bike) | **20–40 %** | where comparable protective effect to a seatbelt can be demonstrated |
| Inappropriate speed | 0–30 % | depends on specificity of speed-related facts and causal proof |
| Inattentive driving | 0–50 % | varies by constellation, often disputed |
| Children under 10 years | **0 %** | § 828 para. 2 BGB – no tortious capacity in road traffic |
| Prima facie evidence scenario (e.g. rear-end collision) | **0 %** in favour of the vehicle struck from behind | until the prima facie evidence is specifically rebutted |
| Right-of-way violation | **80–100 %** at-fault party quota | prima facie evidence against the right-of-way violator |

**Important:** These figures are **benchmark values** from case law and the ADAC/DAV quota catalogue – not binding rules. Each case is decided on the specific evidence.

---

## Who must prove what – and how

### Burden of proof for contributory negligence: the insurer
The insurer invoking § 254 BGB must prove **all facts** that establish contributory negligence. This includes:

1. **Traffic violation** stated concretely (e.g. "you were driving 65 km/h in a 50 km/h zone")
2. **Factual proof** of the violation (police report, witnesses, expert findings)
3. **Causation** between the violation and the specific damage (would a compliant speed have prevented the accident or the injury?)

If even **one** of these three elements is missing, the contributory negligence quota is not substantiated – and our [Decoder strategy](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent) targets exactly this point.

### Burden of proof for your own blamelessness: none
You are **not** required to prove your own blamelessness. This is a common misleading formulation used by insurers. In clear prima facie evidence scenarios (rear-end collision, red light, right of way), the evidential burden is actually shifted in your favour (see [Prima Facie Evidence](/haftpflicht/anscheinsbeweis)).

## How contributory negligence quotas affect your claim

A quota of 30 % against you reduces **all** damage positions by 30 %:

| Item | Full claim | At 30 % quota |
|---|---|---|
| Repair costs | €8,000 | €5,600 |
| Wertminderung (diminished value) | €1,500 | €1,050 |
| Nutzungsausfall (loss of use) | €700 | €490 |
| Pain and suffering | €3,000 | €2,100 |
| Expert report costs | €600 | €420 |
| **Total** | **€13,800** | **€9,660** |

Difference: **€4,140** saved by the insurer. With millions of accident claims per year across Germany's vehicle fleet, it becomes clear why blanket quotas are **systematically** attempted.

## What to do now – concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Never accept a blanket quota without concrete facts. A 30 % quota accepted prematurely becomes **legally binding** and can cost you thousands.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Reject the blanket quota in writing**
Demand specific facts, specific causation, and specific evidence – see [Decoder Contributory Negligence 30 %](/decoder/mitverschulden-30-prozent).

**2. Secure your own evidence**
Request the police report, secure witness contact details, take photographs and sketches of the accident scene, and arrange dashcam footage analysis if applicable. Before disputing quotas, preserving evidence matters more than legal argumentation.

**3. Check the prima facie evidence logic**
In clear scenarios (rear-end collision, red light, right of way), the [prima facie evidence](/haftpflicht/anscheinsbeweis) works **in your favour** – the insurer must specifically rebut it; you need prove nothing at all.

## What the insurance company typically claims

> *"Based on your behaviour at the scene of the accident, we assess a quota of 30 %."*

What specific behaviour? What facts? What evidence? What specific causation regarding the severity of injury or damage? Reject blanket answers.

> *"You should have been wearing your seatbelt."*

Correct in principle – BUT: additional injury must be causally substantiated. In minor rear-end collisions, causal proof is often impossible to establish.

> *"We assume excessive speed."*

"We assume" is not a fact. What specific speed? What measurement? What witness?

## Frequently asked questions

**When does contributory negligence apply?**
When the injured party committed a traffic violation that **causally** contributed to the occurrence or aggravation of the damage. Both the violation and causation must be **specifically proven** by the insurer.

**How is the contributory negligence quota calculated?**
There is no arithmetic formula. Courts and insurers use quota catalogues (ADAC, DAV) and the individual case as reference – weighing the severity of violations and causal contributions.

**Am I contributorily negligent if I was not wearing a seatbelt?**
When injuries result, typically 20–30 %, **if** the insurer can prove that the specific injury would have been prevented or reduced by the belt. For property damage alone there is no quota – the belt protects people, not the vehicle.

**What about children as injured parties?**
Children under 10 years have no tortious capacity in road traffic under § 828 para. 2 BGB – contributory negligence is excluded. For children between 10 and 18, it depends on their individual capacity for insight.

**Can I challenge an established quota?**
In out-of-court proceedings: yes, by objecting and demanding concrete evidence. In litigation: yes, through applications for evidence. In a final and binding judgment: no.

**How high is the typical quota in a rear-end collision?**
In a classic rear-end collision, prima facie evidence applies: **100 %** fault of the vehicle that collided from behind, **0 %** contributory negligence of the vehicle in front. Rebuttal of the prima facie evidence is possible but rarely successful.
