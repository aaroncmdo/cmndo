# Red-Light Violation in an Accident – Who Is Liable

> **In brief:** Anyone who runs a red light and is involved in an accident bears **full liability (100 : 0)** under § 37 StVO (German Road Traffic Regulations) and the prima facie evidence rule (Anscheinsbeweis). Even a brief red-light violation ("amber-to-red") or a qualified red-light violation (red for longer than 1 second) leaves full liability with the offender. The only way to rebut this: proof that the light was still amber or green at the moment of entry — an extremely difficult burden to meet.

## Also Known As

§ 37 StVO · Rotlichtverstoß · Ampelverstoß · Lichtzeichen-Verstoß

---

## Explained in One Sentence

A red-light violation triggers the Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence rule) against the offender — the standard liability split is 100 : 0.

## Explained in Three Sentences

§ 37 StVO requires every driver to stop **before** a traffic light when it shows red — anyone who drives through and is involved in a collision bears **full liability** in virtually every case (100 : 0). The Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence rule) clearly points against the red-light offender; rebuttal is only possible if the offender can concretely prove that the light was **still amber or green** at the moment of entry, or that the signal was **defective**. Even a simple red-light violation (red for less than 1 second) or a qualified red-light violation (red for more than 1 second) results in full civil liability — the only difference lies in the administrative-law consequences (fines and penalty points).

## The Five Key Numbers

- **§ 37 StVO** — traffic signal regulation
- **100 : 0** — standard liability split against the red-light offender
- **Anscheinsbeweis** — prima facie evidence rule, clearly favouring the other party
- **1 second** — threshold for a qualified red-light violation under administrative law
- **Practically 0 %** — rebuttal success rate without hard evidence

## Liability Split by Typical Scenario

| Scenario | Red-light offender : Other party |
|---|---|
| Standard red-light violation | **100 : 0** |
| Amber-to-red (Gelb-Rot-Überfahren) | 100 : 0 |
| Qualified red-light violation (> 1 sec. red) | 100 : 0 |
| Signal failure / defective traffic light | § 8 StVO right-of-way rule applies |
| Other party also ran a red light | 50 : 50 |
| Claim "light was still amber" — without proof | 100 : 0 (no evidence) |
| Witness evidence "amber at point of entry" | case-by-case, possibly 50 : 50 |

## What to Do If the Other Party Claims You Ran a Red Light

If the other party alleges you ran a red light — and you maintain it was green — the outcome is determined by:

1. **Police report** and witness statements
2. **Signal phase plan** — reconstructable for some traffic lights
3. **Dashcam footage** (admissible under BGH VI ZR 233/17)
4. **In disputed cases**: expert reconstruction and evidence preservation

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is liable in a red-light accident?**
In virtually all cases, the red-light offender — Anscheinsbeweis. Liability split: 100 : 0.

**What about an amber-to-red transition?**
Anyone who drives through on the amber-to-red transition and is involved in a collision bears full liability. Amber requires drivers to stop.

**What if the traffic light was defective?**
In that case, § 8 StVO right-of-way rules apply — depending on the directions of travel.

**Am I entitled to pain and suffering compensation if the other party ran the red light?**
Yes — often at an increased amount due to the punitive/satisfaction function (gross negligence on the other party's part).
