# Shock Damage – When Family Members Are Traumatised by an Accident

> **In brief:** Schockschaden (shock damage) is the **independent compensation claim of close relatives** who suffer **their own psychological illness** as a result of a traffic accident involving a family member (PTSD, adjustment disorder, severe depressive episode). BGH line since the landmark ruling VI ZR 174/97 (1997): even where the relative was not involved in the accident, being informed of it or witnessing it can give rise to an independent claim — provided the illness goes beyond ordinary grief and is medically diagnosable. Compensation ranges: typically €5,000–€25,000, up to €50,000 for chronic sequelae.

## Also Known As

Schockschaden · Angehörigen-Trauma · Sekundär-Geschädigter · Schockschaden-Klausel

---

## Explained in One Sentence

Schockschaden is the independent compensation claim of close relatives who suffer their own psychological illness as a result of the injury or death of a family member.

## Explained in Three Sentences

When a traffic accident severely injures or kills a close family member, **relatives** can themselves suffer significant psychological consequences — post-traumatic stress disorder, persistent depressive episodes, or clinically significant adjustment disorders. German case law has recognised these **Schockschäden** as independent compensation claims since the BGH landmark ruling VI ZR 174/97 (1997) — requirements are a close family relationship, a medically diagnosable illness **of clinical significance** (going beyond ordinary grief), and a direct causal link to the accident. Compensation ranges: typically €5,000–€25,000 (standard shock damage), up to €50,000+ for chronic PTSD — entirely independent of the primary injured party's claims.

## The Five Key Figures

- **BGH VI ZR 174/97** (1997) — landmark ruling on Schockschaden
- **§ 823 BGB** as the legal basis for claims
- **€5,000–€25,000** typical standard compensation range
- **Up to €50,000+** for chronic PTSD
- **ICD diagnosis** required (PTSD 6B40, adjustment disorder F43.2)

---

## Requirements — What Case Law Demands

Four conditions must be met (BGH line):

### 1. Close Family Relationship
- Spouses, registered civil partners
- Parents, children, siblings
- In individual cases: fiancés, long-term partners

### 2. Medically Diagnosable Illness of Clinical Significance
- **PTSD** (ICD-11 6B40)
- **Adjustment disorder** (ICD-11 6B43 / ICD-10 F43.2)
- **Severe depressive episode** (F32.x)
- **Prolonged grief disorder** (ICD-11 6B42)

→ **Not** sufficient: ordinary grief, temporary distress, concern for a relative without clinical symptoms. The illness must exceed what would "normally" be expected in response to such news.

### 3. Causal Link to the Accident
- The illness must have been triggered by the accident or its consequences
- A pre-existing psychological condition does not automatically reduce the claim (eggshell skull doctrine)

### 4. Legal Appropriateness of the Claim
- BGH standard: "reasonable assessment" — the claim must appear legally appropriate given the circumstances

## Typical Scenarios

### Death of a Family Member
The most common and most clearly recognised Schockschaden scenario:
- Spouse dies in a traffic accident
- Parents lose a child in a traffic accident
- Child loses parents or siblings

### Severe Life-Threatening Injuries
- The severely injured person is in a coma
- Relatives face nursing and care responsibilities
- Shock caused by emergency call, identification, hospital visit

### Witnessing the Accident
- Passenger position with death or severe injury of the driver
- Parents witness their child's accident
- Witnessing a fatal accident (in rare cases also without a family relationship)

## Relationship to Hinterbliebenengeld

Since 2017 (§ 844 para. 3 BGB), there is also [Hinterbliebenengeld](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld) (bereavement compensation) — a lump-sum payment for the loss of a close relative (typically €10,000). Key distinction:

- **Hinterbliebenengeld** = lump-sum acknowledgement of emotional suffering without a medical diagnosis requirement
- **Schockschaden** = independent compensation claim **in addition**, where a medically diagnosed illness exists
- Both claims can be pursued **in parallel**

→ Schockschaden + Hinterbliebenengeld combined: realistic ranges of €15,000–€60,000+ where a close relative dies in an accident and the claimant has their own PTSD diagnosis.

## What to Do Now — Concrete Steps

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
Schockschaden requires a **medical diagnosis of clinical significance**. Ordinary grief is not enough — a specialist psychotherapeutic diagnosis is the gateway to a claim.

### 🛠 Four Steps

**1. Obtain your own psychiatric or psychotherapeutic diagnosis**
ICD diagnosis. For longer waiting times: trauma outpatient clinic.

**2. Document the start of treatment**
Treatment records serve as the evidentiary foundation.

**3. Instruct a lawyer**
Schockschaden is highly complex — a specialist lawyer is essential.

**4. Pursue both claims in parallel: Hinterbliebenengeld + Schockschaden**
Where the accident was fatal, assert both legal bases simultaneously.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Schockschaden?**
An independent compensation claim by close relatives who suffer their own clinically significant psychological illness as a result of the accident (PTSD, adjustment disorder, severe depressive episode).

**Who can claim Schockschaden?**
Close relatives — spouses, registered civil partners, parents, children, siblings. In individual cases, long-term partners as well.

**Is ordinary grief sufficient for Schockschaden?**
No. There must be a **clinically diagnosable** illness of clinical significance — going beyond the grief that would normally be expected in response to such news.

**How much Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering compensation) is awarded for Schockschaden?**
Standard: €3,000–€10,000; with a PTSD diagnosis: €10,000–€25,000; for chronic cases: up to €50,000+.

**Can it be combined with Hinterbliebenengeld?**
Yes, both in parallel. Hinterbliebenengeld as a lump sum (typically €10,000), Schockschaden individually depending on the clinical picture.

**What if I did not witness the accident myself?**
Schockschaden is also possible where the accident was communicated indirectly (by phone, police notification) — provided the illness is causally linked.
