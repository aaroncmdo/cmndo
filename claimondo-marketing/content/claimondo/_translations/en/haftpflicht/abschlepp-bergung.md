# Towing and Recovery Costs After a Traffic Accident

> **Quick summary:** Towing costs are fully recoverable under §249 BGB (German Civil Code on damage compensation) if your vehicle is **undriveable or unsafe to drive**. Recovery costs cover more complex operations — retrieving a vehicle from the roadway, specialist equipment, crane use. **Storage fees** at the workshop are recoverable while you are waiting for the expert report or repair authorisation. Typical ranges: €150–400 towing, €200–800 recovery, €15–30/day storage.

## Also known as

Towing costs · Towing reimbursement · Recovery costs · Storage fees · Impound fees

---

## In one sentence

Towing and recovery costs are the direct mobilisation costs after an accident — fully recoverable, provided they are reasonable.

## In three sentences

If your vehicle is no longer driveable or roadworthy after an accident, you are entitled to reimbursement of **towing costs** to the nearest suitable workshop — and, in more complex situations (retrieval from the roadway, use of specialist equipment, crane deployment), the higher **recovery costs**. While your vehicle is waiting for the expert report (Gutachten) or repair authorisation, **storage fees** charged by the workshop are also recoverable — typically €15–30 per day. The applicable standard is the **locally customary rate** and the proportionality of the specific measures taken — the opposing liability insurer bears full reimbursement under §249 BGB.

## The five key figures

- **§249 BGB** as the legal basis for the claim
- **€150–400** typical towing costs
- **€200–800** typical recovery costs
- **€15–30/day** storage fees at the workshop
- **Locally customary rate** as the BGH standard

---

## What is payable and when

### Towing
- Vehicle is **no longer driveable** (technical failure as a result of the accident)
- Vehicle is **no longer roadworthy** (even if it can physically move)
- Towing to the **nearest** suitable workshop — no further than necessary

### Recovery
- Accident scene is difficult to access (ditch, forest, water channel)
- Use of specialist equipment (crane, auxiliary tools)
- Multiple personnel required
- Special vehicles or adverse weather conditions

### Storage fees / impound
- While the expert report (Gutachten) is being prepared
- While awaiting repair authorisation or insurer clarification
- Workshop storage of the vehicle
- Typically €15–30/day

---

## What the insurer typically claims

> *"Towing costs are excessive — we are reducing the amount to [lower figure]."*

The locally customary rate is the applicable standard. For unusually high invoices (e.g. overnight emergency service): provide documentation and justification; the workshop-risk principle under the BGH line of case law may apply.

> *"Storage fees were not necessary — you should have made a decision sooner."*

Where an expert report or insurer clarification is outstanding, storage fees are recoverable — you cannot make a decision before the assessment exists.

---

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
Submit the towing invoice to the insurer immediately — the workshop invoice often includes storage fees within a flat-rate line item.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Keep all documentation** — towing invoice, recovery receipt, workshop intake confirmation.
**2. Notify the insurer** — ideally immediately after towing.
**3. If storage fees are disputed:** Reference the expert report (Gutachten) — storage fees are recoverable for as long as the report was not yet available.

---

## Frequently asked questions

**Who pays for towing after an accident?**
In a no-fault accident, the opposing liability insurer pays as part of the damage claim under §249 BGB.

**How much are recoverable towing costs?**
Typically €150–400 for standard towing. For more complex recovery operations, €200–800. The locally customary rate is the applicable standard.

**Am I also reimbursed for storage fees?**
Yes — for as long as the vehicle is waiting for the expert report or repair authorisation. Typically €15–30/day.

**What if I had to be towed at night?**
Emergency surcharges are generally recoverable, because the emergency situation itself is caused by the accident.

**Do I have to choose the cheapest towing service?**
No — in an acute accident situation, proportionality applies. The duty to mitigate losses only comes into play where a provider is clearly and recognisably overcharging.
