# Medical Treatment Costs After a Traffic Accident

> **In brief:** All accident-related treatment and therapy costs are fully reimbursable under § 249 BGB — from the emergency room through your GP, specialist, rehabilitation, and psychotherapy. Your statutory health insurer initially covers mandatory benefits and then recovers those costs from the at-fault party's insurer via **§ 116 SGB X subrogation**. You claim directly: **co-payments, private-pay services, travel costs, medical aids** and all amounts your health insurer does not cover.

## Also known as

Treatment costs · Medical costs · Therapy costs · Rehabilitation costs · Additional medical expenses

---

## Explained in one sentence

Medical treatment costs (Heilbehandlungskosten) are all accident-related medical expenses — from the emergency physician to rehabilitation — and must be borne in full by the at-fault party's liability insurer, minus the health insurer's subrogation claim.

## Explained in three sentences

After a traffic accident you did not cause, all accident-related **medical treatment costs** (Heilbehandlungskosten) are reimbursable under § 249 BGB — emergency care, hospital stays, outpatient treatment, medication, therapies, rehabilitation, psychotherapy, medical aids, and travel costs to appointments. Your statutory health insurer (gesetzliche Krankenkasse) initially covers the mandatory benefits and then pursues **subrogation** (Regress) against the opposing liability insurer under § 116 SGB X — no additional steps are required from you. You personally claim the amounts your health insurer did **not** cover: **co-payments** (pharmacy, hospital, therapeutic aids), **private-pay services** (e.g. private clinic, private practice, IGeL), **travel costs**, and **medical aids** that exceed the statutory health insurer's standard.

## The five key figures

- **§ 249 BGB** — legal basis for your claim
- **§ 116 SGB X** — health insurer subrogation (automatic)
- **€10/day** — maximum hospital co-payment (28 days)
- **€10** — typical pharmacy co-payment per prescription
- **€0.30/km** — typical reimbursable travel cost to appointments

## What counts as medical treatment costs

### Acute phase
- Emergency physician call-out, ambulance
- Emergency room, initial treatment
- Diagnostics (CT, MRI, X-ray, laboratory)
- Surgeries required for the first time

### Inpatient treatment
- Hospital stay
- **Co-payment €10/day (max. 28 days/year)** as your personal contribution — reimbursable by the insurer

### Outpatient treatment
- GP
- Specialists (orthopaedist, neurologist, ENT, ophthalmologist)
- Specialist diagnostics
- Private practice fees (where medically indicated or in the event of a supply shortage)

### Therapies
- Physiotherapy (often with 10 % co-payment)
- Occupational therapy
- Speech therapy
- Psychotherapy (statutory health insurer or self-pay therapy when waiting times apply)

### Rehabilitation
- Post-acute rehabilitation (Anschlussheilbehandlung, AHB)
- Rehabilitation clinic including co-payments
- Outpatient rehabilitation

### Medication and medical aids
- Prescription medication (co-payments)
- Orthoses, bandages, compression stockings
- Hearing aids (where hearing damage results from airbag noise)
- Visual aids, where accident-related

### Other costs
- Travel to appointments (€0.30/km or taxi where self-driving is not possible)
- Accommodation costs for treatment away from home
- Additional care costs (see [Vermehrte Bedürfnisse](/haftpflicht/vermehrte-beduerfnisse))

## Health insurer vs. self-pay — who pays what

### Health insurer pays directly
- All standard benefits under the statutory health insurance (GKV) catalogue
- Then pursues § 116 SGB X subrogation against the insurer

### You pay first — then recover from the insurer
- **Co-payments** — pharmacy, hospital, therapeutic aids (€10 per prescription, €10/day inpatient)
- **Private-pay services** (private clinic, private practice, IGeL)
- **Travel costs** to appointments
- **Additional costs** above the statutory health insurer's standard (e.g. premium visual aids after glasses are lost in the accident)

### Self-pay therapy where waiting times apply
Where the waiting time for trauma therapy exceeds 4 months, this constitutes a supply shortage: self-pay private practice fees of €80–120/hour are fully reimbursable (in line with BGH case law on accident-related additional costs).

## Private vs. statutory coverage — reimbursement lines

| Scenario | Reimbursement |
|---|---|
| Privately insured | Fully reimbursable (private-rate tariff) |
| Statutory insured, standard treatment | Health insurer + § 116 SGB X subrogation |
| Statutory insured, private practice | Additional costs reimbursable where medically indicated or supply shortage exists |
| IGeL services | Reimbursable where medically indicated after accident (BGH case law) |

## What to do now — in concrete terms

### 💡 The most important rule
Keep every receipt. Even small co-payments (€10 here, €15 there) add up quickly to €200–500 over the course of treatment.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Archive all treatment receipts**
Every prescription, receipt, hospital invoice, and practice receipt — including minor amounts.

**2. Document travel costs**
Date, destination, kilometres driven (€0.30/km) or taxi receipts.

**3. Actively inform your health insurer**
Your health insurer must know the injuries stem from a traffic accident — it will then automatically pursue § 116 subrogation.

**4. Submit self-pay receipts to the insurer**
In monthly or quarterly batches to the opposing insurer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who pays the medical bill after a car accident?**
For statutory health insurance members: the health insurer pays initially and recovers the costs from the insurer (§ 116 SGB X). You only pay co-payments (€10/day inpatient, €10 prescription co-payment) and privately provided services — you recover these directly from the insurer.

**Can I bill therapy privately?**
Where there is a supply shortage (waiting time > 4 months for trauma therapy) or the treatment is medically indicated: yes, additional costs are reimbursable.

**Do I get co-payments reimbursed?**
Yes — all accident-related co-payments (pharmacy, hospital, therapeutic aids) are fully reimbursable as a damage item.

**How much can I claim for travel costs?**
€0.30/km for each treatment trip in your own vehicle. Taxi costs where medically necessary (e.g. where you are unable to drive yourself).

**What are IGeL services?**
IGeL (Individuelle Gesundheitsleistungen) are privately offered medical treatments outside the statutory health insurer's catalogue. Reimbursable where medically indicated following an accident.

**What if the health insurer refuses to cover treatment?**
Use the private-pay route and submit the invoice to the insurer. Reimbursable where there is a clear medical indication.
