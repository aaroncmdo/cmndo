# Social Welfare Subrogation under § 116 SGB X – When Your Health Insurance Joins the Recovery

> **In brief:** When your **statutory health insurance, employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft), or pension insurance** provides accident-related benefits (sick pay, medical treatment, disability pension), your damages claim transfers to that social welfare body to that extent — this is an automatic assignment of claims under § 116 SGB X (German Social Code, Book X). You retain only claims for **your own** out-of-pocket expenses (co-payments, private treatments, the gap between your net income and sick pay, pain and suffering). This is **not a burden** on you — the process runs automatically between the insurers.

## Also known as

§ 116 SGB X · Assignment of claims · Sozialträger-Regress · Priority-of-recovery clause (Quotenvorrecht-Klausel) · Cessio legis

---

## In one sentence

Sozialträger-Regress is the automatic, statutory transfer of your damages claim to statutory social insurance bodies, to the extent they have provided accident-related benefits.

## In three sentences

§ 116 SGB X establishes an **automatic, legally mandated** transfer of claims: as soon as your statutory health insurance, Berufsgenossenschaft, pension insurance, care insurance (Pflegekasse), or social welfare authority provides accident-related benefits, your damages claim against the opposing liability insurer **transfers to that body to that extent** — the social welfare body then pursues its own subrogation claim directly against the insurer and recovers its costs. You retain only the claims for **your own expenses** (co-payments, private treatments, the shortfall between your net income and sick pay, pain and suffering, Wertminderung) — these you submit directly to the insurer. This is **not a burden** on you — you owe the health insurance nothing; the subrogation runs between the insurance carriers.

## The five key figures

- **§ 116 SGB X** — legal basis for social welfare subrogation
- **§ 6 EFZG** — employer subrogation (continued salary payments)
- **§ 86 VVG** (applied by analogy) — health insurance / comprehensive cover subrogation
- **€ 0** for you — no personal outlay
- **Quotenvorrecht** — injured party takes priority over social welfare body when liability is apportioned

---

## Which social welfare bodies pursue subrogation

| Body | What it pays | What it recovers |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory health insurance** | Medical treatment, sick pay | Treatment costs + 70 % of sick pay |
| **Berufsgenossenschaft (employers' liability association)** | Commuting accidents: full cover | Medical treatment + injury allowance |
| **Pension insurance** | Disability pension | Pension payments, to the extent accident-related |
| **Care insurance (Pflegekasse)** | Care allowance, benefits in kind | Care costs |
| **Social welfare authority** | Subsistence support | Subsistence expenditure |
| **Employer (§ 6 EFZG)** | Continued salary for 6 weeks | Continued salary payments |

→ The transfer of claims occurs **automatically** upon the provision of benefits — you do not need to do anything.

---

## What remains with you — what you claim directly from the insurer

| Damage item | Where? |
|---|---|
| **Standard medical treatment costs** | Health insurance → subrogation |
| **Co-payments** (hospital, pharmacy) | You claim directly from the insurer |
| **Private treatments** (private clinic, private practice, IGeL out-of-pocket services) | You claim directly |
| **Gap between sick pay and net income** | You claim directly |
| **Continued salary, weeks 1–6** | Employer → subrogation |
| **Loss of earnings from week 7 onwards (net income minus sick pay)** | You claim directly |
| **Pain and suffering (Schmerzensgeld)** | You claim directly (always your personal claim) |
| **Diminished value (Wertminderung)** | You claim directly |
| **Rental car / Nutzungsausfall (loss of use)** | You claim directly |
| **Expert report costs (Gutachten)** | You claim directly |
| **Legal fees** | You claim directly |

→ You do **not** have to personally pursue the health insurance's share — the health insurance handles that independently.

---

## Priority of recovery for the injured party (Quotenvorrecht)

Where [contributory negligence / apportionment of liability](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-stvg17) is involved, an important question arises: if the insurer pays only 70 % of the damages, who receives which share?

§ 116 para. 1 sentence 3 SGB X establishes the **Quotenvorrecht (priority of recovery) of the injured party**: where reimbursement is limited, the injured party receives their share on a priority basis — the social welfare body ranks behind.

→ In practice: on a 70/30 apportionment, the insurer pays 70 % of the total claim; out of that, the injured party first receives their concrete out-of-pocket expenses, and the social welfare body is paid from whatever remains.

## What to do now — concretely

### 💡 The most important rule
You owe **nothing** to the health insurance. The subrogation runs automatically between the insurance carriers.

### 🛠 Three steps

**1. Notify your health insurance of the accident**
Submit a loss notification to your own health insurance — it will pursue its own subrogation claim.

**2. Submit your own expenses directly to the opposing insurer**
Co-payments, private treatments, income shortfall, pain and suffering — submit these separately.

**3. Keep the Quotenvorrecht in mind**
Where contributory negligence is involved: you take priority over the social welfare body — engage a lawyer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to repay money to the health insurance?**
No. The health insurance recovers its costs directly from the opposing insurer — you are not involved.

**What is the assignment of claims under § 116 SGB X?**
An automatic transfer of claims: as soon as the health insurance has provided accident-related benefits, your claim against the insurer transfers **to that extent** to the health insurance.

**What do I still receive directly?**
Co-payments, private treatments, the gap between sick pay and net income, pain and suffering (Schmerzensgeld), diminished value (Wertminderung), rental car costs, and all items not covered by the health insurance.

**What is the Quotenvorrecht?**
Where contributory negligence / apportionment applies: the injured party receives their share on a **priority** basis ahead of the social welfare body (§ 116 para. 1 sentence 3 SGB X).

**Does the Berufsgenossenschaft also pursue subrogation?**
Yes, for commuting accidents or workplace accidents. The mechanism is identical to that of the health insurance.

**What about the employer's continued salary payments?**
For the first six weeks, the claim transfers to the employer under § 6 EFZG — you have no personal loss-of-earnings claim for that period.
