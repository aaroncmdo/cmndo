# Lane-Change Accident – Who Is Liable in a Lane-Change Collision

> **In brief:** Lane changes are governed by § 7 Abs. 5 StVO (German Road Traffic Regulations): **heightened duty of care** – a lane change may only be made if endangering other road users is ruled out. Anyone who collides with a vehicle travelling straight ahead while changing lanes typically bears **70:30 to 100:0** of the fault – the prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis) is against the lane-changer.

## Also known as

§ 7 Abs. 5 StVO · Fahrstreifenwechsel · Spurwechsel-Kollision

## Fault split by scenario

| Scenario | Lane-changer : Straight-ahead driver |
|---|---|
| Standard lane change without accounting for traffic | **100 : 0** |
| Lane change with straight-ahead driver approaching | 80 : 20 |
| Lane change + straight-ahead driver speeding | 70 : 30 |
| Simultaneous lane change (both parties) | 50 : 50 |
| Lane change + following driver inattentive | 50 : 50 |

## Legal framework

§ 7 Abs. 5 StVO: *"Anyone changing lanes must rule out endangering other road users."*

**Consequence:** A stricter duty of care applies than for the straight-ahead driver – the Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie presumption) is against the lane-changer. This presumption can only be rebutted by proven gross misconduct on the part of the straight-ahead driver (significantly excessive speed, running a red light, sudden acceleration).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is at fault in a lane-change accident?**
The lane-changer – Anscheinsbeweis and § 7 Abs. 5 StVO. Typically 100:0.

**What if the straight-ahead driver was speeding?**
If a substantial speed excess is demonstrable, a contributory fault share of 20–30% may apply.

**What if the lane markings were unclear?**
Where the lane geometry was objectively unrecognisable, rebuttal of the Anscheinsbeweis may be possible, though the evidentiary threshold is high.
