# Who Is the Injured Party — Primary Entitlement to Claim After a Road Accident

> **In brief:** Under German tort law, the injured party (Geschädigter) is any person who directly suffers harm to their body, health, property, or any other legally protected interest as a result of the accident. They hold **independent claims** against the vehicle keeper (§ 7 StVG), the driver (§ 18 StVG), and may assert those claims **directly against the liability insurer** under § 115 VVG (German Insurance Contract Act). The persons primarily concerned: the driver and passengers of the non-at-fault vehicle, injured pedestrians and cyclists, and injured third parties (owners of buildings or animals at the accident scene).

## Also Known As

Primary injured party · Directly injured party · Claimant · Injured person

---

## Explained in One Sentence

The injured party is any person who directly suffers harm to their body, health, or property in the accident — they hold independent claims against the keeper, driver, and liability insurer.

## Explained in Three Sentences

German tort law distinguishes between **primary injured parties** (directly harmed) and **secondary claimants** (e.g. surviving dependants). Primary injured parties are persons whose physical integrity, health, or property was directly affected by the accident — they hold claims under § 7 StVG (keeper), § 18 StVG (driver), § 823 BGB (fault-based liability), and § 115 VVG (direct claim against the liability insurer). Who qualifies as an injured party does **not** depend on vehicle ownership — drivers without a licence, passengers not wearing a seatbelt, and parties involved in the accident with no insurance relationship can all hold primary claims.

## The Five Key Legal Provisions

- **§ 7 StVG** — keeper liability towards the injured party
- **§ 18 StVG** — driver liability
- **§ 823 BGB** — fault-based liability
- **§ 115 VVG** — direct claim against the insurer
- **§ 116 SGB X** — subrogation by social security bodies

---

## Who Is a Primary Injured Party

A primary injured party is any person whose legally protected position was directly affected by the accident:

### Persons
- **Driver of the non-at-fault vehicle** — entitled to claim for property damage even without a medical certificate
- **Passengers / front-seat passengers** — independent claims; see [passenger claims](/haftpflicht/beifahrer-anspruch)
- **Pedestrians** — where a motor vehicle was involved
- **Cyclists** — when harmed by a motor vehicle
- **Owner of the damaged vehicle** — even if they were not driving at the time

### Property Damage Claimants
- **Owner of a damaged building, wall, or fence** at the accident scene
- **Owner of a damaged item in or on the vehicle**
- **Keeper of an animal that was killed or injured**

### Important Special Cases
- **Driver without a licence** — claim is not excluded (contributory negligence possible)
- **Passenger who was not wearing a seatbelt** — claim exists, but [contributory negligence](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-bgb254) applies
- **Injured party travelling in the same vehicle as the at-fault party** (e.g. a family member in the car of an at-fault driver) — claim against the liability insurer **of that driver's own** policy

## Direct Claim Against the Insurer — § 115 VVG

One of the most important provisions for injured parties: **§ 115 VVG** allows you to assert your claim **directly against the at-fault party's liability insurer** — without going through the keeper. In practice:

- Direct correspondence with the insurer
- Direct right to bring proceedings
- The insurer stands in the same position as the policyholder

→ The keeper's insurer is the correct address for your claims — even where the keeper and driver are different persons.

## What to Do Now — Step by Step

### 💡 The Most Important Rule
You are an injured party if the accident directly harmed you or your property. Vehicle ownership, possession of a driving licence, and insurance contract relationships are secondary.

### 🛠 Three Steps

**1. Identify your claims**
Which heads of damage apply? Property damage + personal injury + out-of-pocket expenses.

**2. Identify the keeper and insurer**
Police report / keeper information from the KBA (Federal Motor Transport Authority). Direct claim against the insurer under § 115 VVG.

**3. Instruct a lawyer**
Legal costs are paid by the insurer (BGH VI ZR 235/13). You bear no financial risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who is the injured party in a road accident?**
Any person or legal entity whose body, health, property, or other legally protected interest was directly harmed by the accident.

**Do I have a claim as a passenger?**
Yes, independently — see [passenger claims](/haftpflicht/beifahrer-anspruch).

**Do I have a claim as a driver without a licence?**
Yes, the claim exists — but may be reduced due to [contributory negligence](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-bgb254).

**Can I claim directly against the insurer?**
Yes, § 115 VVG grants you the direct claim.

**What if I am not the owner of the vehicle?**
Ownership is secondary. The injured party is whoever was directly harmed by the accident — for property damage, that is the owner of the vehicle (which may be a third party, e.g. a leasing company).
