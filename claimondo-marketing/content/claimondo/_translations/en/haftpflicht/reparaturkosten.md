# Repair Costs After a Road Accident – What Is Covered and What Is Not

> **In brief:** Repair costs are the central property damage claim in road accident cases – covered items include hourly labour rates, original spare parts with UPE (list-price) surcharges, paintwork, transfer costs and test drives. Up to the **130% threshold** of the replacement value (BGH VI ZR 70/04), repair is a mandatory reimbursement entitlement – even if it costs more than a replacement vehicle. You choose between **notional settlement** (based on the expert report, without VAT) and **actual settlement** (based on the workshop invoice, with VAT).

## Also known as

Repair reimbursement · repair damage · property damage · vehicle repair costs

---

## Explained in one sentence

Repair costs are the costs of professionally restoring your vehicle to its pre-accident condition – fully reimbursable up to the 130% threshold, with a choice between notional and actual settlement.

## Explained in three sentences

When your vehicle is damaged, the insurer owes full reimbursement of the costs required for restoration under § 249 BGB (German Civil Code, statutory basis for damage reinstatement) – repair costs are the primary item and cover hourly labour rates, original spare parts, UPE surcharges, paintwork, transfer costs and test-drive costs. For newer vehicles (under 3 years old) or those with an uninterrupted authorised-dealer service history, the authorised-workshop entitlement applies, commanding higher hourly rates and original parts – see [Werkstatt-Netz](/decoder/werkstatt-netz). You decide between **actual settlement** (following genuine repair, including 19% VAT) and **notional settlement** (based on the expert report without actual repair, excluding VAT) – both are permissible under the BGH line of decisions.

## The five key figures

- **130%** threshold (replacement value × 1.30 = maximum reimbursable repair cost)
- **6 months** retention requirement when repair costs exceed 100% of replacement value
- **10–20%** typical UPE surcharges on spare parts
- **§ 249 Abs. 2 BGB** as the statutory basis
- **0% VAT** under notional settlement, **19% VAT** under actual settlement

---

## What counts as repair costs

### Hourly labour rates
- **Authorised workshop:** typically €130–200/h – reimbursable where the entitlement applies
- **Independent workshop:** typically €90–130/h
- Insurer reductions to a "regional average" are impermissible where an authorised-workshop entitlement exists (see [Decoder Werkstattnetz](/decoder/werkstatt-netz))

### Spare parts + UPE surcharges
- **Original parts** are mandatory where the authorised-workshop entitlement applies
- **UPE surcharges** (surcharges on the manufacturer's recommended retail price) typically 10–20% on list price – recognised by the BGH as customary
- **Pattern parts / remanufactured parts** only with your express consent

→ For an explanation of the differences between original, pattern and used parts, and which you are entitled to: [Spare-Part Quality](/haftpflicht/ersatzteil-qualitaet).

### Paintwork costs
- Paint labour hours + materials
- Higher for modern three-coat paintwork
- Special finishes (pearl, metallic): surcharge justified

→ For metallic and effect finishes, **blending of adjacent panels** is also required – technically unavoidable in most cases and one of the most frequently struck line items: [Beilackierung](/haftpflicht/beilackierung).

### Calibration of driver-assistance systems
- Required after work on the windscreen, bumper, mirrors or chassis
- A separate operation requiring dedicated equipment – not included in the hourly rate
- Safety-critical: a misaligned automatic emergency braking system triggers no fault warning

→ When calibration is due and why the manufacturer specification governs: [ADAS Calibration](/haftpflicht/adas-kalibrierung).

### Transfer costs (Verbringungskosten)
- Transport between the bodywork shop and the paint shop
- Typically €100–300
- Insurer reductions citing "not necessary" are frequently unsubstantiated

### Test-drive costs
- Functional check after repair
- Standard line item, fully reimbursable

## The 130% rule – when repair takes precedence over replacement

Even if repair costs more than a comparable replacement vehicle, you are entitled to have your vehicle repaired – as long as repair costs do not exceed **130% of the replacement value**. Requirements (BGH VI ZR 70/04):

1. **Integrity interest** – you wish to keep your vehicle
2. **Professionally executed repair** in accordance with the expert report
3. **6-month retention requirement** – you continue using the vehicle for at least 6 months after repair

### Example calculation

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Replacement value (per expert report) | €15,000 |
| 130% threshold | **€19,500** |
| Repair costs per expert report | €18,000 |
| → Repair within 130% threshold | ✓ |

Where repair costs **exceed** 130%: only replacement value minus residual value. See [Decoder "Reparatur unwirtschaftlich"](/decoder/reparatur-unwirtschaftlich).

---

## Notional vs. actual settlement

You decide **how** to settle – both routes are permissible under the BGH line of decisions:

| | Notional settlement | Actual settlement |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis** | Expert report | Workshop invoice |
| **Repair carried out** | No (or self-repair) | Yes |
| **VAT** | **Not reimbursable** | **Reimbursable (19%)** |
| **Hourly labour rate** | Per expert report | Actually paid |
| **When advisable** | If you repair the vehicle yourself or sell it | If you have the vehicle repaired at a workshop |

### Practical consequence
On a notional settlement of €15,000, the VAT difference amounts to approximately €2,400 – meaning actual settlement at an authorised workshop yields more financially, but is contingent on the repair being carried out.

→ If you settle on a notional basis but claim Nutzungsausfall (loss of use) or rely on the 130% rule, the insurer will require proof that the vehicle was actually repaired: [Reparaturbestätigung](/haftpflicht/reparaturbestaetigung).

---

## Workshop risk – you are not liable for workshop errors

A key BGH principle: if the workshop carries out a defective repair or overcharges, that is the **at-fault party's risk** – not yours (the so-called "Werkstatt-Risiko" under established BGH case law). You are not required to supervise the workshop, and you are not required to choose the cheapest provider.

→ Consequence: even if the workshop invoice exceeds the expert report because the workshop charged more, the additional amount is enforceable against the insurer.

## What to do now – step by step

### 💡 The most important rule
Commissioning your own expert report (Gutachten) is essential – it is the basis for calculating repair costs. Insurer-commissioned assessments tend to set hourly rates and UPE surcharges lower.

### 🛠 Four steps

**1. Commission an expert report**
A BVSK-certified expert of your choosing. Costs are borne by the insurer (see [SV-Kosten](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten)).

**2. Choose your workshop**
Where the authorised-workshop entitlement applies: use an authorised dealer. Otherwise, any independent workshop of your choice – the insurer's partner workshop is not mandatory (see [Decoder Werkstattnetz](/decoder/werkstatt-netz)).

**3. Decide on the settlement method**
Notional or actual settlement – depending on whether the vehicle has been repaired and whether you need VAT reimbursement.

**4. Instruct a lawyer if reductions exceed €500**
In disputes over UPE surcharges, hourly rate reductions or transfer costs, escalation is worthwhile. Legal fees are borne by the insurer.

## What the insurer typically claims

> *"The hourly rates are excessive – we are reducing them to the regional average per Schwacke."*

Impermissible where an authorised-workshop entitlement exists (BGH VI ZR 320/12). The Schwacke average is not the benchmark when an authorised-workshop entitlement applies.

> *"UPE surcharges cannot be reimbursed."*

Incorrect. The BGH recognises UPE surcharges as customary. Insurer reductions are routinely unsubstantiated.

> *"Transfer costs are not necessary."*

A blanket argument. Where the bodywork shop and paint shop operate separately, transfer is necessary – and therefore reimbursable.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much in repair costs can be reimbursed after a road accident?**
Full reimbursement of the costs required for professionally restoring the vehicle – hourly labour rates, spare parts, UPE surcharges, paintwork, transfer costs and test-drive costs. Up to the 130% threshold of the replacement value.

**What is notional settlement?**
You receive the repair costs per the expert report without actually having the repair carried out. VAT (19%) is then **not** reimbursable – it is only reimbursed following actual repair.

**Does the insurer have to pay for an authorised-workshop repair?**
For vehicles under 3 years old or with an uninterrupted authorised-dealer service history: yes (BGH VI ZR 53/09). Higher hourly rates and original spare parts are also fully reimbursable.

**What if the workshop overcharges?**
Workshop errors or excessive charges are the **at-fault party's risk** under the BGH Werkstatt-Risiko line of decisions – the insurer bears any additional costs, not you.

**Is VAT always reimbursed?**
Only under actual settlement (vehicle genuinely repaired). Not under notional settlement – this is an important distinction.

**How do repair costs interact with Wertminderung (diminished value)?**
Wertminderung is a **separate claim item** in addition to repair costs. Even after a professionally executed repair, the vehicle is regarded on the used-car market as an accident vehicle – see [Wertminderung](/haftpflicht/wertminderung).
