# Motor Vehicle Third-Party Liability Claims – The Complete Guide for Injured Parties

> **In brief:** Anyone injured in a no-fault road accident in Germany has very extensive legal claims – from repair costs and Wertminderung (diminished value) to pain-and-suffering compensation and lost earnings, through to lifelong annuities for severe injuries. Legal costs are paid by the opposing insurer (BGH VI ZR 235/13); you carry no financial risk. This guide takes you systematically through every type of claim, all deadlines, typical conflicts with the insurance company, and the right response to every insurer letter.

## Also known as

Motor vehicle liability claim settlement · Road accident claims · Compensation for car accident · Third-party liability claim guide

**Just happened?** If you are looking for the first steps immediately after the accident, the step-by-step guide [Accident – what to do as an injured party?](/unfall-was-tun-als-geschaedigter) will take you safely through the first 48 hours. This guide then explains your claims in detail.

---

## Explained in one sentence

In a no-fault road accident you are entitled to compensation for vehicle damage, personal injury and consequential costs – the opposing liability insurer pays in full, legal costs included.

## Explained in three sentences

German damages law gives road accident victims a comprehensive package of claims: property damage (repair, Wertminderung, rental car, Nutzungsausfall (loss of use), expert report costs), personal injury (Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering), medical treatment, lost earnings, household management losses), lifelong annuities for severe injuries (increased needs, loss of earning capacity) and all consequential costs – from towing to psychotherapy. The legal bases are § 7 StVG (keeper liability), § 18 StVG (driver liability) and § 823 BGB (fault-based liability); claims are enforced directly against the tortfeasor's liability insurer (§ 115 VVG direct claim). Legal costs are paid by the opposing insurer (BGH, judgment of 18.07.2017, VI ZR 235/13) – those who negotiate without a lawyer statistically leave 20–80 % on the table.

## The five key figures

- **§ 7 StVG** Keeper liability without fault
- **§ 115 VVG** Direct claim against the liability insurer
- **BGH VI ZR 235/13** Legal costs fully reimbursable in a no-fault accident
- **3 years** Limitation period (§ 195 BGB), for personal injury claims up to 30 years (§ 199 para. 2 BGB)
- **4 weeks** Reasonable review period; thereafter default with interest and legal costs

## <a name="akut"></a>1. The first 72 hours – immediate measures

In the hours and days after the accident, the strength of your subsequent negotiating position is determined. Adrenaline masks pain; evidence loses its probative value quickly. The following steps are essential:

### Directly at the accident scene
1. **Safety**: hazard lights, warning triangle, attend to injured persons
2. **Call the police (110)**: for personal injuries, property damage above approx. €750, disputes, suspected hit-and-run
3. **Photo documentation**: damage, final vehicle positions, skid marks, lighting conditions, other party's personal details, vehicle registration document
4. **Secure witness addresses**: impossible to reconstruct later
5. **Exchange personal details** and note down the claim reference number

### Within the first 24 hours
1. **See a doctor** – even for apparently minor complaints. Adrenaline masks pain; whiplash symptoms often appear only after 6–24 hours.
2. **Notify your own insurer** of the accident
3. **[Commission your own expert](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten)** – BVSK member, freely chosen, costs paid by the insurer

### Within 7 days
1. **Request the police report**
2. **Start a symptom diary** (daily)
3. **Archive treatment records** without gaps
4. **Instruct a lawyer** – legal costs are paid by the opposing insurer where liability is clear

## <a name="haftung"></a>2. Who is liable – the liability system

The German motor vehicle liability system has **three parallel pillars** that together cover practically every damage scenario:

### Pillar 1: § 7 StVG – Keeper liability without fault
The keeper of a motor vehicle is liable **regardless of fault** for any damage arising from the operation of their vehicle – this is the central **strict liability** provision of German road traffic law. The only defence is force majeure (§ 7 para. 2 StVG), which succeeds very rarely in practice.

→ [Detail: § 7 StVG operational risk](/haftpflicht/betriebsgefahr-stvg7)

### Pillar 2: § 18 StVG – Driver liability with presumed fault
The driver is personally liable; their fault is **presumed** – to be relieved of liability they must prove they exercised the required standard of care. This succeeds rarely in practice.

→ [Detail: § 18 StVG driver liability](/haftpflicht/fahrerhaftung-stvg18)

### Pillar 3: § 823 BGB – General fault-based liability
Primarily relevant for non-motor-vehicle tortfeasors (cyclists, pedestrians) and for particularly serious breaches.

→ [Detail: § 823 BGB fault](/haftpflicht/verschulden-bgb823)

### Where multiple vehicles are involved: § 17 StVG shared liability
Where multiple vehicles are involved, liability is apportioned according to causative contributions – combining fault and operational risk.

→ [Detail: § 17 StVG contributory liability](/haftpflicht/mitverschulden-stvg17)

### The decisive lever: prima facie evidence (Anscheinsbeweis)
In standard constellations ([rear-end collision](/haftpflicht/auffahrunfall), [right-of-way](/haftpflicht/vorfahrt-rechts-vor-links), [red light](/haftpflicht/rotlicht)), the **Anscheinsbeweis (prima facie evidence) automatically operates in your favour** – you need prove nothing; it is for the other side to rebut it. In most typical road accident constellations, this is the key legal point.

→ [Detail: Anscheinsbeweis](/haftpflicht/anscheinsbeweis)

### Direct claim against the insurer – § 115 VVG
You communicate **directly** with the tortfeasor's liability insurer, not via the keeper. This is the practically most important provision for claims settlement.

## <a name="anspruchsberechtigte"></a>3. Who has claims

An injured party is **any person** whose body, health, property or legally protected position was directly affected by the accident. This covers far more people than is often assumed:

### Primary injured parties
- **Driver of the non-responsible vehicle**
- **[Front-seat and rear-seat passengers](/haftpflicht/beifahrer-anspruch)** – including within a family vehicle context
- **[Pedestrians and cyclists](/haftpflicht/dritte-beteiligte)** – where a motor vehicle is involved
- **Owners of damaged vehicles** – even if they were not driving
- **Owners of damaged property** (buildings, fences, animals)

→ [Detail: Primary injured parties](/haftpflicht/geschaedigte-primaer)

### In a fatal accident – bereaved relatives and heirs
- **[Hinterbliebenengeld (bereavement compensation)](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld)** – lump sum €5,000–€15,000 per close relative
- **[Unterhaltsschaden (maintenance loss)](/haftpflicht/unterhaltsschaden)** – annuity claim for surviving spouses and children
- **[Funeral costs](/haftpflicht/beerdigungskosten)** – in keeping with the deceased's station in life
- **[Succession of heirs](/haftpflicht/erben-rechtsnachfolger)** – all claims arising up to the time of death
- **[Schockschaden (shock damage)](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich)** – where relatives develop genuine psychological illness

### Social security subrogation
Health insurers, employers' liability insurance associations and pension insurers automatically reclaim their outgoings from the tortfeasor's insurer – you retain only the uncovered portion of the claim.

→ [Detail: Social security subrogation § 116 SGB X](/haftpflicht/sozialtraeger-regress)

## <a name="fristen"></a>5. Deadlines – default, limitation, acknowledgement

### The 4-week settlement deadline
Where liability is clear and all documents have been submitted, the insurer must settle **within 4 weeks**. After that, [default under § 286 BGB (German Civil Code on default)](/haftpflicht/verzug-bgb286) arises automatically – with default interest and fully recoverable legal costs.

→ [Detail: 4-week deadline](/haftpflicht/4-wochen-frist)

### Default interest
5 percentage points above the base rate, running from the date of default, on the full amount properly due.

→ [Detail: § 288 BGB default interest](/haftpflicht/verzugszinsen-bgb288)

### Limitation
**3 years** from the end of the year in which the injured party became aware of the loss and the tortfeasor (§§ 195 + 199 BGB). For personal injury claims, the long-stop is 30 years.

→ [Detail: § 195 BGB limitation](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195)

### Acknowledgement and settlement – the most important legal distinction
An acknowledgement of liability (§ 212 BGB) restarts the limitation period – advantageous for you. A settlement (§ 779 BGB) definitively ends the dispute – **never sign without a reservation for late-onset consequences**.

→ [Detail: § 212 BGB acknowledgement / § 779 BGB settlement](/haftpflicht/anerkenntnis-bgb212)

## <a name="szenarien"></a>6. Standard accident scenarios and their apportionment percentages

In the vast majority of road accident constellations, the apportionment is already clear by virtue of the Anscheinsbeweis – you do not need to prove the same point from scratch each time.

### Clear Anscheinsbeweis constellations (100 : 0)
- [Rear-end collision](/haftpflicht/auffahrunfall) – Anscheinsbeweis against the vehicle that drove into the back
- [Red light violation](/haftpflicht/rotlicht) – against the red-light runner
- [Right-of-way: right before left](/haftpflicht/vorfahrt-rechts-vor-links) – against the right-of-way violator
- [Priority signs](/haftpflicht/vorfahrt-schilder) – against the driver who ignored the sign
- [Opening a car door](/haftpflicht/tueroeffnen) – against the person who opened the door
- [U-turn](/haftpflicht/wenden) – against the driver performing the manoeuvre

### With a higher duty of care on one party (typically 80 : 20 to 100 : 0)
- [Lane change](/haftpflicht/spurwechsel)
- [Left-turn](/haftpflicht/linksabbieger)
- [Overtaking](/haftpflicht/ueberholen)

### Special situations
- [Car park](/haftpflicht/parkplatz) – depends on the character of the road
- [Collision with wildlife](/haftpflicht/wildunfall) – comprehensive insurance (Kasko) matter, not third-party liability
- [Black ice / aquaplaning](/haftpflicht/glatteis-aquaplaning) – appropriate speed was mandatory

## <a name="aktionsplan"></a>9. What to do now – step by step

### Phase 1: Acute (0–72 hours)
1. Police, photo documentation, secure witness addresses
2. See a doctor (even if you feel "fine")
3. Inform your own insurer
4. Commission an expert

### Phase 2: Assessment (weeks 1–2)
1. Wait for the expert report (Gutachten)
2. Place workshop order or arrange replacement vehicle
3. Archive treatment records
4. Instruct a lawyer – essential for personal injury, recommended from €1,500 in property damage

### Phase 3: Claim (weeks 2–4)
1. Letter of claim with 4-week deadline (registered mail with return receipt)
2. List all heads of loss in full – miss nothing
3. Await the insurer's response

### Phase 4: Negotiation
1. Reject blanket arguments using the decoder responses
2. Contributory negligence quota: demand specific factual grounds
3. Schmerzensgeld: refer to Hacks/Wellner tables
4. If progress stalls: force the insurer into default by allowing the deadline to expire

### Phase 5: Litigation / dunning proceedings
1. Obtain legal counsel's assessment of the litigation merits
2. Dunning proceedings for clear monetary claims
3. Litigation for disputed constellations
4. Legal costs are paid by the insurer

## <a name="anwalt"></a>10. When a lawyer is essential

### A lawyer is clearly recommended for:
- **Every personal injury case** – however minor. Late-onset consequences are more common than expected.
- **Property damage above €1,500** – the gap between your figure and the insurer's assessment is typically 15–40 %.
- **Disputed liability** – enforcing the Anscheinsbeweis correctly.
- **Settlement offers** – a reservation for late-onset consequences is essential.
- **Blanket reductions** – these can be specifically challenged.

### A lawyer is not essential for:
- **Minor property-only damage** (< €750) without disputes
- **Fully settled claim** without any reductions

### Costs
In a no-fault accident: **€0** for you. Legal costs are paid by the opposing insurer as default damages (BGH VI ZR 235/13).

→ [Detail: Legal costs reimbursement](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung)

## <a name="faq"></a>11. Frequently asked questions

**1. Who pays after a road accident?**
In a no-fault accident, the opposing liability insurer. All property damage and personal injury losses, plus legal costs.

**2. How long may the insurer take to review the claim?**
4 weeks where liability is clear, 6–8 weeks for complex personal injury cases (BGH standard). After that, default arises with interest.

**3. Can I claim Schmerzensgeld even for minor injuries?**
Yes – for whiplash (HWS-Distorsion) from grade I already €250–€1,500. For grade II €800–€3,500. For chronic PTSD up to €80,000.

**4. What does a lawyer cost me?**
In a no-fault accident: nothing. Legal costs are paid by the insurer (BGH VI ZR 235/13).

**5. May the insurer dictate which workshop I use?**
No. You have free choice of workshop. Where you are entitled to a manufacturer-authorised workshop (vehicle under 3 years old or unbroken service history), you even have the right to insist on one.

**6. When do my claims become time-barred?**
3 years from the end of the year in which you became aware of the loss and the tortfeasor. For personal injury claims the long-stop is 30 years.

**7. What does the 130 % rule mean?**
Repair costs up to 130 % of the Wiederbeschaffungswert (replacement value) are recoverable – provided you keep the vehicle and have it repaired properly and professionally (BGH VI ZR 70/04).

**8. Can I claim Nutzungsausfall even without hiring a replacement car?**
Yes. The flat-rate Sanden-Danner daily rates of €27–€175/day apply; the presumption of private use operates and you do not need to prove individual journeys.

**9. Are late-onset consequences still possible after a settlement?**
Only if the settlement text contains an express reservation for late-onset consequences. Without such a reservation, all subsequent consequences are extinguished.

**10. Who is liable in a rear-end collision?**
As a rule, the vehicle that drove into the back – Anscheinsbeweis, apportionment 100 : 0.

**11. Can I claim compensation for a collision with wildlife?**
From comprehensive insurance (Kasko – either partial or full), not from third-party liability insurance. There is no tortfeasor in the classical sense.

**12. What if there is a hit-and-run?**
Verkehrsopferhilfe (road traffic victims' fund) steps in where the tortfeasor is unknown or uninsured. Application must be made within 3 years.

**13. What happens with continued salary payment and sickness benefit?**
The health insurer and employer pursue direct subrogation claims against the insurer (§ 116 SGB X, § 6 EFZG). You claim only the uncovered difference (net income minus sickness benefit) plus Schmerzensgeld directly.

**14. Can I claim against my own family member?**
You claim against their liability insurer, not against them personally. The insurer pays objectively, without personal conflict.

**15. What is Hinterbliebenengeld?**
A lump-sum payment of €5,000–€15,000 to close relatives in the event of a fatal accident (§ 844 para. 3 BGB). Introduced in 2017, in addition to Schockschaden (shock damage compensation) and Unterhaltsschaden (maintenance loss).
