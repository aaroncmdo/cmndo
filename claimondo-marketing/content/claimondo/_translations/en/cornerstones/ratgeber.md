# What You Really Want to Know After a Road Accident – The Guide

> **In brief:** An accident that wasn't your fault raises not only legal questions but deeply human ones: Who can I trust? What do I tell the insurance company? Am I imagining these symptoms? No pressure, no sales pitch, no legal posturing. We are your independent expert appraiser (calculation basis: Audatex III), we work together with a specialist road traffic law firm, and we know the insurers' patterns from a thousand similar cases.

## <a name="frisch"></a>1. If someone has just driven into you

### The situation

Two or three days ago, something happened that you never wanted: someone rear-ends you, cuts into you, fails to see you when turning left. Nobody is seriously injured, but the car is damaged and your head is full of questions. You are getting calls from the opposing insurance company. Perhaps from a repair shop that somehow already knows about it. Possibly from an "expert" who can "come straight over." And you are thinking: what do I do now? Am I doing something wrong?

### The most important point

**You are the injured party. The other side pays for your damage – not you.** That applies to repairs, Wertminderung (diminished value), rental car, expert report and even lawyer's fees ([BGH VI ZR 235/13](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung)). You do not have to solve everything at once today.

### The most common questions – and the honest answers

**"The opposing insurance company wants to take care of everything – is that a good thing?"**
It sounds helpful, but caution is warranted. The opposing insurance company pays for your damage – it is, however, **not on your side**. Whoever "handles everything" handles it with their own expert, their own repair shop and their own figures. You have the right to **free choice of expert appraiser** and **free choice of repair shop** ([Decoder: "Unser Sachverständiger"](/decoder/unser-sachverstaendiger), [Decoder: Werkstattnetz](/decoder/werkstatt-netz)).

**"Do I even need a lawyer? It's clear who's at fault."**
Precisely because fault is clear, a lawyer is worthwhile. What gets disputed is rarely fault itself, but the **amount** – repair costs, Wertminderung, rental car daily rate, pain and suffering. Lawyer's fees are borne by the opposing insurer in an accident that wasn't your fault. Details: [Lawyer's fee reimbursement](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung).

**"What do I say on the phone to the opposing insurance company?"**
As little as possible. **No** statements on fault, **no** statements on amounts, **no** statements on symptoms. Politely refer them to your expert appraiser and (if engaged) to the law firm. Nothing more is needed today.

**"What does the expert report cost me?"**
In an accident that wasn't your fault: **nothing**. Expert appraiser costs are an independent item of damages (BGH VI ZR 67/06) and must be reimbursed in full by the insurer. Exception: minor damage cases below approximately €750 – a repair shop quote is often sufficient there. Details: [Expert appraiser costs](/haftpflicht/sv-kosten).

**"Should I have it repaired – or take a cash settlement?"**
Both are possible. You can settle **on a concrete basis** (actual repair invoice, including VAT) or **on a notional basis** (net amount per the expert report, excluding VAT). Which route is better depends on your car, your plans and your entitlement to a branded workshop. Details: [Repair costs](/haftpflicht/reparaturkosten).

**"What is this 'Wertminderung'?"**
Even if your car is professionally repaired, it is regarded on the second-hand market as an "accident vehicle" – and is worth less. This **merkantile Wertminderung** (diminished market value) is part of your damage **in addition** to repair costs. It is quantified in the expert report. Typical ranges: €500–€3,000 for mid-range vehicles. Insurers often "forget" this item. Details: [Wertminderung](/haftpflicht/wertminderung).

**"The insurer has paid less than the expert report states – are they allowed to do that?"**
They are **not simply** entitled to delete items. Standard insurer deductions target transport costs, UPE mark-ups, hourly rates and Wertminderung. There is a substantive counter-argument for every deduction, and in most cases a clear BGH line of authority. Response templates: [Decoder Hub](/kfz-haftpflicht-schaden#decoder).

**"Will my own insurance premium go up?"**
For an accident that wasn't your fault, processed through the **opposing party's liability cover**: **no**. Important: do not inadvertently make a claim on your own comprehensive cover where it is not necessary. Details: [Kasko vs. Haftpflicht (comprehensive vs. liability cover)](/haftpflicht/kasko-versicherung).

### What to do next

1. Secure **photographic documentation** – final positions, damage, other party's details, witnesses
2. Request the **police report** (if one was taken)
3. **Instruct your own expert appraiser** – we coordinate within 48 hours
4. **On insurer calls**: politely refer them on, sign nothing
5. **Engage the law firm** through our partner law firm – at no cost to you

## <a name="verletzt"></a>2. If your body only reports the damage later

### The situation

Right after the accident you thought: nothing's wrong. Three days later your neck aches, your head throbs, you're sleeping badly. Perhaps you flinch when braking or no longer drive that stretch of road. You are asking yourself: am I imagining this? Is this normal? And you are wondering: does what I'm going through actually have a legal "value"?

### The most important point

**Delayed symptoms after an accident are the rule, not the exception.** Adrenaline masks pain in the first few hours. Cervical spine distortion (HWS-Distorsion) often does not make itself felt until 6–24 hours later. The fact that you are psychologically shaken is also a **normal stress response**, not a failure. Both may – and should – be medically documented.

### The most common questions

**"Was it worse than I thought?"**
Possibly. Symptoms that appear only hours or days later are typical with HWS-Distorsion in particular. Please have it medically assessed and documented. We are appraisers for your vehicle – for questions about your body, you need qualified medical hands.

**"Do I have to tell the insurance company anything about my injury?"**
**No.** You are not obliged to comment on your injury, the circumstances or fault on the phone. Such conversations are often used against you later. Politely refer them to the law firm and the expert appraiser.

**"Should I keep a symptom diary – or am I just imagining things?"**
Writing it down is exactly right. Pain levels, sleep quality, what you can no longer do – noted briefly every day. This helps you substantiate your situation in a verifiable way and carries legal weight (reduced burden of proof under § 287 ZPO, see [Burden of proof](/haftpflicht/beweislast)). You are not imagining anything.

**"I'm sleeping badly and I won't drive that route any more – is that trauma?"**
These are common responses after an accident, initially a **normal reaction** to something threatening. Whether more develops from them (adjustment disorder, PTSD, driving anxiety) can only be assessed by a professional – your GP, a psychotherapist or the medical on-call service. Please do not treat this as weakness. Crisis numbers: **Telefonseelsorge 0800 111 0 111**, **on-call medical service 116 117**.

**"Does what I'm going through have a legal value?"**
Yes. Physical and psychological consequences of an accident that was not your fault give rise to a claim for [Schmerzensgeld (pain and suffering, § 253 BGB)](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253) as well as reimbursement of [medical treatment costs](/haftpflicht/heilbehandlungskosten) and [loss of earnings](/haftpflicht/verdienstausfall). The prerequisite is evidence: certificates, diagnoses, diary.

**"How much Schmerzensgeld am I entitled to?"**
No reliable figure can be given without the full picture. Indicative ranges from court practice (Hacks/Wellner tables): for HWS-Distorsion Grade I approximately €250–€1,500, Grade II €800–€3,500, Grade III €3,000–€10,000. Significantly higher for chronic developments or PTSD. The amount rises with the quality of your documentation. Details: [Schmerzensgeld § 253 BGB](/haftpflicht/schmerzensgeld-bgb253), [Decoder "Schmerzensgeld angemessen"](/decoder/schmerzensgeld-angemessen).

**"The insurer is offering a lump-sum settlement covering everything – should I accept?"**
As long as your treatment is ongoing and the final outcome is not clear: **no**. A lump-sum settlement without an express **reservation for late-onset consequences** locks you in too early. If symptoms persist later, you may be left with no recourse. Details: [Decoder "Pauschal-Abgeltung"](/decoder/pauschal-abgeltung), [§ 212 BGB acknowledgement/settlement](/haftpflicht/anerkenntnis-bgb212).

**"If symptoms persist later, can I still make a claim further down the line?"**
With the right reservation: yes. There are legal instruments that keep claims for late-onset consequences open so that nothing becomes time-barred. Maximum limitation period for personal injury claims: **30 years** ([§ 195 BGB limitation](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195)).

**"What about my job?"**
If your earning capacity is permanently impaired, that too can form part of your claim – under the headings of [loss of earnings](/haftpflicht/verdienstausfall) and, in serious cases, [loss of earning capacity](/haftpflicht/erwerbsminderungsschaden) under § 843 BGB.

### What to do next

1. **See a doctor** – your GP or, if necessary, A&E. Documentation is invaluable.
2. **Start a symptom diary** – brief, daily, honest
3. **No phone conversations** with the opposing insurer about injuries
4. **Accept no lump-sum settlement** while you are still in treatment
5. **Engage a lawyer** – through our partner law firm, at no cost to you

## <a name="angehoerige"></a>3. If your closest person was injured in the car

### The situation

It has been weeks, and you are functioning – somehow. Your partner, your child, your parent has been seriously injured. You are barely sleeping. You no longer recognise yourself. You have to read letters, coordinate appointments, deal with applications. And at the same time you are asking yourself whether you even have the right to think about money or legal claims.

### The most important point

**You do not have to decide anything today, sign anything or deal with anything.** The law firm handles the legal side. The insurers' correspondence goes to them, not to you. You set the pace – we wait at your pace.

### The most common questions

**"Will I ever get through this?"**
There is no expert appraiser's answer to that question and no lawyer's answer. What we can say is: **you do not have to face this alone.** Your GP, psychotherapy, pastoral care, a crisis service – there are people whose entire purpose is exactly this. In acute crisis: **Telefonseelsorge 0800 111 0 111** (free, anonymous, 24/7), or **112**.

**"I feel wrong for thinking about money."**
That feeling is understandable. This is not about "getting something out of it." It is about ensuring that the injustice done to you and your person is also legally acknowledged – and that you do not collapse financially on top of everything else during this time.

**"What is 'Schockschaden' – and does it apply to me?"**
If a close person is seriously injured or killed in a road accident and you yourself suffer a distinct psychological illness as a result (PTSD, severe adjustment disorder, clinical depression), you may have **your own independent claim** ([BGH line on Schockschaden since 1985](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich)). Whether this applies in your case is assessed by the law firm together with medical documentation – you do not need to work that out yourself. Typical ranges: €5,000–€50,000 depending on severity.

**"What is Hinterbliebenengeld – isn't that a mockery?"**
No sum bears any relation to the loss. That is not the intention. **Hinterbliebenengeld** (bereavement compensation) is a legal acknowledgement of suffering, not a "price" for a human being. It was introduced in 2017 under § 844 para. 3 BGB – typically €5,000–€15,000 per close family member, in addition to any Schockschaden and dependency claims. Details: [Hinterbliebenengeld](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld).

**"What if your person survived the accident but now requires permanent care?"**
The additional care costs are fully reimbursable – including where care is provided by family members free of charge (notional rate of €10–€14/hour). In addition: disability-adapted modifications, aids, long-term therapy. Details: [Pflege-Mehrbedarf (additional care costs)](/haftpflicht/pflege-mehrbedarf), [Vermehrte Bedürfnisse (increased needs)](/haftpflicht/vermehrte-beduerfnisse).

**"In the event of death – what am I entitled to?"**
Four pillars of claims accumulate: [funeral costs](/haftpflicht/beerdigungskosten), [dependency loss](/haftpflicht/unterhaltsschaden) (an annuity for widows/widowers and children), [Hinterbliebenengeld](/haftpflicht/hinterbliebenengeld) and, where applicable, [Schockschaden](/haftpflicht/schockschaden-rechtlich) if you yourself develop an illness. In addition, claims that accrued up to the time of death pass to the [heirs](/haftpflicht/erben-rechtsnachfolger) (§ 1922 BGB), including Schmerzensgeld (inheritable since the BGH's landmark ruling in 1985).

**"Can the law firm really take this on?"**
Yes. The insurers' correspondence goes to the law firm, not to you. You are informed only about what you want to be informed about. If the insurer challenges something – which happens – we intercept it so that it does not add to your burden.

### What to do next

Nothing – if that is what you want. You do not have to decide anything today. When you are ready, call or write to us. We come to you, by phone or in person. Until then: please take care of yourself.

**In acute crisis:** Telefonseelsorge **0800 111 0 111** (free, anonymous, 24/7) · **112**

## <a name="eltern"></a>4. If your child was in the car and you feel responsible

### The situation

A few days ago – you had your child in the car. Someone drove into you. Your child is physically uninjured, but you cannot get past the guilt. Should you have been driving more slowly? Not taken them with you at all? You are barely sleeping, your child is clinging to you. And you are asking yourself: is this my fault?

### The most important point

**An accident in which someone drives into you is not your failure.** You brought your child along properly secured in their child seat – that is exactly what parents do. Guilt is almost the rule after something like this, but it is **not proof of fault**.

### The most common questions

**"Should I have been driving more slowly or not taken my child?"**
Guilt is one of the most common responses after an accident with a child in the car – it is not rational, but an expression of how much you care. You are **not** responsible for someone driving into you. Full stop.

**"Does my child need to see a doctor even if there are no visible injuries?"**
That is a decision for a paediatric doctor, not us. When in doubt: it is always better to have one extra check-up – including for late-onset effects. Important: **children under 10** cannot be held contributorily negligent in road traffic under § 828 para. 2 BGB – contributory fault is excluded, full protection applies. Details: [Third parties / children](/haftpflicht/dritte-beteiligte).

**"What about the child seat?"**
A child seat that was subjected to impact in an accident should, following manufacturer recommendations, be **replaced** – even if it shows no visible damage. These costs are an independent item of damages. **Photograph it before replacing it** – we include it in the expert report.

**"My child is sleeping restlessly and clinging more. Is that the accident?"**
Changes like these can occur after an accident, particularly in young children. Whether it is harmless or requires attention is a matter for a paediatric doctor or child psychology counselling service. Do not dismiss it too quickly. There is nothing wrong with asking early.

**"I'm barely sleeping myself and I'm jumpy at the wheel. But that doesn't matter, does it?"**
It **does matter**. Parents often put themselves last and overlook the fact that they themselves are going through a completely normal stress response. Jumpiness, poor sleep, tension while driving – that is a reaction, not a failure. You can only be there for your child if you yourself are not doing too badly. Contact details above.

**"If my child has a claim – is that dealt with separately?"**
**Very important question.** A child is an **independent claimant** in their own right. If the insurer offers a lump-sum settlement that covers "everything," that settlement must **not** silently consume the child's claim as well. This must be expressly separated and reserved. **Please do not** sign anything that lumps both claims together.

**"Do I need the family court for my child's claim?"**
For settlements involving minors, a **family court approval** may be required above a certain threshold. There are specific protective mechanisms for potential late-onset effects – for children, the limitation period runs **only from the age of majority** plus 3 years, rather than the usual 3 years from knowledge ([§ 207 BGB limitation suspension for minors](/haftpflicht/verjaehrung-bgb195)). The law firm handles this.

**"What if something only becomes apparent when my child starts school?"**
That is precisely what early precautions are for. With children, the possibility of making **later claims** is kept open. Nothing is "forfeited" if something becomes visible years later. The law firm sets this up; we document from today.

### What to do next

1. **Take your child to a paediatric doctor** – even without visible injury
2. **Photograph the child seat** – then have it replaced (claim the costs)
3. **Take your own stress seriously** – you need to be looked after too
4. **No lump-sum settlement** without separate itemisation for child/parent and a reservation for late-onset consequences
5. **Lawyer** through our partner law firm – including coordination with the family court

## <a name="totalschaden"></a>5. If your car is a write-off and your daily life is falling apart

### The situation

Two days ago: the accident. Your car has been towed away, possibly a total loss. You need a rental car tomorrow – you commute to work. The opposing insurer has sent you a residual value offer that sounds suspiciously high. And in the back of your mind you are thinking: what about the finance agreement? Do I even want to own a car again?

### The most important point

**Do not make a quick decision out of exhaustion or avoidance.** Get a rental car now. Look at the expert report calmly. **Do not** sell the wreck before we have checked the residual value. Remaining balance with the bank: a separate matter, not your immediate problem.

### The most common questions

**"Do I get a rental car immediately – and which category?"**
Yes, as the injured party you are entitled to a rental car in the **same category as your own vehicle** – not the cheapest one the insurer likes to suggest. One category below is an acceptable duty to mitigate, several categories is not. If the insurer suggests a partner rental provider: be cautious – see [Decoder "Mietwagen zu hoch"](/decoder/mietwagen-zu-hoch) and [Rental car reimbursement](/haftpflicht/mietwagen).

**"Is my car now a total loss – when does that apply?"**
We calculate this using **Audatex III**. Simplified: economic total loss when repair costs would exceed the Wiederbeschaffungswert (replacement value) minus the Restwert (residual value). **However:** you can also enforce a more expensive repair if you keep the car and have it properly repaired – the **130% rule** (BGH VI ZR 70/04). Details: [Decoder "Reparatur unwirtschaftlich"](/decoder/reparatur-unwirtschaftlich).

**"What is my car still worth?"**
The **Wiederbeschaffungswert** – what you would have to pay for an equivalent vehicle on the regional market. We determine this based on condition, mileage, specification and comparable offers. For **private individuals: gross** (including VAT) per BGH authority.

**"The insurer has sent a residual value offer. Should I accept?"**
**Please not yet.** A common tactic: the insurer obtains an inflated residual value bid through specialist online residual value exchanges from an **out-of-area buyer** – this reduces your pay-out by often €1,500–€3,000. Under BGH authority, what is relevant is the **realistically achievable regional** residual value. Do not sell the wreck before we have compared figures.

**"Am I entitled to Wertminderung on a total loss?"**
For a **genuine total loss** (car is replaced): no. Wertminderung is relevant for **repaired** vehicles. If your case turns out to be a repair case (130% rule), Wertminderung is also assessed – typically €500–€5,000. Details: [Wertminderung](/haftpflicht/wertminderung).

**"The insurer is cutting the rental car costs – what will they actually pay?"**
The dispute centres on the daily rate (Schwacke vs. Fraunhofer – courts generally apply a **blended midpoint**) and on the duration. If you choose **not** to take a rental car, you are entitled to Nutzungsausfall (loss of use) under the Sanden-Danner table – €27–€175/day depending on vehicle category, without needing to prove specific journeys (presumption of personal use).

**"The insurer has made an offer – accept it or litigate?"**
Check it against our calculation. If Wiederbeschaffungswert, Restwert and ancillary costs add up: accepting is fine. If there are material deductions: the law firm issues a response and recovers the difference – litigation is the **last step**, handled by the law firm, not by you. You decide on the basis of figures, not exhaustion.

**"My car was still on finance. What about the outstanding balance?"**
An important point many overlook. Compensation is based on the **value of the car**, not on your outstanding finance – a **shortfall** can remain. Clarify this with your bank and the law firm. We are not the right contact for this, but we flag it early.

**"Do I even need a car again, or is public transport and car-sharing enough?"**
Your decision. We earn nothing from a car purchase. If a car is practically the right thing for your commute, that is fine. If you have been thinking about alternatives anyway, that is fine too. The decision should come from your **actual needs** – not from avoidance because driving feels frightening right now.

**"I don't want to get in a car at all right now – is that normal?"**
After an experience like this, that is entirely understandable. If the discomfort persists or worsens: medical or psychotherapeutic help – driving anxiety is treatable (ICD-11 6B03, often classified as a specific phobia). If you need contact details, let us know.

### What to do next

1. **Rental car** – equivalent category, not the insurer's partner suggestion
2. **Your own expert report** calculated with Audatex III
3. **Do not accept** the insurer's residual value offer without a counter-check
4. **Clarify outstanding finance** with your bank in parallel
5. **Lawyer** through our partner law firm

## <a name="alle"></a>6. Key answers that apply to everyone

Regardless of which of the five situations you find yourself in – these eight answers **always** apply:

### 1. You are not alone, and you do not have to solve everything today.
One step at a time – expert report, lawyer, medical treatment. Not all at once.

### 2. The opposing insurance company is not on your side.
It pays, but it pays **as little as possible**. That is its job, not malice – but your protection begins with understanding that.

### 3. You have free choice – expert appraiser, repair shop, lawyer.
Suggestions from the opposing insurer are suggestions, not obligations. BGH case law protects your choice ([Decoder "Werkstattnetz"](/decoder/werkstatt-netz), [Decoder "Unser Sachverständiger"](/decoder/unser-sachverstaendiger)).

### 4. Lawyer's fees are paid by the other side.
In an accident that was not your fault, lawyer's fees are an independent item of damages (BGH VI ZR 235/13) – you bear no cost risk. Details: [Lawyer's fee reimbursement](/haftpflicht/anwaltskosten-erstattung).

### 5. No quick phone conversations, no quick signatures.
What you say on the phone can be used against you later. What you sign is usually final. Both: **wait and have them checked first**.

### 6. Lump-sum settlements without a reservation for late-onset consequences are a trap.
Whether personal injury or property damage – without an express reservation for unforeseeable consequences, everything is settled. Details: [Decoder "Pauschal-Abgeltung"](/decoder/pauschal-abgeltung), [§ 212 BGB acknowledgement](/haftpflicht/anerkenntnis-bgb212).

### 7. Documentation is everything.
Photographs, symptom diary, medical certificates, repair invoices, rental car receipts. What is not documented effectively does not exist as far as insurers are concerned.

### 8. No SF class downgrade for an accident that was not your fault.
As long as the opposing party's liability cover is settling the claim, your own insurance remains unaffected. Details: [Kasko vs. Haftpflicht](/haftpflicht/kasko-versicherung).

## <a name="faq"></a>9. Frequently asked questions

**1. What is the first thing to do after a road accident?**
Safety (hazard lights, warning triangle, attend to injured), call the police if needed, photographic documentation, exchange details, secure witness addresses. See a doctor within 24 hours.

**2. Do I have to give information to the opposing insurer on the phone?**
No. You are not obliged to comment on fault, circumstances or symptoms on the phone. Politely refer them to the law firm and the expert appraiser.

**3. What does a lawyer and expert appraiser cost me?**
In an accident that was not your fault: nothing. Both costs are independent items of damages borne by the opposing insurer (BGH VI ZR 235/13 and VI ZR 67/06).

**4. May the insurer dictate which repair shop or expert appraiser I use?**
No. Under BGH case law you have free choice. Suggestions are recommendations, not obligations.

**5. How long may the insurer take to assess the claim?**
4 weeks for clear liability, 6–8 weeks for complex personal injury cases. After that: default under § 286 BGB with interest on arrears and fully reimbursable lawyer's fees.

**6. Am I entitled to Schmerzensgeld for mild cervical spine symptoms?**
Yes. Indicative ranges by grade: HWS-Distorsion Grade I €250–€1,500, Grade II €800–€3,500, Grade III €3,000–€10,000. Significantly higher for chronic developments.

**7. Should I accept a lump-sum settlement offer?**
Not without legal review. And never without an express reservation for late-onset consequences while the course of recovery is still open.

**8. What rental car category am I entitled to, and for how long?**
The category of your own vehicle; one category below is reasonable. Duration corresponds to the actual repair period – for total loss cases, 14 days' replacement procurement period is the BGH standard.

**9. My car was on finance – what about the outstanding balance?**
Compensation is based on the value of the car, not the outstanding finance – a shortfall can remain. Clarify with your bank.

**10. Will my own insurance premium go up?**
For an accident that was not your fault, processed through the opposing party's liability cover: no. Be careful not to inadvertently make a claim on your own comprehensive cover.

**11. Will there be an entry about me in the HIS (Hinweis- und Informationssystem)?**
Possibly – but an entry as the injured party in an accident that was not your fault is not a "credit black mark." The right to information under GDPR exists.

**12. What if the other driver is unknown (hit and run)?**
[Verkehrsopferhilfe e.V.](/haftpflicht/unversicherte-voh) steps in – a police report must be filed within 7 days.

**13. What is Schockschaden and does it apply to me?**
A distinct psychological illness with clinical significance following the injury or death of a close family member. Recognised since the BGH's landmark ruling in 1985. Prerequisite: medical diagnosis.

**14. When do my claims become time-barred?**
3 years from the end of the year in which you had knowledge of the damage and the person responsible. For personal injury: 30-year maximum limitation period; for minors, limitation runs only from the age of majority plus 3 years.

**15. If my symptoms persist – can I still bring a claim later?**
With a reservation for late-onset consequences: yes. Without one: usually no. That is why you must never sign a settlement without a reservation.
